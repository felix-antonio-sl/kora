# The Oxford Handbook of DBT — Training and Conclusion

## Origins of the Intensive Training Model

Traditional psychotherapy training (university programmes, residencies, institutes) too slow to meet post-RCT demand for DBT. Years-long lag before academic institutions adopt new treatments; most programmes lack combined didactic + clinical supervision for EBTs. Practising clinicians excluded from academic pipelines entirely.

Genesis: Linehan's research lab at University of Washington integrated clinical DBT training with science training for graduate students. Proved insufficient for community demand. Liability concerns limited graduate exposure to high-risk suicidal populations. Linehan created Behavioral Tech, LLC to export training beyond academia, requiring a new model.

Key innovation: team-based training tied to programmatic implementation. DBT consultation team = core treatment mode embedded in training design. Teams form before training, train together, implement jointly, maintain fidelity collectively. All team therapists acknowledge shared responsibility if any team client dies by suicide. Consultation team monitors adherence, reduces burnout, provides backup.

## Structure of DBT Intensive Training

Bipartite model: two 5-day in-person workshops separated by ~6 months of self-study and implementation.

### Pre-Training Preparation

- Required reading: Linehan (1993, 2015) texts, discussed within teams
- Optional: Learning Communities (6-12 weeks before Part 1) with DBT-trained facilitators reviewing texts, practising application

### Part 1: Foundations (5 Days)

| Day | Content Domains |
|-----|----------------|
| 1 | Foundations of DBT (behavioural science, acceptance principles, dialectical principles); DBT case formulation |
| 2 | Structure of treatment; consultation team; functions of comprehensive treatment; levels of disorder and stages; targets of treatment; orientation and commitment |
| 3 | Problem solving; behavioural analysis; CBT procedures; validation; dialectical strategies |
| 4 | Suicide risk assessment and management; crisis strategies; treatment of suicidal individuals |
| 5 | Stylistic strategies; case management; protocols and special treatment strategies |

Daily schedule: 9:00-12:30 morning session, 1:30-5:15 afternoon session (Day 5 ends 4:30).

Teaching methods: didactics, experiential exercises, team-based exercises, homework over lunch/evenings. Mindfulness opens each day. Chain analyses target training-interfering behaviours (e.g., lateness). Teams discuss implementation planning: inclusion/exclusion criteria, recruitment, barrier navigation, strategies for all four modes.

### Self-Study and Implementation Period (~6 Months)

Individual homework: conduct chain analyses, practise DBT skills, read foundational texts.

Team homework: establish consultation team agreements, define programme inclusion/exclusion criteria, identify implementation barriers.

Required: DBT knowledge exam before Part 2. Mock suicide crisis call initiated by trainer with at least one team member; team then practises with all members. Optional additional consultations (team observation, session review).

### Part 2: Implementation and Refinement (5 Days)

Focus: overcoming implementation barriers, clinical consultation, live practice.

- Teams present programme progress and clinical case conceptualizations
- Large group consultation format: cross-team learning, identification of successful strategies
- Trainers provide live feedback on case formulations and programme structure
- Additional didactic training on identified areas of weakness
- Review and discussion of take-home exam and homework applications

### Training Objectives Summary

**Part 1 objectives:** participate in consultation team; demonstrate consultation team strategies; list modes/functions/agreements; implement structure/goals/stages; teach four skills modules; explain biosocial model; create treatment plans using targeting; demonstrate dialectics; conduct cue exposure and response prevention; conduct chain and solution analyses; implement CBT strategies; identify validation levels; demonstrate communication strategies; implement case management and coaching; apply suicide crisis protocols.

**Part 2 objectives:** demonstrate comprehension and application of DBT concepts and strategies; identify and treat in-session dysfunctional behaviours; manage skills training problems; identify and resolve team problems; design DBT service programmes for different populations/settings; formulate programme evaluation plans; practise DBT techniques via coached role play; teach DBT skills per manual; analyse expert therapist video segments.

### Training Methods

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Didactics | In-person instructor-led conceptualization and knowledge domains |
| Demonstration | Authentic therapy session recordings coded as DBT-adherent; live trainer demonstrations |
| Rehearsal | Role play replication of demonstrations; homework requiring intervention practice with team members and clients; mock crisis calls |
| Implementation | Written programme development tasks reviewed at Part 2; programme description and case formulation as consultation basis |
| Contingencies | Homework score sheets required for Letter of Completion; knowledge test scores included; team completion prize at Part 2; teams must remain intact |

## Supplemental Training Programmes

| Programme | Purpose | Target |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| DBT Foundational Training | Content similar to Part 1 of intensive | New members joining existing intensively-trained teams |
| DBT Team Building Intensive | Brings solo practitioners together, forms virtual consultation teams | Solo practitioners without existing teams |

## Training vs. Certification

Intensive training completion does NOT equal certification. Training provides thorough exposure to DBT principles and methods with practice opportunities but includes no individual competency assessment. Intensive training prepares practitioners for separate certification process; training alone does not constitute meeting any particular standard for DBT provision.

## Evidence for DBT Intensive Training

### Implementation Outcomes

UK survey (Swales, Taylor, & Hibbs, 2012): 105 teams trained 1994-2007; 63% actively running DBT programmes 2-15 years post-training. Programmes most at risk for failure in years 2 and 5.

Prospective study (Harned et al., 2016): 75% of teams implemented all four modes 5-12 months post-training; 87% implemented at least three modes. Skills training (96.2%) and consultation team (92.3%) most frequently implemented.

Ditty, Landes, Doyle, & Beidas (2015): at least one year post-training, implementation rates: individual therapy 96%, group skills training 99%, consultation team 97%, phone coaching 87%.

### Factors Contributing to Training Effectiveness

- Bipartite structure with extended inter-training period (~6 months) creates protracted trainer contact vs. one-time workshops
- Team-based format increases adoption likelihood and reach; reduces organizational barriers
- Trainers actively target clinician motivation using contingency management throughout
- Public recognition for highest homework completion; chain analyses for training-interfering behaviours
- Expectation to present outcomes drives proactive implementation

### Therapist Factors Influencing Implementation

**Theoretical orientation:** CBT/DBT-oriented practitioners have more positive EBP attitudes and greater openness. Non-CBT practitioners show lower openness initially, but intensive training significantly increases openness and decreases negative EBP views across all orientations (Botanov et al., 2016a).

**Confidence:** Greater self-reported confidence in treatment delivery predicts adoption post-training (Shapiro, Prinz, & Sanders, 2012; confirmed for DBT by Herschell et al., 2014).

**Burnout:** Training reduces burnout and treatment-related stress (Carmel, Fruzzetti, & Rose, 2014; Perseius et al., 2007). Non-CBT practitioners show significantly higher personal and work burnout at training end vs. population norms (Botanov et al., 2016b). CBT/DBT practitioners score significantly lower on client burnout post-training.

### Team Factors

- Better team cohesion, communication, and climate associated with implementing more DBT elements (Ditty et al., 2015)
- Higher proportion of master's-degree-or-above members and greater prior DBT experience predict more modes implemented (Harned et al., 2016)
- Consultation team commitment + management commitment predict sustainability (van den Bosch & Sinnaeve, 2015; 25 Dutch teams)
- Feeling organizationally embedded, connected with other practitioners, and supported = crucial for sustainability

### Organizational Barriers

| Barrier Category | Key Findings |
|-----------------|--------------|
| Lack of organizational support | Most common reason for programme "death" (68% of failed UK teams); includes insufficient protected time, absent management buy-in, funding difficulties, insufficient resources (Swales et al., 2012) |
| Training and programme needs | More unmet needs = fewer DBT elements implemented (Harned et al., 2016) |
| Public health systems | Lack of administrative support/investment, resource concerns, no reduction in clinical load for DBT delivery (Carmel et al., 2014; Herschell et al., 2009) |
| College counselling centres | Productivity demands, lack of individual therapists, insufficient time for team consultation, unwillingness to offer phone coaching (Chugani & Landes, 2016) |

### Programme Reach

Active UK programmes (62.8% of trained teams): average 15.8 clients per programme at any time (range 2-60). Penetration remains under-researched.

### Measurement Gaps

- Few instruments measure in-session adherence in naturalistic settings for intensively trained practitioners
- DBT knowledge strongly associated with completing intensive training across disciplines (Hawkins & Sinha, 1998)
- DBT Barriers to Implementation Scale (BTI-S) assesses four implementation domains but has low reliability; needs further development (Chugani, Mitchell, Botanov, & Linehan, 2017)
- Needed: valid/reliable implementation measures, RCTs with post-training follow-up, examination of provider adherence, reach, and client characteristics

## Conclusions and Future Directions

DBT = evidence-based treatment for emotion regulation disorders with global reach across ~30 countries on six continents. Intensive training model is primary driver of successful dissemination. Model evolved through iterative empirical self-examination over ~25 years, mirroring DBT's own development process.

### Critical Gap: Scale

Conservative global need estimate: 135 million people experiencing high distress from emotion dysregulation difficulties during lifetime. Number actually receiving DBT falls far short. In-person instructor-led training likely insufficient to reach needed scale alone.

### Priority Future Directions

- Remote/online training models: serious consideration needed for scalability
- Parsimonious training: as treatment component research identifies critical therapeutic ingredients, training models can focus on these specific components
- Long-term sustainability research: programmes most vulnerable in years 2 and 5 post-training
- Treatment penetration: how many clients reached per programme remains under-studied
- Organizational support structures: most common cause of programme failure

### Key Messages for Clinicians

- Evidence-based psychological treatments require evidence-based training methods
- Intensive bipartite model (two 5-day workshops + 6-month implementation period) developed because traditional methods insufficient for DBT demand
- DBT training principles mirror treatment principles: mindfulness, chain analyses, contingency management applied within training itself
- Initial implementation outcomes strong post-intensive training; long-term sustainability and treatment penetration require further investigation
