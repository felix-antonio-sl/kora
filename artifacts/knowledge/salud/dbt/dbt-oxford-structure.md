# The Oxford Handbook of DBT — Structure of Treatment

Covers programme architecture, consultation team operation, skills training delivery, generalization modalities, and team leadership. Source: Swales (Ed.), 2018, Chapters 7-11.

## DBT Programme Structure and Components

Programme structure = tangible and intangible fixed elements supporting treatment objectives. Structural decisions must align with the five functions of comprehensive DBT: (1) engaging/motivating clients, (2) engaging/motivating providers, (3) client skill acquisition, (4) skill generalization, (5) structuring the environment.

### Five Functions of Comprehensive Treatment

| Function | Primary Mode | Key Mechanisms |
|----------|-------------|----------------|
| Client motivation | Individual therapy | Commitment strategies, TIB targeting, diary card "urge to quit" tracking, 4-miss rule |
| Therapist motivation | Consultation team | Agreements, mindfulness, role plays, video review, ongoing training |
| Skill acquisition | Skills group | Didactic instruction, handouts, classroom format, ground rules |
| Skill generalization | Phone coaching | Between-session contact, homework, session recordings, in-vivo rehearsal |
| Structuring environment | All modes | Environmental interventions, case management, family involvement |

Each mode primarily serves one function but attends to multiple functions in practice. Modularity separates functions into independent modules, but emphasis rather than exclusivity governs.

### Physical Infrastructure

Essential spaces: waiting area, bathroom, locked file room with locked cabinets, individual therapy rooms, larger room for skills group and consultation team, communication system for between-session contact.

Larger programmes add: administrative offices, secure server areas, break room, storage. Adaptations for youth/caregiver populations require larger rooms. Milieu-based programmes (inpatient, forensic, day treatment) require infrastructure for education, recreation, vocational training, meals.

### Technology Requirements

- Client-therapist between-session communication (phone, text, email)
- Electronic health records with upload capability (texts, emails, photos)
- Session recording system (video/audio) for supervision and peer review
- Secure storage and scheduled deletion of recordings
- Informed consent for all recording
- Internet access for supplementary teaching materials

### Consultation Team Structure

Core of weekly programme schedule. Aligned with therapist motivation function and environment structuring.

**Assigned roles:** team leader, observer, timekeeper, note-taker.

**Meeting composition:** Two elements run consecutively or on separate days:
1. Weekly training (treatment review, new research, DBT science)
2. Consultation to the therapist (support, case discussion, adherence)

**Team size:** Maximum 8-10 providers per team. Larger programmes split into multiple teams. Clinical lead may attend all team meetings to ensure fidelity across teams.

**Consultation Team Agreements** (read one per meeting after mindfulness):
- Dialectical agreement
- Consultation-to-the-patient agreement
- Observing-limits agreement
- Consistency agreement
- Phenomenological empathy agreement
- Fallibility agreement

**Documentation:** Brief clinical notes on decisions (placed in client files), follow-up items with due dates carried forward on agenda, programme self-evaluation data (therapist participation frequency, request categories, behaviourally specific language use).

### Individual Therapy Mode

Individual therapist bears ultimate responsibility for client's target behaviours in standard outpatient DBT. Programme manual must define client population, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and all roles.

**Key documentation sequence (client entry):**

| Phase | Documents/Tasks |
|-------|----------------|
| Intake | Informed consent (treatment description, team approach, recording consent, information sharing) |
| Pre-treatment | Orientation (up to 4 sessions), commitment, Crisis Response Plan, social network map |
| Assessment | Behavioural Chain Analysis (BCA) for target behaviours per DBT Target Hierarchy |
| Treatment planning | Case conceptualization-driven plan using problem-solving paradigm; update every 90 days |
| Session documentation | Case notes tracking engagement, interventions, chain/solution analyses, homework |
| Ongoing tracking | Diary card (client variables, target behaviours, urges, skills use) |
| Closure | Treatment summary documenting environment structuring, skill generalization, service connections |

**Crisis Response Plan contents:** client contact information (address, phone, messaging, email), significant others with addresses and contact details, favoured safe locations, social network map. Must be readily accessible to clinicians at all hours.

### Skills Group Training Mode

**Format:** Leader (content/learning) + co-leader (process/dynamics). 2-2.5 hours per session. Separate skills diary card tracks skills learned and practised.

**Structural elements:** Mindfulness opening, homework review, didactic instruction with handouts/worksheets, experiential exercises, homework assignment. Ground rules prohibit self-harm discussion and intimate relationships between members.

**Tracking:** Attendance tracked per session, progress notes per client, consecutive misses monitored (4-miss rule triggers team action). Group-interfering behaviour communicated to individual therapists.

**Calendar:** Six months to teach all four modules. New clients enter during mindfulness module reviews. Optional graduation ceremony for departing clients.

### Telephone Coaching Mode

Published target hierarchy: (1) crisis management, (2) skill generalization, (3) relationship repair. Calls typically under 10 minutes. Therapists observe personal limits and discuss them with clients during pre-treatment.

Programme decisions: direct therapist contact versus rotating on-call schedule versus independent crisis service. Direct therapist contact preferred for relationship repair and skill generalization functions.

### Ancillary Treatments

Psychiatrists, chemical dependency specialists, dietitians, exposure therapists, case managers may attend consultation team if they follow DBT principles. Individual therapist retains primary treatment responsibility. Confidentiality agreements required for external professionals.

### Programme Manual

Each programme creates its own working manual defining: client population, providers and roles, principles and practices, materials, deviations from published manuals with rationale, consent procedures, assessment batteries, treatment plan format. Serves as accountability document. Updated as science evolves.

### Administration and Human Resources

**Hiring criteria:** Previous adherence to evidence-based practices, positive response to supervision, willingness/flexibility, non-judgmental approach. Commitment session with problem-solving and devil's advocate conducted with candidates.

**Training model:** Reading + formal lecture + demonstration/practice with feedback + ongoing supervision. Behaviourally defined benchmarks with direct observation. DBT certification in treatment modes available.

**Retention:** Employees mastering competencies retained and promoted; those failing to master competencies redirected to alternative modalities.

**Scheduling:** Track appointments kept, rescheduled, and missed for 4-miss rule enforcement. Individual therapists retain substantial control over session scheduling to manage engagement.

### Data Collection

**Two classes of programme data:**

| Class | What to Track | Sources |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| Client outcomes | Target behaviours, urges, skills use, psychological variables (depression, anxiety, anger) | Diary cards, questionnaires, interviews |
| Programme fidelity | Adherence to treatment model, diary card completion rates, chain analysis completion, role-play frequency | Case notes, consultation team tracking, session video review |

Benchmark outcomes against published research. Outcome data supports programme advocacy, resource requests, and quality assurance. Consent forms must notify clients of data tracking.

## Running an Effective DBT Consultation Team

Consultation team = essential component of comprehensive DBT. Dual purpose: enhance therapist motivation and increase therapist capability, both serving treatment adherence.

### What Distinguishes DBT Teams

| Feature | DBT Team | Traditional Team |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| Client ownership | Community of therapists treating community of clients | Individual therapists own their clients |
| Meeting focus | Therapy for the therapist | Discussion about clients |
| Agenda | Therapists put themselves on agenda | Clients put on agenda |
| Vulnerability | Required; all members have clinical responsibility | Not expected |
| Dialectics | Intensely acceptance AND intensely change | Typically one or the other |

If one therapist's client dies by suicide, all team members say they had a client die by suicide. If a team member notices non-adherence, they are obligated to speak up.

### Team Agreements

**Standard Consultation Team Agreements:**

| Agreement | Core Commitment |
|-----------|----------------|
| Dialectical | Search for what is left out; find the other pole; move toward synthesis |
| Consultation-to-the-patient | Do not serve as intermediary; coach clients to navigate interactions themselves |
| Observing-limits | Observe own limits; stretch when needed; approach others' limits non-judgmentally |
| Consistency | Consistency between team members not required; inconsistency = client skill practice opportunity |
| Phenomenological empathy | Non-judgmental, compassionate stance toward teammates, clients, families, self |
| Fallibility | Admit mistakes; accept help; remain open to feedback; address own team-interfering behaviour |

**Additional agreements for new members:**
- Provide DBT only; do not combine modalities unless effectively and mindfully done
- Conceptualize behaviour from a behavioural perspective consistently
- Treat team meetings like therapy sessions (attend weekly, arrive on time, stay to end, come prepared, speak every meeting)
- Consider yourself part of a community of therapists treating a community of clients
- Provide therapy for the therapists; speak up about non-adherence regardless of seniority
- Maintain clinical responsibility as condition of membership
- Call out elephants in the room
- Remain one-mindful and focused (no phones, side conversations)
- Assess sufficiently before offering solutions
- Follow team coverage and absence policies
- Continue following agreements even when burned out or frustrated

### Meeting Structure

**Standard agenda items:**
1. Mindfulness practice (brief, 5 min)
2. Read one Consultation Agreement aloud
3. Review previous meeting notes (may generate additional agenda items)
4. Therapy for the therapist (bulk of time)
5. Reports of effective therapist and client behaviour

**Optional additions:** Therapist of the week (rotating case formulation/video/role-play), coverage assignments, skills group update, psychiatrist updates, brief personal check-ins.

**Agenda-setting methods:** Verbal check-in, written form, whiteboard. Each therapist placed on agenda once per meeting. Priority rating (1-5 matching diary card) determines order and time allocation. Priority based on DBT hierarchy: life-threatening behaviour highest, quality-of-life issues lowest.

**Request types when placed on agenda:**
- Help with assessment
- Help with problem solving
- Help building empathy for client
- Validation needed
- Help with burnout
- Help figuring out what I need

### Maintaining Team Culture

**Observer role (rotating):** Monitors team for deviations from agreements. Rings bell when observing:
- Therapist did not speak
- Non-mindfulness (doing two things at once)
- Lateness or unpreparedness
- Defensiveness
- Judgmental or non-compassionate comments
- Solutions offered before sufficient assessment
- Someone treated as fragile (unaddressed elephant)
- Unresolved dialectic

Team responses to flagged issues: quick restatement, gentle reminder, irreverent comment, direct confrontation, validation, chain analysis, solution analysis. If problem persists, place on agenda for extended exploration.

### Team Leader Role

Distinct from rotating meeting leader. Does not rotate. Oversees team health, manages logistics, interfaces with administration, recruits members, facilitates departures when necessary.

Core function: supporting and maintaining team culture through modelling vulnerability and therapy-for-the-therapist behaviour (not acting as instructor or controlling member behaviour).

### Common Team Problems and DBT-Based Solutions

All team problems addressed using DBT strategies: non-judgmental stance, dialectical thinking, validation, chain analysis, missing links analysis, check the facts, DEAR MAN, distress tolerance.

**Key principle:** Balance addressing problems that interfere with motivation/capability against ignoring those insufficiently severe to affect adherent treatment. Not all problems need solving.

Problems requiring one-on-one team leader attention: certain legal/ethical issues, highly personal matters, member unresponsive to repeated team interventions. Problems beyond team capacity (e.g., personal relationships consuming excessive team time): recommend external therapy while team monitors capability/motivation.

### Managing Absences

Team absences treated seriously: correction acknowledges and fixes damage caused; over-correction provides benefit to team from the absence (bringing back workshop learnings, covering for someone else, small gestures). Tardiness addressed via chain analysis or missing links analysis with commitment to solution, preferably via email to avoid consuming agenda time.

## Skills Training: Principles and Group Delivery

Skills training addresses pervasive skills deficits as core problem in emotion dysregulation. Biosocial theory: behavioural dysfunction arises from poor fit between temperamentally sensitive individual and invalidating environment, leading to deficits in emotion regulation and distress tolerance.

### Four Skills Modules

| Module | Target Dysregulation | Core Content |
|--------|---------------------|-------------|
| Core Mindfulness | Self-dysregulation, identity disturbance, dissociation | Observe, describe, participate; one-mindfully, non-judgmentally, effectively. Repeated between each other module (6x/year) |
| Interpersonal Effectiveness | Chaotic relationships, abandonment fear | Assertive asking/refusing (DEAR MAN), relationship building (GIVE), self-respect (FAST), modulating intensity, shaping relationships |
| Emotion Regulation | Emotional arousal (acute and chronic) | Understanding emotions, labelling, reducing vulnerability (ABC PLEASE), increasing positive experiences, check facts, problem solving, opposite action |
| Distress Tolerance | Impulsive actions, self-harm | Crisis survival skills (short-term), reality acceptance skills (radical acceptance, turning the mind, willingness) |

### Guiding Principles

**Zen/acceptance:** Attentional control and compassion. Mindfulness module repeated 6x/year. Brief mindfulness opens and closes every session. Group leaders model non-judgmental stance throughout.

**Behaviour therapy:** Clear behavioural targets hierarchically organized:
1. Extinguish therapy-destroying behaviours
2. Increase skills acquisition, strengthening, generalization
3. Decrease therapy-interfering behaviours (TIBs)

**Skills training procedures:**

| Procedure | Purpose | Methods |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Acquisition | Develop new behaviour | Verbal instruction, modelling, self-disclosure stories, metaphors |
| Strengthening | Increase use and accuracy | Behavioural rehearsal (role-play, imaginal, in-vivo), reinforcement, feedback, coaching |
| Generalization | Flexible use across contexts | Homework, multiple skill options per situation, stimulus generalization exercises |

**Targeting TIBs in group:** Client TIBs (homework non-completion, lateness, missing group, off-task behaviour, refusal to speak). Trainer TIBs (reinforcing ineffective behaviour, over/under-preparing, presenting material ineffectively). Therapy-destroying behaviours (self-injury in group, selling drugs, violent threats) must be addressed immediately with behavioural assessment, functional analysis, skills plan, and contingency management.

**Dialectics in skills group:** Leader and co-leader deliberately offer opposing poles (acceptance vs. change skills). Validation balances change-oriented content. Devil's advocate strategy highlights cons of skillful behaviour to elicit client arguments for skills use.

### Group Session Structure

| Component | Duration | Function |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Announcements | Brief | Schedule changes, new/graduating members, absences |
| Mindfulness practice | 2-5 min | Experiential exercise strengthening mindfulness skills; variety across sessions |
| Homework review | ~30 min | Each member shares skill application from previous week; dragging out behaviour for non-completers |
| Break | ~10 min | |
| New skill instruction | ~45 min | Acquisition, strengthening, generalization strategies sequentially applied |
| Homework assignment | Brief | Relevant worksheets; additional activities (e.g., distress tolerance kits) |
| Closing ritual (optional) | Brief | Observation sharing, intention setting, contingency highlighting |

### Curriculum Flexibility

**Slowing pace:** When therapy-destroying/group-destroying behaviours consume time, or cognitively/emotionally challenging skills (radical acceptance, model for describing emotions) require extended instruction.

**Adding content:** When group deficits require supplementary material (e.g., adding validation handouts when GIVE module reveals insufficient validation skills).

**Speed, movement, flow:** Rapidly redirect tangential or dysregulating contributions back to skills training.

### Practical Decisions for Skills Groups

**Population considerations:** Define clear inclusion/exclusion criteria (diagnoses, age, sex/gender, suicidality/homicidality, psychosis, contagion-risk behaviours). BPD groups are functionally multidiagnostic (90%+ mood disorder comorbidity, 64% substance use, 88% anxiety, 53% eating disorder).

**Curriculum selection:** Standard schedules from Linehan (2015): 24 weeks, 20 weeks (McMain), 16 weeks (Neacsiu). Specialized curricula available for BPD + substance use, transdiagnostic emotion dysregulation, advanced skills.

**Open vs. closed groups:** Typically closed with entry points during mindfulness reviews or first weeks of new module. Substance abuse settings may allow mid-module entry to capitalize on client motivation.

**Role stability:** One clear leader and one clear co-leader for at minimum one entire module, ideally full six months. Rotation of homework review vs. teaching within a module is common but distinct roles must remain clear.

### Clinical Decision Framework: Skills Training Adaptations

**Full-model DBT vs. skills-only:**
- Full-model required for: recent suicide attempts, suicidal ideation with plan/intent/means, history of suicide attempts or homicidal acts, severe therapy-interfering/destroying behaviours
- Skills-only appropriate for: no suicidal ideation, primary mood/anxiety disorder, currently in weekly evidence-based individual therapy; requires individual therapist to manage crises and TIBs

**Individual vs. group skills training:**
- Group preferred (default): vicarious learning, social support, informal exposure to interpersonal situations
- Individual warranted for: potential group-destroying behaviour, poor temperamental fit, inability to commit to full cycle, logistical barriers (timing, insufficient clients for group), severe social anxiety unresponsive to graduated exposure
- If individual: ideally delivered by a second therapist (not primary individual therapist) to maintain role delineation

**Multi-family vs. identified-patient-only groups:**
- Multi-family preferred for adolescents and dependent young adults: provides common vocabulary, enhances generalization, models appropriate management, offers in-vivo validation practice
- Patient-only preferred when: family members perpetrate traumatic invalidation/abuse, treatment targets focus on developing independence rather than changing family dynamics, family unwilling to participate

### Empirical Support for Standalone Skills Groups

Standalone DBT skills groups effective across: BPD, eating disorders, substance use, ADHD, intimate partner violence, correctional settings, vocational rehabilitation. Skills use mediates reductions in suicide attempts, depression, anger. Dismantling study (Linehan et al., 2015): standard DBT and DBT skills + case management outperformed individual DBT without skills on NSSI frequency and depression/anxiety severity.

## Generalization Modalities: Phone, Text, and Email Coaching

Generalization = transferring skills learned in treatment (~2.5-3 hours/week) into the remaining 165 hours of the client's week. Often overlooked despite being one of five core DBT functions.

### Generalization Methods

1. Teaching skills in ways that promote acquisition and generalization
2. Phone coaching between sessions
3. Session recordings for client re-listening
4. In-vivo behavioural rehearsal of skills
5. Structuring the environment to promote skills use

### Traditional Phone Coaching

**Target hierarchy:** (1) decrease suicide crisis behaviours, (2) increase skills application to everyday life, (3) resolve interpersonal crises or therapist-client relationship ruptures.

**Core principles:**
- Focus on skills use in the present moment, not problem resolution
- Keep calls brief (~10-15 minutes)
- Not another therapy session; no extended problem-solving or extensive validation
- Validate concern as requiring session time; call focuses on coping until then
- Call itself practices interpersonal effectiveness (asking for help) and self-validation

**24-hour rule:** After self-harm/suicidal behaviour, client cannot contact therapist for coaching for 24 hours. Rationale: adds aversive consequence to self-injury, promotes help-seeking before crisis. Exception: brief risk assessment if client reports current suicidal risk. May be modified for adolescents.

### Communication Modalities

| Medium | Strengths | Risks/Limits |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Phone call | Richest interaction, best for complex coaching, most shaping opportunity | Requires both parties available simultaneously |
| Text message | Accessible when calling impossible (meetings, public settings), preferred by younger clients | May reinforce passive problem-solving; harder to assess nuance; privacy concerns |
| Email | Allows longer reflection | May enable avoidance of in-session engagement; excessive description without help-seeking |
| Mobile apps | Constant availability, interactive coaching, behaviour tracking | Very limited empirical evidence; low usage despite constant access |
| Web-based coaching | Can target specific vulnerable moments (e.g., early morning for school refusal) | Small evidence base; feasibility demonstrated |

**Guiding principle:** Choose medium based on what promotes skills generalization for the specific client. If texting does not facilitate generalization, encourage calling. If emailing enables avoidance, target as TIB.

### Orienting Clients to Coaching

Introduce during one of first few individual sessions. Cover rationale (three functions), procedures, privacy considerations, realistic expectations about response time and call structure. Sports timeout analogy effective for explaining in-the-moment coaching vs. retrospective review.

**Behavioural rehearsal:** Role-play a coaching call in session. Therapist may step outside office to simulate realistic call. Schedule a planned coaching call during client's most emotionally vulnerable time of week to lower threshold for first contact.

### Shaping Effective Coaching Behaviour

Clients shaped over time to call effectively. First call met with abundant reinforcement. Therapists instruct clients to call with good news and effective skill use (met with strong reinforcement to avoid differential reinforcement of crisis-generating behaviour). Therapist-initiated calls to praise specific skillful behaviour also promote generalization.

**Coping card strategy:** Client lists five skills to try before calling therapist. Reduces therapist burnout and promotes independent skills generalization.

**Pre-call worksheet:** Client defines problem, lists skills tried, lists others consulted before contacting therapist. Shapes calls to be skills-focused and efficient.

### Common Barriers

**Client barriers:**
- Self-invalidating beliefs ("I shouldn't need help," "This isn't a big deal")
- Beliefs about burdening therapist
- Ambivalence about skills/treatment
- Forgetting coaching is an option
- Confusion about parameters
- Social anxiety or lack of interpersonal skills to initiate call

**Interventions:** Check facts with therapist about how they feel being called, DEAR MAN for asking help, pros/cons for commitment, physical cues (post-it notes, phone reminders), scheduled practice calls, role-play exposure.

**Therapist barriers:**
- Organizational rules prohibiting personal number sharing or after-hours contact
- Personal limits on availability
- Burnout from excessive/ineffective calls

**Solutions:** Answering services, free phone apps providing alternative number, rotating on-call coverage (one therapist covers all team clients for a week), training on-call clinicians in DBT skills, clear limits communicated early, keeping calls brief and skills-focused.

### Research on Phone Coaching

Data consistently shows low utilization: average 1-2 calls/month/client. In 6-month BPD treatment (n=51), median 11 calls total; 4 of 35 completers accounted for 56% of contacts. More frequent contacts associated with reduced dropout, reduced psychological symptoms, increased client and therapist satisfaction. Problem is under-use rather than over-use. No dismantling studies yet confirming phone coaching as essential component.

## Team Leadership: Structuring the Wider Environment

Team lead = central role responsible for two functions: structuring the environment for programme sustainability and enhancing therapists' capabilities/motivation. Requires recognized authority from both DBT team and wider organization.

### Organizational Pre-Treatment

**Three phases:**
1. **Identify organizational goals** at different levels. Executive/board goals (evidence-based care, suicide reduction, patient experience) typically align with DBT. Middle management goals (waiting times, cost pressures) may conflict initially.
2. **Orient the system** to programme requirements and realistic timelines. Link DBT outputs to organizational goals. Present data (audit of high-frequency crisis presenters, staff sickness correlation).
3. **Gain commitment** from system for investment of time and money. Negotiate accommodations (e.g., relaxed waiting-time targets during pilot period). Maintain regular communication with management throughout implementation.

**Minimum viable programme resource:** 20 hours/week of staff time. Below this threshold, programme is either impossible or cost-ineffective. Team lead signals closure if resource drops below threshold and follows through if no resolution.

### Most Common Programme-Destroying Behaviour

Organizational withdrawal of already-allocated resources. Each team member requires 10-12 hours/week for learning and delivering DBT. Staff turnover in mental health services combined with failure to adjust job plans erodes viability within years of training.

**Contingency hierarchy for systems problems (escalating intensity):**

| Level | Strategy |
|-------|----------|
| 1 | Ignore |
| 2 | Model |
| 3 | Highlight |
| 4 | Assess |
| 5 | Problem solve |
| 6 | Advise |
| 7 | Instruct |
| 8 | Insist |
| 9 | Add contingencies |

Use more active procedures only after conservative ones fail.

### Assessing Team Assets

**Staff assessment areas:**
- Training and self-identified strengths/weaknesses
- Reinforcers for team membership (adherence emphasis, client intensity, access to consultation, group belonging)
- Dialectical style tendencies (natural validators vs. change-pushers)

**Ongoing evaluation:** Annual individual meetings reviewing skills, strengths, development areas, reinforcement adequacy. Increased frequency for struggling therapists. Direct observation via video/audio tape review.

**Leveraging strengths:** Delegate tasks to members reinforced by mastery. Pair beginners with creative/experienced trainers. When team becomes unbalanced, invite natural validators to generate change solutions and natural change-pushers to operate from acceptance.

### Addressing Team Deficits

1. **Recruitment strategy:** Include DBT capacity/willingness in job descriptions. Negotiate training budget for new and existing staff.
2. **Retention:** Maintain reinforcers for membership; actively problem-solve punishers of engagement.
3. **Staff development:** Reserve consultation team time for teaching, support attendance at DBT training events and related approaches (applied behavioural analysis, CBT, mindfulness), develop local/national DBT network participation.
4. **Removing ineffective members:** Team lead must have authority to insist members leave who: fail to acquire competence despite support, refuse to embrace DBT, violate consultation team agreements, refuse to discuss work or remain defensive to feedback. Structured feedback outside consultation team precedes removal.

### Dialectical Dilemmas for Team Leads

| Pole A | Pole B |
|--------|--------|
| Utilizing authority | Enhancing collaboration |
| Focus on other | Focus on self |
| Obligation to management | Obligation to team |
| Holding standards | Allowing mistakes |
| Rigid adherence to form | Lack of attention to form |
| Taking on tasks | Delegating tasks |
| Influencing priorities | Being influenced by others' priorities |
| Carrying morale | Being demoralized |

### Structuring the Consultation Team Meeting

**Attendance:** Register kept. Unscheduled misses require investigation. Some teams require circulated written chain analysis for non-attendance to avoid consuming meeting time.

**Agenda integrity:** Team lead prevents degeneration into narrative reporting, organizational moaning, or tick-box exercise. Business items deferred to email or monthly business hour. Reading of consultation agreement must be meaningful, not perfunctory. Mindfulness must transition naturally into work.

**Modelling effective help-seeking:** Team lead prepares specific, behaviourally defined questions in advance (e.g., "Here is the chain and my solutions, can you help?") rather than narrative case descriptions. Challenges myth that extensive background is needed before helpful suggestions can be made.

**Shaping vulnerability:** Team lead models vulnerability and therapy-for-the-therapist (not instruction or control). Ensures all members participate in addressing team-interfering behaviour rather than leaving confrontation to the lead alone. Uses dialectical delegation: assigns specific roles in difficult discussions to distribute emotional labour.

### Supporting Adherence

Team leads ensure adherence does not slip in any modality. External supervision with video/audio rated on University of Washington Adherence Rating Scale maintains personal fidelity. Learning points shared with team via written summaries or played examples. DBT accreditation pursued by team lead first to model willingness and acquire skills for dissemination.

### Managing Demoralization

Take systematic approach: draw up hierarchy of demoralizing factors, prioritize, problem-solve from top, identify quick wins. Specific strategies: role-play practice for difficult group situations, active mindfulness exercises, external supervision, workshop attendance, training for non-DBT staff to build collegial support, mindfulness exercises identifying "shining moments" and reframing self-judgments.

Network with other team leads for mutual support and comparison. Team lead's attitude has profound effect on morale; getting help when demoralized is harder for the lead because they are expected to carry the team through difficulties.
