# The Oxford Handbook of DBT — Theoretical Underpinnings - Parte 02

## Dialectics in DBT

Sayrs & Linehan (2018). Dialectics = world view + assumptions about change + dialogue/discourse form + thinking method. Middle path between universalism (fixed truths) and relativism (no a priori order): fixed truths exist briefly before evolving; truths are contextual.

### Dialectical World View: Three Characteristics

1. **Holistic and contextual**: everything related to everything else; events cannot be understood independent of context; coherence of whole is primary; disorder is systemic -- search for relational patterns among elements, not single causal element
2. **Relational identity**: identity is constituted by relations within the whole, not static elements; disorder = identity generated from part without reference to whole; isolation experience arises from lack of appreciation for connections to others/universe
3. **Fundamental process of change**: reality = ongoing change; relations change the whole, changed whole alters relations; logic of process, not structure; dialectical model focuses on reciprocal person-environment impact + ongoing evolution of both in transaction

Removes judgment and blame: instead of client as "the problem", examine transactions between client and environmental variables; find validity in both client responses and environmental responses; identify where systemic interventions are useful.

### Dialectical vs. Dichotomous Strategies

Dichotomous (Kegan, 1982): old abandoned for new (e.g., cognitive therapy replaces dysfunctional thought with rational thought). Dialectical: old incorporated with new, creating unforeseen opportunities. Thesis-antithesis-synthesis: synthesis not compromise but complex interplay where both extremes coexist, producing entirely new context greater than sum.

### Step-by-Step Dialectical Treatment

**Step 1: Adopt and model dialectical world view.** Requires giving up being right all the time without falling into "anything goes." Therapist searches for relational patterns, attends to own impact on client and client's impact on therapist, monitors reinforcement contingencies operating on therapist.

**Step 2: Balance treatment strategies and therapeutic positions.** Primary dialectical strategy = balance: acceptance with change, flexibility with stability, nurturing with challenging, capabilities focus with deficits focus. Balance does not mean 50-50; fully embody both poles, implement strategically per moment. Three characteristics of dialectical stance:

- Speed -- keeps client off balance, facilitates change
- Alertness -- respond just enough to maintain therapeutic flow
- Certainty and commitment -- move with total commitment while remaining open to shifting position

**Step 3: When polarization occurs, work for synthesis.** Notice dialectical impasse; search for what is left out of both positions; create new orderings including what was previously excluded. Behavioural shaping is itself dialectical: simultaneous acceptance of limitations + realization of capacity for change.

**Step 4: When one dialectical strategy fails, try another.** Any strategy finding antithesis, synthesizing thesis/antithesis, and allowing synthesis to propel forward movement is dialectical.

### Specific Dialectical Strategies

**Magnify tension between opposites:**

- *Devil's advocate* (Goldfried, 1978): present extreme thesis, get agreement, argue extreme antithesis. Example: therapist obtains commitment to no self-harm, then asks "Why would you agree to this?" -- client strengthens own arguments against self-harm while acknowledging difficulty.
- *Extending* (from Aikido/strategic family therapy): join with client's movement, move with them, extend beyond their intended position, leaving them off balance. Example: client says "If I can't see you today, it's all over" -- therapist extends to suicide crisis response, responding to implicit threat rather than session scheduling, causing client to back off from extreme statement.

**Enter the paradox:**

- Highlight contradictions, leave tension unresolved for client to tolerate. Example: "It seems like you want to work on this target and you don't."
- Validate position while disagreeing: "I understand completely how it is reasonable for you to believe I don't care about you, and it is reasonable for me to disagree."
- *And vs. but technique* (Hayes et al., 1999): substitute "and" for "but" to remove contradiction. "I love my boyfriend but I'm angry at him" (dichotomous) becomes "I love my boyfriend and I'm angry at him" (both true, depolarized, solution-generative).
- *Making lemonade out of lemons*: reconceptualize problem as opportunity without invalidating pain. "I know you're miserable, and this is the most worthwhile misery you will probably ever experience" (heroin withdrawal).

**Develop metaphors:**

- Stories/parables provide indirect, less threatening communication; capture essence of thesis/antithesis to enable synthesis
- Example: client resists change despite pain -- "You are sitting in a corner of a burning building with only one door out. Running through the smoke is the only escape. Let's run." Synthesizes "too painful to change" with "must change" into "temporary pain increase leads to long-term improvement."
- Example: "I don't do skills" -- "You remind me of a house builder who says I don't do walls."

### When Dialectical Strategies Are Beneficial

Useful when polarization occurs -- emphasis on movement and larger context facilitates depolarization. Examples:

- Client and therapist stuck on opposite poles (client insists on drug use; therapist insists on stopping) -- synthesis: drugs solve a problem the client lacks skills for, but create new problems; generate new solution for underlying need
- Client and therapist stuck on same pole (husband is terrible) -- without antithesis (husband's behaviour is caused and makes sense given context), understanding stagnates and solutions are not generated
- Client's attention needs capturing when therapy stagnates -- unexpected agreement can produce enough confusion to restart therapeutic movement
