# El Codice de las Corrientes Vivas - Parte 05

## Employment conditions

- [P147] **HECHO** — ~150M people migrated for work in 2013 (ILO 2015); economic inequalities between countries incentivize migration with higher exploitation risk

## Gender

- [P148] **HECHO** — women tend to live longer than men despite reporting more ill health in most of world
- [P149] **HECHO** — gender inequities → violence against women, discrimination against female children, unfair work divisions, lower wages, restricted education/healthcare

## Indigenous health

- [P150] **HECHO** — Indigenous peoples have significantly worse health than non-Indigenous; determinants: colonization, land dispossession, discriminatory policies, racism → `Indigenous peoples`

## Determinants of mental health

- [P151] **HECHO** — common mental illness (including substance abuse): ~20% global disease burden in years lived with disability (Whiteford et al. 2013)
- [P152] **HECHO** — depressive disorders = major/growing disease burden cause; contribute to ~800K suicides/year globally

## Urbanization and health

- [P153] **HECHO** — 2008: first time in history majority of world population lived in urban environments
- [P154] **HECHO** — urban health risk exposures cluster: environmental (air/noise/traffic), human (social isolation/safety), behavioural (eating/physical activity)
- [P155] **HECHO** — urbanization contributes to global issues: climate change + migration

## Globalization and commercial determinants

- [P156] **HECHO** — income inequality increased in nearly all world regions over recent decades (Alvaredo et al. 2018)
- [P157] **HECHO** — 2016: of top 100 world economies only 31 = countries; most = TNCs (Walmart, Royal Dutch Shell, Exxon Mobile, Apple)
- [P158] **HECHO** — TRIPS agreement requires ≥20-year patent protections; shaped by global pharmaceutical firms → `WTO`
- [P159] **HECHO** — IP extension in trade deals delays cheaper generic medicines → reduced affordable access → `TNCs`
- [P160] **DEFINICION** — commercial determinants of health = practices/strategies of corporations driving NCD risk factors (Kickbusch et al. 2016)
- [P161] **HECHO** — WHO FCTC (Framework Convention on Tobacco Control) adopted 2003, ratified by 181 countries; explicitly prevents engagement with tobacco companies

## Social determinants of health behaviours

- [P162] **HECHO** — Finnish North Karelia programme: rare successful heart health programme; success from community-level strategies + policy changes, not individual knowledge
- [P163] **TENSION** — despite evidence for structural determinants of behaviour, behaviourism still promoted as lifestyle disease solution ignoring structures; US individualism ideology undermines structural appreciation (Tesh 1988)
- [P164] **HECHO** — high Aboriginal Australian smoking partly = coping with daily racism; overweight/obesity primarily from marketing/supply of high fat/sugar foods → `commercial determinants`
- [P165] **REGLA** — behaviour change strategies alone likely increase health inequities; behaviour makes most difference when other life conditions favourable

## Climate change and social determinants

- [P166] **HECHO** — climate change effects fall most heavily on countries/groups already subject to socially determined health inequities
- [P167] **HECHO** — Syrian civil war: >5M refugees left country + ~6M displaced within Syria

## Complexity — temporality and multiple scales

- [P168] **REGLA** — SDH perspective: look "upstream" from biology/behaviour → address "causes of the causes"; modest changes in social/environmental factors across populations produce larger gains than high-risk individual interventions (Rose 1985; CSDH 2008)
- [P169] **HECHO** — Australian current smoker 9x more likely to develop lung cancer vs non-smoker; but temporal lag between exposure → health effect may be years/decades
- [P170] **DEFINICION** — "double burden of disease" = continued infectious disease impact among disadvantaged while NCDs predominate overall (Wahden 1996)

## Political challenges

- [P171] **HECHO** — Preston Curve: for LICs, national income growth from low base → marked increases in average life expectancy (Bloom & Canning 2007)
- [P172] **HECHO** — neoliberal development model adverse effects: poor nutrition, reduced healthcare, income inequalities, environmental degradation, poor urban conditions, low wages, population displacement, loss of domestic agriculture
- [P173] **HECHO** — South Australia pioneered "Health in All Policies" approach = whole-of-government action on SDH → `HiAP`
