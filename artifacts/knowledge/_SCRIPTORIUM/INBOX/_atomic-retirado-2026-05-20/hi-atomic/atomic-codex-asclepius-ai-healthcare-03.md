## Chapter 10 — Navigating Data Diversity and Equity in Healthcare with AI

### Health Equity
- **P438** · `definition` · Health equity = treating individuals according to their specific health needs/resources, accounting for differences in starting positions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P439** · `definition` · Equality = granting same chances/services/resources to all; equity = compensating avoidable differences based on individual social situation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P440** · `definition` · Health differences = natural (genetic/physiological) variation; health disparities = man-made, result of systemic/structural inequalities · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P441** · `definition` · Social determinants of health = non-medical factors (age, gender, ethnicity, socio-economic status) shaping individual health + access to healthcare · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P442** · `fact` · Health disparities affect individual opportunities for social participation → matter of social justice, not only medicine · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Bias
- **P443** · `definition` · 3-level bias taxonomy: (1) data bias, (2) algorithmic bias, (3) outcome bias · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P444** · `definition` · Data bias = training/analysis data inadequately represents population; algorithmic bias = parameters/target variables exclude certain groups; outcome bias = consequence of data/algorithmic bias on clinical decisions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P445** · `definition` · Signal problem = signals from certain individuals/groups not detected by algorithm → not represented in model · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P446** · `definition` · Bias cascade = data bias → algorithmic bias → outcome bias propagating sequentially through pipeline · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P447** · `fact` · Friedman & Nissenbaum (1996) distinguished preexisting bias (social practices/attitudes), technical bias (computer system mechanisms), emergent bias (outcomes of analysis) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P448** · `fact` · Obermeyer et al. (2019) found US healthcare algorithm used health costs as main risk parameter → African-Americans assigned lower risk labels because less costs historically invested in them due to structural discrimination · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P449** · `fact` · Obermeyer study = exemplar of bias cascade: biased data (cost disparities) → biased algorithm (cost-based risk) → biased outcome (services withheld from African-Americans despite health need) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P450** · `definition` · Ontic occlusion = data model ignores trait → individuals with that trait become invisible → exclusion from data causes social exclusion from healthcare services · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P451** · `definition` · Epistemic injustice in AI = distorted representation of groups in data models → health needs ignored → health disparities exacerbated · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P452** · `definition` · Algorithmovigilance = strategies for evaluating, monitoring, preventing negative outcomes of AI-based treatments (analogous to pharmacovigilance) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P453** · `tension` · Bias not only technical; systemic factors + human-algorithm interaction (e.g., variable selection) also cause bias → debiasing must target social practices + structural factors · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Technical Approaches
- **P454** · `definition` · Algorithmic fairness = strategies for bias mitigation in ML applied at pre-processing, in-processing, post-processing phases · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P455** · `definition` · Importance weighting (pre-processing) = data from underrepresented groups assigned stronger weight/significance in analysis · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P456** · `definition` · Resampling (pre-processing) = correcting original data by obtaining more diverse subsamples accounting for minority groups · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P457** · `definition` · Relabeling (pre-processing) = changing ground truth labels to reduce bias · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P458** · `definition` · Perturbation (pre-processing) = adjusting attribute values so distributions closer together while preserving ranking; iterative until bias < defined threshold · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P459** · `definition` · In-processing: modify loss function by adding regulation term → penalizes discriminatory error margins; or apply constraints defining max bias level during training · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P460** · `definition` · Adversarial learning (in-processing) = classification model predicts ground truth while adversary model exploits fairness issues · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P461** · `definition` · Adjusted learning (in-processing) = external decision makers intervene; classification model learns to abstain from predictions in specific cases · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P462** · `constraint` · In-processing + post-processing bias mitigation may reduce overall model performance/accuracy · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P463** · `definition` · Post-processing bias mitigation = modifying output of already-trained models via input correction, classifier correction, or output correction (modifying predicted labels) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Non-technical Approaches
- **P464** · `definition` · HCAI (Human-Centered AI) = shift focus from algorithms to humans/needs/values; evaluate AI by impact on users + society, not only algorithmic performance · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P465** · `rule` · HCAI principle: high automatization must go hand-in-hand with high human control; applications must be reliable, safe, trustworthy · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P466** · `definition` · Thick data = approach acknowledging social embeddedness of data genesis/collection; data needs contextualization with social background + practices · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P467** · `fact` · Thick data operationalized via ethnographic approaches: interviews, social media analysis, stakeholder workshops → contextualizes health data to overcome signal problem · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P468** · `fact` · EU AI Act (AIA) adopted 2024 by all EU member states; classifies AI in 3 risk classes (low → high); goes beyond medical product regulation to cover non-medical apps (e.g., ChatGPT) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P469** · `fact` · US: HHS + Office of National Coordinator implemented regulations targeting AI-driven discrimination in healthcare, focusing on algorithmic transparency; supplements FDA regulation under FDCA · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P470** · `tension` · Defining "diversity" per use case is difficult; regulations for data diversity must balance with existing data protection laws (GDPR in EU, HIPAA in US) that limit health data collection · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P471** · `tension` · Horizontal legislation (AIA) intersecting vertical regulation (MDR) → risk of conflicts or unnecessary duplication · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

## Chapter 11 — Regulatory Frameworks for AI: Legal and Ethical Perspective

### Transparency and Informed Consent
- **P472** · `fact` · Many AI applications (especially deep learning) function as "black boxes" → healthcare professionals cannot explain decisions → patients cannot make informed choices · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P473** · `tension` · Adaptive AI systems continuously evolve after approval → informed consent once given may no longer be valid if assessment patterns shift · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P474** · `requirement` · Informed consent requires patients comprehend what they agree to; if AI decision-making lacks transparency, this fundamental requirement is undermined · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Bias and Fair Decision-Making
- **P475** · `fact` · AI-assisted skin cancer diagnoses less accurate for individuals with darker skin tones; ER algorithms may unintentionally reinforce social inequalities · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P476** · `rule` · If biases in training data not actively identified/corrected → certain patient groups face systematic disadvantages + research findings lack generalizability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Liability for AI-Supported Decisions
- **P477** · `tension` · AI introduces liability uncertainty: unclear whether responsibility for incorrect diagnoses lies with medical staff, healthcare institutions, or manufacturer · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P478** · `rule` · Medical professionals remain responsible for critically evaluating AI-supported recommendations; manufacturers/facilities accountable for system reliability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P479** · `tension` · Adaptive AI systems evolving after market introduction → liability allocation unclear; absence of clear legal guidelines creates implementation obstacles · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### AI and the Role of the Healthcare Professions
- **P480** · `requirement` · Healthcare professionals must grasp how algorithms function, critically assess outputs, incorporate into interdisciplinary decision-making · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Data Protection and Research
- **P481** · `tension` · Distinction between clinical care and data-driven research increasingly blurred; AI systems evolve through continuous application → patient data used to refine algorithms without explicit consent · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P482** · `tension` · AI models demand extensive/diverse datasets for reliability vs. strict data protection laws limit collection/use of personal data → conflict intensifies in international cross-border research · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Regulatory Framework Conditions in the EU
- **P483** · `fact` · EU AI regulation = 3 pillars: (1) AI Act (cross-industry safety/transparency/risk), (2) GDPR (data protection), (3) MDR (sector-specific medical devices) + non-binding ethical guidelines · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### AI Act: Risk-Based Regulation of AI
- **P484** · `fact` · EU AI Act = first comprehensive AI regulatory framework in EU; classifies AI systems into 4 risk levels (minimal → high-risk) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P485** · `scope` · Most healthcare AI systems fall under high-risk category (assist diagnoses, influence treatment, intervene in medical processes) → strict traceability/transparency/accountability requirements · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P486** · `requirement` · AI Act mandates explainability: medical professionals must understand reasoning behind AI-generated diagnoses/recommendations · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P487** · `obligation` · AI Act requires developers ensure training data is diverse, representative, robust; mandates mechanisms for bias detection, reduction, regular validation of training datasets · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P488** · `requirement` · High-risk adaptive/self-learning AI must remain under human oversight; no uncontrolled modifications; professionals must be able to review/adjust/override AI decisions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P489** · `deadline` · AI Act provisions implemented gradually starting 2026 · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### GDPR: Requirements for the Handling of Health Data
- **P490** · `rule` · GDPR Article 9: health data = specially protected category; processing restricted unless explicit/informed/voluntary consent obtained or legal exemption applies · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P491** · `constraint` · GDPR purpose limitation restricts data use to clearly defined/legitimate purposes → challenges if AI models need to expand scope or retrain with new datasets · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P492** · `constraint` · GDPR data minimization requires processing only essential data → conflicts with need for large/diverse AI training datasets · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P493** · `requirement` · GDPR transparency: data controllers must inform individuals how/why data processed, especially in automated decision-making; complex for deep learning systems · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P494** · `rule` · GDPR: personal data from EU citizens may only be shared with third countries (e.g., US) if adequate protection level ensured → limits international research collaboration data exchange · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P495** · `fact` · Pseudonymization + anonymization used to comply with GDPR while preserving AI training data quality · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### MDR: Requirements for AI-Supported Medical Devices
- **P496** · `definition` · MDR risk-based classification: Class I (low risk, admin software, minimal regulation), Class IIa/IIb (medium-high risk, diagnostic/therapy/monitoring, extensive scrutiny), Class III (high risk, independent decisions/active intervention, comprehensive clinical evaluation required) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P497** · `rule` · MDR: manufacturers must demonstrate safety/effectiveness via technical documentation + clinical evaluation + risk management protocols regardless of risk class · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P498** · `rule` · MDR: Class I devices → self-declaration by manufacturer; Class IIa+ → conformity assessment by notified body (external accredited inspector) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P499** · `obligation` · MDR mandates post-market surveillance (PMS) for continued safety/performance of AI medical devices; especially critical for adaptive AI with continuous learning · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P500** · `requirement` · MDR: adaptive AI deployed post-market must remain compliant with regulatory standards; requires ongoing testing + regular reassessments if significant modifications occur · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Ethical Guidelines for AI in the EU
- **P501** · `fact` · EU ethical guidelines for trustworthy AI are non-binding but set best practices; emphasize human-centric design, fairness/non-discrimination, accountability, data protection/transparency · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P502** · `rule` · AI Act prohibits autonomous decision-making in high-risk AI; ethical guidelines go further → AI must not create dependency or reduce medical staff responsibility · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P503** · `fact` · Many EU companies voluntarily follow ALTAI (Assessment List for Trustworthy AI) or participate in EU AI Alliance to implement ethical standards · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Medical Device Regulations and the Role of the FDA
- **P504** · `definition` · SaMD (Software as a Medical Device) = stand-alone AI applications (diagnostic algorithms, therapy planning); SiMD (Software in a Medical Device) = software integrated into physical devices (imaging, surgical assistance) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P505** · `fact` · FDA = primary US regulatory authority for AI healthcare applications; oversees approval/monitoring of SaMD + SiMD · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P506** · `fact` · FDA categorizes AI medical devices by risk/clinical significance: low-risk (admin software, general standards only) → higher-risk (diagnosis/treatment, formal approval) → high-risk (cancer detection, surgical assistance, rigorous testing) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P507** · `definition` · FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan = manufacturers pre-register planned modifications → post-approval algorithm updates without full re-review per change · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P508** · `tension` · FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan accelerates AI development but raises concerns about long-term oversight/traceability vs. EU AI Act mandates continuous validation of learning systems · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Data Protection Framework Conditions (US)
- **P509** · `scope` · HIPAA governs Protected Health Information (PHI); applies only to "Covered Entities" (hospitals, insurers, healthcare providers) → many AI developers/tech companies (health apps, wearables, AI diagnostics) not subject to HIPAA · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P510** · `fact` · HIPAA allows broader/more flexible data use than GDPR: health data can be leveraged for AI model development/optimization without same restrictions as EU · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P511** · `fact` · US lacks uniform federal data protection framework; state-level laws (CCPA, CPRA in California) grant additional consumer rights but apply only within their state → inconsistent landscape · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Ethical Guidelines and Voluntary Self-Regulation (US)
- **P512** · `fact` · NIST AI Risk Management Framework offers recommendations on fairness/transparency/security but carries no legal weight · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P513** · `fact` · Biden administration Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights promoted human-centric AI principles (data protection, security, non-discrimination) but served only as guidance, no legal obligations · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Comparison: Regulatory Approach
- **P514** · `fact` · EU = preventive, risk-based, uniform framework (AI Act + MDR + GDPR); US = sector-specific, market-driven, flexible (FDA + HIPAA) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P515** · `fact` · EU: manufacturers liable for high-risk AI, unclear for evolving AI; US: doctors/hospitals liable for AI errors, manufacturers only for clear product defects · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P516** · `fact` · EU mandates transparency/explainability for high-risk AI; US has no legal transparency mandate → voluntary industry self-regulation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P517** · `tension` · EU rigorous compliance process slows innovation; US flexible system accelerates deployment but → greater uncertainty for patients/providers (no consistent bias mitigation/monitoring requirements) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Data Protection Comparison
- **P518** · `fact` · GDPR data minimization + purpose limitation restrict AI training; HIPAA applies only to covered entities → many tech companies processing health data not subject to HIPAA · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P519** · `tension` · GDPR opening clauses allow EU member states supplementary regulations → further fragmentation within EU despite uniform framework design · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P520** · `tension` · International health data exchange hampered: EU entities must comply with strict GDPR; US lacks equivalent comprehensive framework → legal discrepancies hinder cross-border research + may disadvantage EU companies globally · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Liability for AI Decisions
- **P521** · `rule` · EU: primary liability on manufacturers of high-risk AI (AI Act + MDR); doctors must independently verify AI recommendations; uncertainty if self-learning AI alters decisions post-market → unclear if re-certification required · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P522** · `rule` · US: no specific AI liability regulation; governed by general product liability + medical malpractice; doctors primarily liable for adopting incorrect AI recommendation; manufacturers liable only if clear product defect demonstrated · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P523** · `fact` · FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan allows post-approval AI model updates without full re-evaluation → approved AI may change decision-making without new regulatory review · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Outlook and Future Challenges
- **P524** · `tension` · EU AI Act uncertainty: how much can approved AI system evolve before requiring recertification; practical implementation challenges remain · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P525** · `definition` · Regulatory sandboxes = controlled environments where new AI technologies tested before final market approval; proposed solution for balancing innovation + oversight · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P526** · `fact` · XAI (Explainable AI) increasingly important for regulatory compliance with accountability + patient education requirements · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P527** · `fact` · Federated learning + privacy-preserving AI models = potential solutions for leveraging large health datasets while complying with privacy regulations · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P528** · `tension` · GDPR national opening clauses → member states impose varying requirements on medical data processing → AI companies in some EU countries have easier access to health data than others · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

## Chapter 12 — Ethical Theories for AI in Healthcare

### Ethics Concepts and Theories
- **P531** · `definition` · Common morality = rules about right/wrong conduct widely accepted across cultures and moral traditions; includes prohibitions against lying, stealing, causing harm · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P532** · `definition` · Utilitarianism = moral worth of actions determined by consequences/outcomes; action ethical if benefits exceed harms; derives from John Stuart Mill · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P533** · `fact` · Public health policies often rely on utilitarian framework, e.g., promoting vaccines with side effects for few while benefiting many · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P534** · `rule` · Utilitarian approach to AI requires critical examination of both favorable and adverse consequences, including over-reliance on AI and biased outcomes affecting marginalized groups · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P535** · `definition` · Deontology = adherence to moral duties/rules; certain actions inherently right or wrong regardless of consequences; derives from Immanuel Kant · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P536** · `obligation` · Deontological approach: healthcare providers must ensure patients fully informed about AI role in care, potential benefits/risks, even if AI improves diagnostic accuracy · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P537** · `definition` · Virtue ethics = emphasizes character and desirable moral qualities of decision-makers; shifts focus from rigid rules or consequences; derives from Aristotle · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P538** · `rule` · Virtue ethics requires cultivating compassion, integrity, fairness in healthcare professionals navigating AI; physician must consider ethical implications of AI recommendations on patient well-being · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P539** · `definition` · Principlism (Four Principles Approach) = framework derived from Beauchamp & Childress combining 4 principles: beneficence, non-maleficence, respect for autonomy, justice/fairness · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P540** · `definition` · Beneficence = moral obligation to act to benefit others · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P541** · `definition` · Non-maleficence = moral obligation to avoid or prevent harm to others · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P542** · `definition` · Respect for autonomy = obligation to honor individual's right to determine what is in their best interests · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P543** · `definition` · Justice/Fairness = obligation to ensure benefits and burdens distributed fairly; equals treated equally, unequals treated appropriately per norms of just society · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Ethical Concerns Associated with AI in Healthcare
- **P544** · `definition` · Patient-centered care = providing care respectful of and responsive to individual patient preferences, needs, values; patient values guide all clinical decisions (Institute of Medicine / National Academy of Medicine) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P545** · `fact` · Community-centered care requires consideration of social determinants of health (SDOH) and community values for policy-making when adopting AI tools · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P546** · `definition` · Equity (AHRQ) = providing care that does not vary in quality because of gender, ethnicity, geographic location, socioeconomic status · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P547** · `fact` · AI systems trained on data not fairly representing diverse populations may perpetuate/exacerbate health disparities; studies show humans inherit AI biases · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P548** · `obligation` · Patients must be informed when AI influences their care; clinician obligated to advise patient of AI recommendation and ensure patient understands reasons for accepting/rejecting it · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P549** · `tension` · Current consent processes may be inadequate when patients/providers do not fully understand AI technology involved · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P550** · `fact` · PHI in electronic databases presents potential for accidental/criminal release; very limited effective regulatory oversight of AI data security at present · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Accuracy and Interpretability
- **P551** · `fact` · AI can fabricate data appearing authentic (hallucinations), use wrong data, or ignore relevant data → flawed outputs with potential for harm · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P552** · `fact` · Consensus: AI not ready for unsupervised use in most clinical settings due to inability to reliably identify errors or prevent hallucinations · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P553** · `definition` · Interpretability = ease with which humans can understand and rely on AI recommendations/decisions; sine qua non for trust and usability in clinical setting · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Accountability and Responsibility
- **P554** · `fact` · Parties involved with AI in clinical setting: healthcare providers, AI developers (individual/corporate), implementing institutions; clear lines of accountability essential for patient safety · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P555** · `rule` · Clinician who relies on AI must own responsibility for ethical/legal ramifications of AI-based decisions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P556** · `obligation` · AI developers bear responsibility for ensuring technologies safe, reliable, free from bias, used appropriately; must conduct rigorous testing, maintain transparency, regularly update systems · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P557** · `requirement` · Governments/regulators must establish clear regulatory frameworks defining roles/responsibilities of stakeholders in AI development and deployment; address data privacy, algorithmic transparency, liability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P558** · `requirement` · Education/training for clinicians on technical aspects of AI models and ethical implications must be provided · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P559** · `requirement` · Collaboration between AI developers, healthcare providers, patients, ethicists, business interests, regulatory bodies must be required to facilitate accountability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Erosion of Trust and the Clinician Patient Relationship
- **P560** · `tension` · AI integration risks diminishing personal aspect of healthcare; patients may feel interacting with machines rather than human caregivers → reduced open communication · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P561** · `tension` · Reliance on AI may lead to complacency among healthcare professionals, undermining cognitive/procedural skills, clinical judgment and expertise · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Evaluate AI's Impact on Patient Care
- **P562** · `fact` · Most successful AI diagnostic uses: assessing visual images (radiologic, ophthalmologic, dermatologic); AI can match/surpass radiologists in certain contexts · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P563** · `constraint` · AI image interpretation not yet at accuracy/reliability level to dispense with human clinician oversight; cognitive diagnosis AI not nearly as evolved · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P564** · `fact` · AI can personalize treatment plans using medical records, genetic information, lifestyle factors, personal health monitoring devices · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Monitoring AI in Healthcare
- **P565** · `requirement` · Continuous AI oversight via: regular audits of algorithms/transformers, monitoring discrepancies between AI recommendations and actual outcomes, human oversight in decision-making · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P566** · `requirement` · Clear performance metrics must be established for assessing reliability/accuracy of AI recommendations over time · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P567** · `requirement` · AI developers must provide clear documentation of data/methodologies used to train AI systems and how systems arrive at conclusions; accessible to healthcare professionals · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P568** · `requirement` · Feedback loops allowing healthcare professionals to report adverse outcomes/concerns about AI systems must be implemented · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Guidelines to Effect Ethically Responsible Use of AI

- **P570** · `definition` · WHO 6 core principles: (1) protect autonomy; (2) promote human well-being, safety, public interest; (3) ensure transparency, explainability, intelligibility; (4) foster responsibility/accountability; (5) ensure inclusiveness/equity; (6) promote responsive/sustainable AI · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

- **P573** · `fact` · No comprehensive standardized ethics guidelines specifically tailored to unique challenges of AI in healthcare exist yet · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Proposed Ethical Guidelines for AI in Healthcare

- **P579** · `rule` · Guideline 6 (Continuous Monitoring): AI systems subject to ongoing evaluation — regular audits, safety assessments, updates reflecting latest medical knowledge · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P580** · `rule` · Guideline 7 (Stakeholder Engagement): Wide range of stakeholders (patients, providers, developers, ethicists, regulators, policymakers) must be engaged in guideline development · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

- **P583** · `rule` · Guideline 10 (Regulatory Frameworks): Governments/regulatory bodies must create comprehensive legal frameworks — standards for AI, compliance monitoring, accountability enforcement · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Four-Box Approach

- **P592** · `scope` · Box 2 (Preferences of Patient or Other Beneficiary) addresses autonomy: POB informed of benefits/risks/alternatives, decisional capacity, preferred option, valid reasons to override · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P593** · `scope` · Box 3 (Quality of Outcomes) addresses beneficence/non-maleficence/autonomy: prospects with/without tool, bias detection (conscious/unconscious/unknown + power relationships), quality-of-outcome assessments · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
