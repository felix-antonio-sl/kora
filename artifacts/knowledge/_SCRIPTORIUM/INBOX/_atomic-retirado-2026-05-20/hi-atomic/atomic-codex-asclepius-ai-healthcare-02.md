### Transition to the IRLM
- **P204** · `definition` · IRLM (Implementation Research Logic Model) = integrated model combining determinants + implementation strategies + mechanisms of change + outcomes in one framework; extends traditional logic model · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P205** · `fact` · IRLM developed because projects sometimes used multiple disparate models, failed to justify how context + actions + results fit together · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P206** · `fact` · IRLM adds rigor/transparency by making causal pathways + assumptions explicit → improves scientific rigor, reproducibility, ability to test how/why implementation succeeds · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P207** · `definition` · IRLM Determinants = contextual factors (barriers/facilitators) influencing implementation success; e.g., organizational structures, provider readiness, patient engagement, resource constraints, stakeholder commitment, workflow alignment · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P208** · `definition` · IRLM Implementation Strategies = targeted actions to facilitate implementation by addressing barriers + leveraging facilitators; each strategy should correspond to specific determinant · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P209** · `fact` · Powell et al. (2015) provide refined compilation of 73 implementation strategies based on expert consensus (ERIC project) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P210** · `rule` · IRLM requires specifying who implements strategies, what actions taken, why — ensuring clear evidence-based rationale for each approach · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P211** · `definition` · IRLM Mechanisms = processes/mediators/events explaining how implementation strategy → change; clarify causal link between strategy and intended outcomes · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P212** · `fact` · Mechanisms can manifest as shifts in determinant factors (e.g., improved organizational climate) or proximal changes (e.g., evolving user attitudes) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P213** · `fact` · Fogg Behavior Model: motivation + ability + triggers → behavior change; applicable as theoretical underpinning for mechanism design in IRLM · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P214** · `definition` · IRLM Outcomes = 3 categories: (a) implementation outcomes (adoption rates, fidelity, acceptability), (b) service outcomes (efficiency, quality of care, safety), (c) recipient/clinical outcomes (diagnostics, treatment, hospitalizations) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Key Components of the IRLM
- **P215** · `definition` · IRLM determinants at 3 levels: patient/workforce level, organizational level, macro level · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P216** · `fact` · Patient/workforce determinants: trust in AI-driven care (explainability, alignment with human decision-making, AI literacy, data security fears) + clinician AI literacy/trust (training gaps, liability/responsibility concerns) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P217** · `fact` · Organizational determinants: workflow compatibility/EHR interoperability (bias, privacy, security risks) + leadership/institutional commitment (governance policies, AI-friendly infrastructure) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P218** · `fact` · Macro determinants: regulatory uncertainty/liability risks + economic incentives/reimbursement models · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P219** · `fact` · Patient/workforce strategies: AI literacy + patient engagement programs (transparency, informed consent) + clinician AI education + decision-support training (simulations) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P220** · `fact` · Organizational strategies: seamless AI integration into clinical workflows (EHR embedding, decision-support pathways) + strong leadership/AI governance policies (multidisciplinary task forces) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P221** · `fact` · Macro strategies: regulatory standardization/liability frameworks + financial incentives/reimbursement mechanisms (value-based incentives) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P222** · `fact` · Patient/workforce mechanisms: increased transparency → trust-building + cognitive alignment → decision augmentation (AI insights aligned with medical reasoning) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P223** · `fact` · Organizational mechanisms: reduced cognitive load + workflow efficiency (AI embedded in EHR) + AI accountability → institutional trust (clear governance → reduced liability concerns) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P224** · `fact` · Macro mechanisms: legal certainty + risk mitigation (regulatory standardization) + economic feasibility/sustainability (reimbursement models) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P225** · `fact` · Implementation outcomes: patient acceptance of AI tools + clinician adoption/sustained use + successful AI system integration + AI governance/compliance adherence + regulatory approval/legal acceptance + economic viability/financial sustainability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P226** · `fact` · Service outcomes: improved patient engagement/self-management + reduced clinician workload/burnout + increased hospital workflow efficiency + higher clinical decision-making accuracy + improved population health monitoring/early disease detection + enhanced healthcare system responsiveness · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P227** · `fact` · Recipient outcomes: better health outcomes/quality of life + increased clinician decision confidence/decreased interrater variability + reduction in medical errors/adverse events + better patient-provider communication/shared decision-making + reduction in healthcare disparities + sustainable health system cost reduction · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P228** · `rule` · IRLM structured mapping = testable hypothesis: "Implementing Strategy X to address Barrier Y → triggers Mechanism Z → leads to Outcome O" · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P229** · `fact` · ML prediction model outperformed statistical scores predicting major adverse events in cardiac ICU (death, resuscitated cardiac arrest, cardiogenic shock) → helped risk stratification · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P230** · `fact` · LLM generated better material for patient education + shared decision-making compared to existing sources · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P231** · `fact` · Cost simulation for colorectal cancer: AI screening → reduced incidence/mortality rates → cost reductions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Complementary Role of Other Implementation Frameworks

- **P238** · `definition` · CFIR (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research) identifies key implementation determinants: organizational readiness + external influences; useful for understanding barriers/facilitators · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Human-Centered Implementation Science
- **P241** · `definition` · Human-centered implementation science extends user-centricity into integration/uptake process; prioritizes user needs, workflow integration, training, organizational culture equally with technical installation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P242** · `rule` · Principle 1 "Meeting Users Where They Are": adapt AI to fit existing routines/systems, not expect users to radically change; embed AI alerts into EHR interface; time outputs to clinical decision points; maintain clinician autonomy — AI supports, not overrides judgment · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P243** · `fact` · AI tools not woven into clinicians' normal processes quickly fall by wayside (known barrier) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P244** · `rule` · Principle 2 "Training and Support": implementation plans must include robust education/training (formal sessions + quick reference guides + responsive IT support); goal = build user confidence + competence; ongoing support via helpdesk/regular check-ins · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P245** · `fact` · AI sepsis early warning system deployment: comprehensive training + communication about AI purpose/workflow essential for frontline staff preparation + smooth adoption · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P246** · `rule` · Principle 3 "Champion Engagement + Leadership Buy-In": identify/empower clinical champions (respected end-users who advocate for AI tool); champions communicate benefits to peers + mentor colleagues; organizational leadership support signals change is valued + allocates resources · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P247** · `rule` · Principle 4 "Iterative Adaptation + Feedback Loops": treat deployment as iterative process not one-time event; collect user feedback + performance data systematically; use debrief meetings after pilot phases, monitor usage patterns/outcomes, establish accessible feedback channels (surveys) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P248** · `fact` · In one AI deployment, regular meetings with nurses/physicians identified workflow misalignments → modifications in alert escalation pathways to fit clinical roles · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Outlook and Conclusions
- **P249** · `tension` · AI applications appear so stunning that attention solely rests on what application achieves + how much better single-task performance vs humans; context/user-driven mindset needed to lead implementation → clinically meaningful success · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P250** · `fact` · Past decades witnessed great failures putting grand technology plans into practice (e.g., national eHealth strategies); AI applications carry larger portion of risks including failure risks vs previous technologies · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P251** · `rule` · AI applications require environment to be factored in during implementation; measures may be similar to other technologies but impact more crucial due to potential AI harms + benefits · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P252** · `requirement` · Strong need to evaluate overall AI system performance guided by logical models + their pathways toward system impact; context + human-centered designs + ethical considerations = bridge between artificial and human intelligence during implementation/evaluation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)


## Chapter 6 — Artificial Intelligence in Dermatology

### Literature Review: AI in Dermatology Over the Past Decade
- **P253** · `fact` · DL outperformed 136/157 dermatologists in head-to-head dermoscopic melanoma classification task · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P254** · `fact` · Esteva et al. (2017) trained CNN on 129,450 clinical images across 2,032 diseases; performed on par with 21 board-certified dermatologists classifying 2 critical skin cancer types · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P255** · `fact` · Tschandl et al. (2019) showed ML algorithms outperformed human experts classifying pigmented skin lesions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P256** · `tension` · AI models face challenges with out-of-distribution images → reduced reliability outside training distribution · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P257** · `fact` · AI-assisted tele-dermatology diagnosis improved diagnostic accuracy by up to 12% and reduced unnecessary referrals/biopsies · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P258** · `fact` · AI-assisted diagnosis increases agreement between primary care physicians and reference dermatologists · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Use Case: MoleMe
- **P259** · `definition` · MoleMe = AI-powered skin monitoring app launched 2019 in Taiwan by dermatologists + AI researchers; analyzes moles/lesions for early malignancy signs · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P260** · `fact` · MoleMe used by > 200,000 users · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P261** · `fact` · MoleMe AI core trained on > 30,000 consumer-taken mole images; risk ground truth determined by board-certified dermatologists · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P262** · `fact` · Each original image spawned into 20 derived images (different lighting, background hues, sizes, angles) → robust to skin tones + photo-taking skill variation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P263** · `fact` · MoleMe classifies lesions into benign / suspicious / malignant categories · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P264** · `fact` · MoleMe integrates telemedicine for remote dermatologist consultations · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P265** · `fact` · MoleMe AUC = 0.94 (ROC curve), published in British Journal of Dermatology; outperforms most GPs classifying cutaneous pigmented lesions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P266** · `fact` · MoleMe sensitivity = 0.96, specificity = 0.87 · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P267** · `fact` · > 90% users satisfied with MoleMe usability + positive impact on daily life · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P268** · `constraint` · AI-powered medical tools must undergo rigorous validation, clinical testing, approval before deployment; regulatory frameworks favour high-resource environments → barriers for LMICs · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P269** · `tension` · Most AI training data comes from western countries; most dermatology textbook skin images = Type I (white) skin → perpetuates health inequities · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P270** · `requirement` · AI models need sufficient external validations during evaluation to avoid overfitting · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P271** · `requirement` · MoleMe should integrate into pre-visit process of primary care practices for full potential · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Comparative Analysis of AI Dermatology Tools
- **P272** · `fact` · MoleMe: AI diagnosis=Yes, mobile app=Yes, teledermatology=Yes, regulatory approval=Pending, data training diversity=Moderate · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P273** · `fact` · DermEngine: AI diagnosis=Yes, mobile app=Yes, teledermatology=Yes, regulatory approval=Approved, data training diversity=High · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

- **P275** · `fact` · MoleMe = only tool of 3 with teledermatology + pending regulatory approval; DermEngine = only tool with high data training diversity · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### 4-Stage AI Evolution in Dermatology
- **P276** · `definition` · Stage 1: Predictive (Perception) AI = analyzes medical images to detect patterns + classify skin conditions using DL on large datasets · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P277** · `tension` · Patients require AI to surpass dermatologist-level accuracy or healthcare cost to be unbearable before accepting AI-only assessments · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P278** · `definition` · Stage 2: Generative AI = GANs + GPT synthesize realistic skin lesion images, enrich training datasets, mitigate annotated image scarcity for underrepresented skin tones · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P279** · `fact` · Generative AI enables simulation of disease progression (e.g., nevi → melanoma evolution) for training + patient education · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P280** · `fact` · LLMs (ChatGPT) show promise providing secondary opinions on dermatological diagnosis/treatments; limitations persist in medication coding + specificity · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P281** · `definition` · Stage 3: Agentic AI = shift from passive decision-support → systems autonomously guiding clinical workflows, synthesizing multimodal patient data with minimal human intervention · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P282** · `tension` · Agentic AI deployment must balance efficiency vs care quality, broader access vs preservation of patient-clinician relationships · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P283** · `definition` · Stage 4: Physical AI = humanoid robots conducting dermatological assessments via advanced image recognition + tactile sensing · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P284** · `fact` · Automated robotic devices already explored for hair transplant + energy-based treatments (laser resurfacing, mole removal) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P285** · `tension` · Psychological acceptance of humanoid robots limited by "uncanny valley effect" · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Ethical and Systematic Considerations

- **P289** · `tension` · Tele-dermatology AI raises ethical challenges: exacerbation of health disparities due to lack of standardized regulations / informed consent protocols · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P290** · `obligation` · Ethical principles for AI in dermatology: fairness, inclusivity, transparency, accountability, privacy · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### AI-Based Fall Detection Sensors
- **P303** · `fact` · ALGOCARE fall detection system used 3D sensors gathering depth data about older adults' movement in rooms; ML algorithms processed data to identify objects/individuals/movements · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

- **P305** · `tension` · Major challenge: unavailability of training data on older adults' falls; real-world data hard to collect due to restricted access to diverse older populations · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

- **P307** · `tension` · Synthetic data creation excluded older adults from development process → voices/needs relatively absent in AI development · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### The Humanoid Robot Pepper
- **P308** · `definition` · Pepper = 1.2m-tall social robot with human-like body, moveable arms/fingers, wheels; uses microphones, sensors, 3D cameras for navigation/speech/hearing/object+face detection; chest-integrated tablet; produced since 2014 · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P309** · `fact` · Mishra et al. (2024) tested Pepper functions → found issues with face recognition, navigation, conversation accuracy · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P310** · `fact` · Stommel et al. observed miscommunication in all 36 interviews with older adults using Pepper, particularly "trouble hearing" → repetition + frustration · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P311** · `tension` · Pepper adjusted entertainment content based on estimated age + facial expressions → decisions made for older adults rather than by them; positioned older adults as passive users · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P312** · `fact` · Pepper use did not ease caregiver workload; required additional staff time/resources to facilitate resident-robot interactions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### The Robot Seal Paro
- **P313** · `definition` · Paro = robotic seal; development began 1993 as activation therapy for older adults, often used with dementia patients; responds to touch + speech; example of emotional robotics · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P314** · `fact` · Paro design chosen as baby seal because unfamiliar animals accepted more easily; features: light sensors, touch sensors (head/whiskers/flippers/back/belly), anti-bacterial white coat, "baby face" with large eyes · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P315** · `fact` · In Japan > 60% Paro robots owned by private customers; in Europe + US mostly used in public LTC settings · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P316** · `fact` · Paro acquisition cost ~6,000 EUR + training costs for care staff · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P317** · `tension` · Paro challenges: infection concerns (non-removable/non-washable fur passed between residents), stigma of interacting with animal robot perceived as "toylike" → infantilization, negative emotional responses (fear/anger) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P318** · `fact` · Paro hardly used during Covid-19 peak phase; care staff feared cross-infection between residents via robot · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P319** · `fact` · Paro fur turned yellow over time from repeated disinfectant wipe use · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P320** · `fact` · Care staff used Paro to engage older residents in dialogues about past experiences + provided information about how system worked · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Pathways Towards AI-Enriched Long-Term Care
- **P321** · `requirement` · Pathway 1: Actively involve older adults in AI development; participatory/responsible innovation strategies not yet fully developed/contextualized for LTC sector · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P322** · `tension` · AI developers used synthetic data / existing datasets as cheaper alternatives → hardly engaged with LTC sector, little knowledge about actual settings → stereotypical assumptions about older adults · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P323** · `requirement` · Pathway 2: Acknowledge both human + technological vulnerabilities; AI technologies need care too (maintenance, cleaning, updates) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

- **P325** · `requirement` · Pathway 3: Foster meaningful connections via shared learning spaces where technology developers, care staff, older adults exchange knowledge · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P326** · `rule` · AI in LTC = not technological optimization alone; requires social, material, ethical considerations; AI should be part of care network, not replacement for human care · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P327** · `fact` · ALGOCARE project funded by Vienna Science and Technology Fund (WWTF) + State of Lower Austria, project ICT20-055 · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

## Chapter 8 — Generative AI to Assist Physicians (Rutledge)

### Introduction
- **P328** · `definition` · Generative AI / LLMs = AI applications using generative methods based on large language models · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P329** · `fact` · Pre-LLM AI methods (rule-based expert systems, Bayesian networks, ML) suffered "cliff effect" when confronted with inputs not explicitly in knowledge representation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P330** · `fact` · Largest LLMs embed vast information array → respond appropriately to virtually any human expression · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P331** · `definition` · Hallucination = LLM fills knowledge gaps with reasonable-sounding output lacking direct support; analogous to confabulation in Korsakoff syndrome · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Foundation Model LLMs Versus Medical LLMs
- **P332** · `definition` · Foundation model LLMs = models with trillions of parameters trained on comprehensive collections of essentially all available electronic documents · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P333** · `fact` · Foundation model LLMs (early 2025): Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT, Google Gemini, Meta LLaMA, Mistral · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P334** · `fact` · Foundation models perform surprisingly well on medical problems despite no specific medical curation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P335** · `fact` · Hallucinations occur where gaps in knowledge exist; medical applications demand minimal confabulation rate · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P336** · `fact` · Medical LLMs refine base models with curated reliable medical data to improve scope + minimize confabulation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P337** · `fact` · Medical LLM examples: Google Med-PaLM (medical reasoning/Q&A), Nuance DAX Copilot + AWS Healthscribe (medical scribing → EHR notes), Hippocratic AI (medical conversations), Clinical Camel (open-source on LLaMA) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P338** · `tension` · Medical LLMs tailored to specific clinical task may be less performant at interpreting patient interview language because underlying foundation model is smaller · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Administrative Simplification — Claims Coding
- **P339** · `fact` · Fathom + Nym Health use clinical language understanding to fully automate CPT/HCPCS procedure code generation and verify ICD-10 codes support selected procedures · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P340** · `fact` · Claims-coding applications trained specifically on structured coding systems (CPT, ICD-10) can use smaller open-source foundation models with incremental training · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Administrative Simplification — Managing Message Queues
- **P341** · `fact` · Stanford study used Epic MyChart + GPT-3.5 Turbo (triage) + GPT-4 (draft responses); categories = general, results, medications, paperwork · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P342** · `fact` · AI draft response used 20% of time; did not reduce average clinician response time · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P343** · `tension` · Clinician reactions heterogeneous: some valued reduced cognitive effort + patient-friendly language; others found AI responses too lengthy / contained irrelevant information · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P344** · `fact` · Successful AI message response implementation may require fine-tuning to match each clinician's desired response characteristics · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Clinical Documentation — Chart Review and Summarization
- **P345** · `fact` · AI-authored summaries of prior medical records = similar quality to clinician summaries but less likely to omit important information + more patient-friendly language · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P346** · `fact` · Breast cancer study: AI-generated + AI-assisted summaries better than human summaries; human = 26 min effort vs AI-generated = 1.7 min · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P347** · `fact` · Health systems implementing ambient listening medical scribes as first AI use in clinical workflow → reduced after-hours documentation ("pajama time"), reduced cognitive effort · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Clinical Documentation — Ambient Listening
- **P348** · `definition` · Ambient listening = recording + processing audio of doctor-patient encounter → extract relevant clinical features → generate clinical note documenting subjective history + objective findings · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P349** · `fact` · Commercial ambient listening applications: Abridge AI, Augmedix, Robin Healthcare, IKS Health · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P350** · `tension` · AI scribes may misinterpret medical terms, omit critical details, fail to capture key clinical findings; larger concern = doctors relying on AI notes without reviewing for gaps · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P351** · `requirement` · AI scribes require very high note completeness/accuracy rate + very low hallucination rate; further studies needed to confirm performance · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Differential Diagnosis
- **P352** · `fact` · Largest LLMs have sufficient embedded medical knowledge to independently review clinical findings → suggest likely diagnoses; diagnostic performance consistently better than experts · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P353** · `fact` · GPT-4 achieved 96% accuracy diagnosing common ambulatory care cases vs 72% for doctors · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P354** · `fact` · GPT-4 included correct diagnosis in top-10 differential for 61% of complex cases vs medical residents 44% vs medical faculty 49% · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P355** · `fact` · Studies used foundation LLM models (GPT-4) not fine-tuned for medical applications; medical LLMs have not yet demonstrated significantly better diagnostic performance · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P356** · `tension` · Unclear if advances in medical LLMs will outpace advances in general-purpose LLMs; for interpreting patient dialog, general-purpose LLMs are exceptionally capable · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Will AI Replace Doctors?
- **P357** · `fact` · LLMs fall short at taking patient history: when interacting directly with patients, fail to identify all relevant features → diagnostic performance drops · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P358** · `constraint` · LLMs lack planning capability to systematically pursue structured inquiry areas (congenital, inflammatory, toxic, traumatic, neoplastic, degenerative etiologies) as physicians do · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P359** · `fact` · Without planning abilities, LLMs ask about features present in most likely explanations only, missing systematic coverage · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P360** · `constraint` · Treatment planning requires considering: confirmatory vs exclusionary testing, observation vs therapeutic trial, cost barriers, patient preferences → beyond current LLM capability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P361** · `fact` · AI not ready to replace doctors but can operate in supervisory capacity: identify care gaps, suggest alternative diagnoses, expand differential, provide background suggestions · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### An AI-Based Physician Assistant
- **P362** · `definition` · Dr. A.I. = GPT-4-based virtual physician assistant on HealthTap; conducts pre-visit patient interview via API, generates draft clinical note for doctor · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P363** · `rule` · Dr. A.I. does not offer patient any diagnoses or treatment plans; asks questions until no more value, fixed question limit reached, or patient ends interview · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P364** · `fact` · Dr. A.I. builds + stores computed differential diagnosis but does not show differential to doctor · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### An AI-Based Physician Assistant — Evaluation
- **P365** · `fact` · Dr. A.I. evaluation: doctor's 1st ICD-10 diagnosis found in Dr. A.I. top-10 differential = 88% (109/124), top-3 = 81% (100/124), top-1 = 62% (77/124) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P366** · `fact` · Doctor's 2nd ICD-10 diagnosis found in Dr. A.I. top-10 = 80% (45/56), top-3 = 61% (34/56), top-1 = 23% (13/56) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P367** · `fact` · Doctor's 3rd-7th ICD-10 diagnoses found in Dr. A.I. top-10 = 46% (25/55), top-3 = 27% (15/55), top-1 = 4% (2/55) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P368** · `fact` · GPT-4-based patient interview diagnoses correspond to high degree with diagnoses assessed by doctors who evaluated patients · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Outlook and Conclusions
- **P369** · `rule` · GPT-4 cannot be used in medical settings without direct human supervision for foreseeable future · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P370** · `fact` · Rapid adoption already occurring: administrative applications, clinical summarization, ambient listening · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P371** · `constraint` · AI replacing doctors requires: planning capability, understanding individual patient preferences, empathetic/compassionate support for physical/mental distress → remains far off · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

## Chapter 9 — AI Supporting Nursing Documentation, Workflows and Patient Care (Hovenga)

### Introduction: Artificial Intelligence and Nursing Practice
- **P372** · `fact` · AI evolution began simultaneously with computing technologies; AI technologies most commonly relate to data + information processing, a core nursing/midwifery function · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P373** · `rule` · AI needs big, accurate, complete, unbiased data to provide meaningful results · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P374** · `requirement` · Nursing documentation must adopt global standard language + data structures for point-of-care data to serve as source data for AI applications · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P375** · `scope` · AI support for nursing covers: patient safety, workflows, demonstrating nursing value, decision support algorithms, resource management, data analytics, continuity of care, cross-organization research, interoperability, person-centred outcomes · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### An Important AI Pre-requisite: Quality Data
- **P376** · `definition` · Data quality characteristics = accuracy, consistency, validity, timeliness, accessibility, reliability, completeness, uniqueness, comprehensiveness · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P377** · `requirement` · Generating large datasets for AI requires system interoperability → compliance with agreed technical standards · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P378** · `fact` · Author advocates ISO 18104 standard categorial structure to represent nursing practice in terminological systems; linkable to any standard nursing terminology (SNT) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P379** · `fact` · ISO 18104 categories can relate to clinical knowledge models (archetypes) structured per ISO 13606-2:2019 standard in next-generation EHR systems · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P380** · `fact` · Model attributes bound to SNTs → used as standard nursing data value sets → optimizes semantic interoperability · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### AI Sciences and Technologies
- **P381** · `definition` · AI capabilities stack (Peter & Riemer, 7 levels): 1) pattern recognition, 2) classification, 3) prediction, 4) recommendation, 5) automation, 6) content generation, 7) user interaction (chatbots/avatars) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P382** · `definition` · Robot (ISO 8373) = programmed actuated mechanism with degree of autonomy to perform locomotion, manipulation, or positioning; includes control system · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P383** · `tension` · Scanning big data to distinguish/segment based on common elements may produce biased/inaccurate results if data not representative of domain as whole · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P384** · `tension` · Generative AI risks production of deepfakes; recommendation systems risk "group think" based on frequent concept use preventing innovation · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P385** · `requirement` · Computer processing optimal when data standardized at back end; requires cognizance of data supply chain (collection → processing → transfer → linkage → storage → retrieval) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Use of AI Technologies and Risk Mitigation
- **P386** · `fact` · Clinical data aggregated/processed for: best practice determination, outcomes monitoring, public health, health system performance reporting · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)

### Foundational Data Concepts and Interoperability
- **P391** · `definition` · Data = re-interpretable representations of information in formalized manner suitable for communication/interpretation/processing (ISO/IEC 11179) · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P392** · `definition` · Data elements = descriptors of things/concepts/codes; range from atomic to most general level of granularity · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P393** · `fact` · Interoperability today achieved via: FHIR, openEHR archetypes, OMOP CDM · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P394** · `fact` · openEHR archetypes = object models incorporating context; can model agent behaviours; gathering momentum for next-generation systems · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P395** · `fact` · AI use of clinical data requires access to large number of data points available through openEHR methodologies or large proprietary vendors using cloud + AI capabilities · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P396** · `requirement` · Nursing data needs well-defined data models with data elements having defined meaning + format; adoptions require national (ideally global) governance strategies · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P397** · `fact` · Degree of interoperability depends on combination of information interchange schema + computing foundations → flow-on impact on trustworthiness of data used by AI · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P398** · `rule` · Health professionals remain responsible for actions taken irrespective of advice from any AI system · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P399** · `fact` · FDA (US) / TGA (Australia) endorsement of clinical AI applications improves trustworthiness; greater accuracy tolerance acceptable when data used only for administrative purposes · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
- **P400** · `fact` · Legacy systems = outdated hardware/software/formats still critically supporting operations; continuing use creates technology debt requiring future reworking · [src:S01](../../INBOX/hi/ia med.md)
