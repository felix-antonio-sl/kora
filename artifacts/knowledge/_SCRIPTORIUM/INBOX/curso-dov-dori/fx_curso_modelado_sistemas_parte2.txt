Description
The first step in modeling a system in OPM
is to describe it so all the stakeholders can understand what the system is for and what it does,
even if they lack domain expertise.
This will help us communicate the system's function, structure and behavior
to managers, customers, suppliers and experts from other fields.
What we have just seen can be described as a system for picking, transporting, and serving fruit.
In OPM, the overall description of the system is expressed in the first OPD we create,
called the System Diagram or SD for short.
System Diagram
SD aims to provide all the stakeholders with a top-level, bird's eye view of the system,
It therefore must be simple and clear, with minimal technical details.
SD has five components: purpose, function, enablers, environment, and problem occurrence.
The first component is the system's purpose.
Who are the beneficiaries (The people for whom the system was developed)?
What benefit does the system provide to them?
In our case, the purpose of the system is to provide people with fruit effortlessly.
The second SD component is the system's main function:
What is the main process and the object it transforms that together deliver the benefit?
Our system picks, transports, and delivers fruit.
The third component, enablers, includes the objects that enable the system's main process:
allowing the system to operate.
There are two kinds of enablers: agents and instruments.
Here, the instruments are the robots, the table, the table cloth, and the plate.
Agents are always people.
In our system, they operate the robots.
The fourth SD component is the system's environment:
what things - objects and processes - are not part of our system, but still affect its operation?
Here, we include the fruit trees and the robots transportation path.
We can also include processes, such as 'wind blowing' and 'rain falling',
which can also affect the system's operation.
The fifth and last SD component is the problem occurrence.
Any artificial system aims to solve some problem.
In our case, people are hungry and want to eat fresh fruit without having to leave their seat!
The Technion's President, who benefited from the system,
is an example of someone for whom
we would usually present only the System Diagram, SD, with its five components.
Your assignment is to present your CEO
with the SD of the robotics-based electric cars manufacturing system.
So let's order flight tickets to get to the robotics lab,
and take the opportunity to model the SD of the flight tickets ordering system.
Unlike the system we modeled before, which was physical,
this system is informatical,
as it focuses on ordering flight tickets, rather than actually flying.
The system's website allows us to easily and quickly find and order flight tickets.
The system's purpose is thus to shorten travelers time
and improve their convenience of finding flights and ordering tickets.
The function of the system is to change the status of a seat in a flight
from available to purchased.
The enablers include the agent of the main process - the Traveler,
and the instrument - the Website.
The environment includes Internet connection, which is also an instrument of the main process.
Finally, the problem this system solves is the difficulty of finding flights and ordering tickets.
Let's summarize: when modeling SD, we represent five system components:
Summary
purpose, function, enablers, environment and problem occurrence.
Using the online flight ticketing system,
we now have tickets for our flight,
and in the next video, we will meet at the airport.
The System Diagram, SD, defines the system's purpose, scope, and main function
in terms of its main object, main process, boundary, and stakeholders.
SD aims to provide all the stakeholders with a top-level, bird's eye view of the system,
focusing on its function and the benefit it delivers.
It therefore must be simple and clear, with minimal technical details.
A steakholder is an individual, a group of people
or an organization, that has an interest in
or might be affected by, a system.

### System purpose

Hello, dear engineer!
We are at the airport, on our way to the robotics lab.
You were tasked by your CEO with modeling the top-level part
of a model of a robotic system for manufacturing electric cars.
Until we get to the check-in counter, let's spend some time to understand the system's "purpuse"
-the first component of the System Diagram, or SD for short - the top-level view of our system model.
Let's look around! Many systems surround us, each with its own purpose.
Artificial systems aim to benefit people:
someone planned for whom the system will operate, and for what purpose.
When describing an existing system, we start with the purpose.
So let's begin modeling.
First, we identify the beneficiaries. In an Airplane Flying system, the beneficiaries are the passengers.
They benefit from the system by shortening the time it takes them to arrive at a distant location.
To model the system purpose in SD, we first identify the Beneficiary Group.
Here, we call them Passenger Group.
By OPM conventions, object names must be singular,
so for humans, we represent plurality by adding the word ''Group'',
and the group is single. Humans are physical,
so the Passenger Group is represented in OPM as a physical object.
Here comes HopCat to provide us with a definition.
A beneficiary is a stakeholder who extracts value and benefits from the system.
A beneficiary group is a group of two or more beneficiaries.
Thank you, HopCat!
Now, we need to identify the Beneficiary Attribute.
An OPM attribute is an informatical object, like this one.
States of an attribute are called "values".
The Beneficiary Attribute is an attribute of the Beneficiary Group
whose value changes, and this change benefits the Beneficiary Group.
In an Airplane Flying system, this attribute of Passenger Group is Travel Time,
and its possible values are long and short.
Shortening the Travel Time through the Flying process
is the benefit that the Passenger Group receives.
Beneficiary attribute is an attribute that describes the beneficiary in terms of how
she or he benefits from the system.
To express the fact that Travel Time is an attribute of Passenger Group,
let us introduce a new kind of structural relation - Exhibition-Characterization.
This is the relation between a thing and its attribute.
In our case, the object Passenger Group exhibits the attribute Travel Time.
Graphically, this relation is the link depicted by a line with a black-in-white triangle along it.
The tip of the triangle points to the exhibitor - the thing that exhibits the attribute,
while the base of the triangle is connected to the attribute.
Once we added this link to our model, a new OPL sentence was created:
"Passenger Group exhibits Travel Time."
Our time as engineers traveling to the robotics lab is precious, so we want to save as much time as possible.
The system's purpose is to save passengers travel time.
How shall we describe this in OPM?
We will specify that Airplane Flying changes the value of Travel Time from long to short.
First, we add long and short as two values of Travel Time.
The new OPL sentence we now got is:
"Travel Time of Passenger Group can be long or short."
This is indeed what we wanted to say, so we are doing OK!
What changes the value of the Travel Time from long to short? The Flying process!
To express this, we will connect the two values of Travel Time to the Flying process using an input-output link pair, or in-out link pair, for short.
The resulting OPL sentence reads:
“Airplane Flying changes Travel Time of Passenger Group from long to short.”
This sentence clearly expresses the system's purpose - exactly what we aimed to do!!
System purpose - A key benefit that an artificial system is expected to provide to its beneficiaries.
Let's look around the airport. We should be able to identify the purpose of every system we see:
shops, coffee houses, and the electronic flights information screen.
What about the people around us? Is there a system that produces them?
People are created by an incredible natural system: the womb.
Let's model this system in OPM and while doing this,
learn an important difference between artificial and natural systems.
Purpose relates to humans and their intentions and goals.
In science, we analyze observations to understand root causes of natural and social phenomena.
These were not designed by humans, so we cannot talk about purpose here.
In model-based system engineering, we treat such phenomena as systems.
We ascribe to a natural or social system an outcome rather than a purpose.
The outcome of a natural system will depend on the modeler's objective and focus of research.
Who is the beneficiary of the Fetus Developing system? The fetus.
And what is the Beneficiary Attribute of Fetus?
We can define Developmental Stage as this attribute, with the values embryo and baby.
But we can simplify our model by removing the Beneficiary Attribute and assigning
embryo and baby as states of Fetus itself. Since Fetus is an object, which is not an attribute,
embryo and baby are now states rather than values of Fetus.
The updated OPL sentence is:
"Fetus can be embryo or baby."
Let's now add the main process, Fetus Developing,
and connect it with the states of Fetus using an in-out link pair.
The OPL sentence reads:
“Fetus Developing changes Fetus from embryo to baby.”
We couldn't say this better!
More examples of natural systems we could model are:
Tree growing, the water cycle, and the cell division mechanism.
HopCat seems anxious to jump in. Let's see what it wants to say.
System outcome - A result or effect that a natural system can have on its “affectees”,
which may be beneficial or detrimental.
We're approaching the check-in counter,
where we will hand in our suitcase and receive the boarding pass.
Meanwhile, we have learned how to model the purpose of artificial systems and the outcome of natural ones.

### Main function of System

Hi!
We are waiting to board the airplane on our way to the robotics lab.
We have already learned how to model the system's purpose in SD.
The second SD component is the function of the system.
The purpose of the Airplane Flying system was to shorten the travel time
for passengers going to distant destinations.
What is the main function of this system?
It is Airplane Flying: moving the airplane quickly through air from its origin to its destination.
While the system's purpose concerns benefitting people,
the system's function concerns
the transformation of the system's main object by the system's main process.
This transformation provides the benefit.
Hence, the main function of the system delivers its purpose to its beneficiaries.
The function name can simply be the object name followed by the process name Airplane Flying.
An object undergoing transformation is a transformee, sometimes also called operand.
The system's function is comprised of a pair: a transformee and the process that transforms it.
The main process is Flying, and the main transformee is Airplane.
Pretty simple, right?
For extra clarity, we can combine the transformee as the first word of the main process name.
If we do this, the process name will be the same as the name of its function, Airplane Flying.
When modeling a system, it is often better to add to the Benefit-Providing Object
a Benefit-Providing Attribute - the attribute which the main process transforms.
The transformation changes the value of the Benefit-Providing Attribute from problematic to satisfactory,
thereby benefitting the Beneficiary Object.
In our example,
we add to Airplane the attribute Location with values origin and destination.
The Flying process changes the value of the Location attribute of Airplane from origin to destination.
Benefit-providing object
The main object of the system, which is affected by the main process
directly or indirectly through one or more of its attributes.
It must appear in the SD and may appear in other OPDs.
Thanks, HopCat!
Now it is our turn to hand our suitcase and receive the boarding passes.
Remember the last time when you couldn't find the suitcase at your destination airport?
This time, let's model the function of the system that transports your suitcase,
so it doesn't get lost again!
What is the main function of this system?
To transport baggage by changing its location from the origin airport to the destination airport.
Baggage Transporting is the function of the system,
with Transporting being the main process, and Baggage - the transformee.
The Transporting process is physical, as it includes loading and unloading suitcases,
but the system has also informatical parts for managing the location of each suitcase at any point in time.
A thing with both physical and informatical parts is considered physical,
so Transporting is marked as physical.
In the Airplane Flying system, the Benefit-providing Object and main transformee was Airplane.
Similarly, in the Baggage Transporting system,
Transporting changes the state of Baggage from the origin airport to the destination airport.
To model this, we will add to Baggage the attribute Airport Location,
remove the states from ''Baggage'', and instead create them as values of Airport Location.
Now, we can omit the word ''airport'' from each value, simplifying our OPD.
To specify that Airport Location is an attribute of Baggage,
we again use the exhibition - characterization link
to connect the exhibiting thing - Baggage - to its attribute, Airport Location.
The value of Airport Location changes from origin to destination.
To express this, we will connect origin to the main process,
and select the in-out link pair.
The OPL sentences expressing the function of the system are:
“Baggage exhibits Airport Location."
"Airport Location of Baggage can be origin or destination"
and,
"Baggage Transporting changes Airport Location of Baggage from origin to destination".
Benefit-providing attribute
An attribute of the benefit-providing object, which is affected by the main process.
It must appear in the SD and may appear in other OPDs.
Very good!
We now modeled the function of the Baggage Transporting system. So we can send the suitcase and proceed to the departure gate
towards our destination - the robotics lab.
One more thing: what should you do if the main process transforms more than one transformee?
The main process can indeed be connected to more than one transformee,
but the function will include just the Benefit-providing Object.
What will this object be? That depends on what we wish to focus on!
Let's look, for example, at an electric battery.
The battery undergoes transformation - a state transition from depleted to fully charged.
But, as we can see, the Battery Charging process is connected to another transformee: Heat.
Charging the battery yields heat, which increases the temperature.
Let's model this in OPCloud.
The main process here is Battery Charging.
We will add the Battery, which is of course physical, and the Benefit-Providing Attribute of Battery - Charge Level.
Being an attribute, Charge Level is an informatical object.
Battery Charging is the process that transforms The Charge Level of Battery from depleted to fully charged,
and in OPL:
“Battery exhibits Charge Level.”
“Charge Level of Battery can be depleted or fully charged.”
Let's connect the object Charge Level to the main process using an in-out link pair.
“Battery Charging changes Charge Level of Battery from depleted to fully charged."
As we have seen, charging the battery yields heat - Thermal Energy, so we add this object to our model.
Are there any other transformees we could connect to the main process?
Charging the battery consumes Electric Energy, so we add this object to our model as well.
The main process is now connected to three transformees: the attribute Charge Level,
Electrical Energy, which is consumed, and Thermal Energy, which is produced.
Only ''Charge Level'' is part of the main function of the system,
but the other two transformees are also important.
We must have electricity to charge the battery, and we must get rid of the extra heat!

### Process enablers

We are about to take off!
Let's recap what we have done so far:
When modeling the SD of any system in OPM,
we should consider five components.
We have already learned about two: Purpose and Function.
Until our airplane takes off, let's learn about the third component: Enablers.
Our pilots are waiting for confirmation from the air traffic control tower before taking off.
Let's watch a video showing an air traffic control system.
The crew at the air traffic control tower is responsible for controlling the traffic
of airplanes in the airport.
Airplanes can only take off with their approval, and only within specified routes.
Airplane landing is also coordinated and must be approved by an air traffic controller.
Here is a simple model of an Air Traffic Controlling System.
The system's purpose is to increase the safety of people within and around all the airplanes
at the airport and in the airspace they are responsible for.
The OPL sentence reads:
"Air Traffic Controlling changes Safety Level of Human Group from low to high
and Safety Level of Airplane Set from low to high."
Since we do not use the plural form in OPM, we use Airplane Set instead of Airplanes,
and Human Group instead of People.
We convert humans to singular and add the word Group,
while for anything other than humans we add the word Set.
The function of the system, which attains the purpose of increasing the safety level,
is to coordinate the flight routes of the airplanes.
In OPL:
"Air Traffic Controlling changes Flight Route Set of Airplane Set
from uncoordinated to coordinated."
In OPM, a human enabler of any process is a physical object called Agent.
Agents are goal-oriented and have natural intelligence.
Both Air Traffic Controller and a Pilot are agents of the Air Traffic Controlling process.
The relation between an Agent and the process it enables is expressed graphically by an Agent link.
This link has a solid circle at its enabled process end, and is therefore nicknamed “Black lollipop”.
But hold on a second!
Is pilot part of the Air Traffic control system?
On one hand, we need pilots to enable the main process,
but on the other hand, as systems engineers of the Air Traffic Control system,
we cannot influence the pilots, as they are beyond the scope of our system.
We will therefore designate Pilot as an environmental object, by changing its contour from solid to dashed.
HopCat is always around to give us some definitions:
An enabler is an object that is required for a process to occur.
An agent is an enabler who is a human or a group of humans.
Thanks, HopCat!
In addition to people, there are also non-human, usually inanimate objects that enable processes.
These are instruments.
The main instrument of Air Traffic Controlling is the Air Traffic Control Tower.
A major instrument in the SD of any artificial system model is the system itself.
As a default, the name of this instrument is the name of the system's function,
or its main process, if it includes the transformee, followed by the word System.
Thus, in our case, the instrument name would be:
"Air Traffic Controlling System".
Often, the system has a commonly accepted name: "Air Traffic Control Tower" in our case,
so we will use it instead.
To designate an object as an instrument of a process, we use the instrument link.
It is similar to the agent link, except that the circle at the process end is empty,
giving it the nickname “White lollipop”.
The OPL sentence reads:
"Air Traffic Controlling requires Air Traffic Control Tower."
Our airplane finally received approval to take off!
Excellent!
Let's get in line for boarding.
I'm glad we were able to learn about enablers:
Agents, who are always humans, and Instruments, which are inanimate objects.
Unlike transformees, enablers of a process are not transformed by the process they enable,
but like transformees, without them the process will not happen.
Information systems, which are not physical, also have enablers.
For example, "the MOOC"-Massive Open Online Course

"MOOC Learning changes Study Stage of MOOC of Student Group from registered to completed."
The agent of the MOOC Learning process is Learner Group.
In OPL:
"Learner Group handles MOOC Learning."
The instrument of the main process is the MOOC.
MOOC Learning requires MOOC.
MOOC is also the Benefit-Providing object in our system!

### System boundary and environment

We have landed at our destination,
and we are waiting for our suitcases before hitting the road to the robotics lab!
There, we hope to get ideas for

When describing an existing system, or designing a new one, it is important to understand
not just the elements of the system, but also its environment.
The system interacts with its environment, which, in turn, affects the operation of the system.
So as we are walking to the baggage claim area, let's talk about the system's environment.
Look at SD of the Airplane Flying system,
we can try to identify what environmental Things - Objects and Processes - affect its operation.
We see that the Takeoff and Landing processes require the approval of the Air Traffic Control Tower.
In our Air Traffic Controlling System model, the Air Traffic Control Tower is, of course, systemic:
it is the main object in the system we are modeling.
However, in the Airplane Flying system, the Air Traffic Control Tower is beyond our control.
We, as the systems engineers of the Airplane Flying System,
have no influence over the Air Traffic Control Tower.
But, we certainly must take it into consideration when modeling our Airplane Flying System.
Therefore, we shall designate it as an environmental object.
Most things in our model are systemic, not environmental.
Systemic things are represented by a solid contour, while environmental ones - by a dashed contour.
Affiliation is the attribute of a Thing whose values are systemic and environmental.
The system's boundary is an imaginary border in the system model
that separates things whose Affiliation is systemic from those with environmental Affiliation.
Here comes cute HopCat to help us nail down a couple of definitions.
Thing Affiliation:
An attribute of a thing that specifies
whether the thing is systemic - part of the system,
or environmental - part of the system's environment.
System Boundary:
An imaginary border in the system model that separates systemic things from environmental ones.
Well said, HopCat!
