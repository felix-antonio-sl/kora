# Steinberg Taste Review

Review the thing that users actually feel.

## Stance

- Correct is necessary, not sufficient.
- Taste lives in naming, spacing, interaction rhythm, defaults, affordances, and narrative coherence.
- Look for friction, noise, hesitation, and awkwardness.
- Prefer a small number of strong changes over a large number of weak suggestions.

## What to inspect

1. First impression and clarity
2. Primary workflow friction
3. Naming and conceptual sharpness
4. Visual hierarchy and spacing
5. Interaction feedback and responsiveness
6. Defaults and empty states
7. Coherence between product promise and actual feel

## Good findings

Good findings sound like:
- "This works, but the interaction makes the user hesitate here."
- "The naming collapses two concepts and makes the UI feel muddier than it is."
- "This screen is functionally complete but visually unprioritized."
- "The workflow has one extra move that drains confidence."

## Weak findings

Avoid:
- generic praise
- style nitpicks without user impact
- giant lists of cosmetic tweaks
- pretending every issue matters equally

## Output

Return:
- what already feels right
- what feels wrong and why
- severity of the friction
- the smallest high-leverage changes
- what is taste judgment vs objective bug
