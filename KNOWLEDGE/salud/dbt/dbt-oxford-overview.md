## Foreword

Author: Marsha M. Linehan (University of Washington), DBT developer.

Core messages:

- DBT developed to treat chronic suicidality in borderline personality disorder (BPD) — providing an alternative to unbearable suffering and a path toward "a life worth living."
- Since initial trials, researchers and clinicians across US, Europe, and beyond have extended DBT to widening client groups, settings, and clinical problems.
- Handbook synthesizes 25 years of development in DBT theory, clinical application, and implementation in routine practice.
- Swales identified as first UK-trained DBT practitioner (Seattle Intensive Training, ~1994); subsequently led systematic dissemination throughout UK and Ireland via British Isles DBT Training (biDBT).

## Preface

Author: Michaela A. Swales.

### Key Milestones

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1992 | Swales qualifies as clinical psychologist; begins clinical work in North Wales inpatient adolescent unit |
| ~1993 | Receives draft DBT manuals from Mark Williams (who obtained them from Linehan during Cambridge sabbatical) |
| 1994 | Attends first Seattle Intensive Training with Barry Kiehn — becomes first UK-trained DBT team |
| 1994 | European cohort includes Martin Bohus (Germany), Weis Van Den Bosch (Netherlands), Karolinska Institute team (Sweden) — catalyzing European dissemination |
| 1997 | Independent UK DBT training begins; initial collaboration with University of Leeds Psychological Therapies Research Centre |
| 2001 | Partnership with commercial entity; biDBT receives UK Department of Trade and Industry designation for pre-eminence |
| 2002 | Swales assumes biDBT directorship from Heidi Heard |
| ~2009-2012 | Knowledge Transfer Programme (KTP): modelling dissemination, sustainability; publications on recursive nature of DBT and organizational pre-treatment |

### Dissemination Insights

- Tension between clinical training quality and commercial scaling produced dialectical syntheses that accelerated diffusion.
- UK dissemination rate compared favourably with US and German models (Swales, Taylor, & Hibbs, 2010).
- Confounding factors: increased evidence-based treatment focus, NICE guidelines for BPD, inpatient cost concerns.
- Implementation challenges persist even for experts — programme closures from insufficient personnel and organizational commitment are recurrent.

### Editorial Rationale

- Handbook balances scholarly/academic chapters with clinical-practice chapters and a client-perspective chapter (Louise Brinton Clarke).
- Brinton Clarke's chapter highlights completing DBT in a system that did not provide PTSD treatment — connecting to Harned & Schmidt and Bohus & Priebe chapters.
