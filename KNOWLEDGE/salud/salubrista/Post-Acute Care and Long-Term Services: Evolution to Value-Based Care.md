## Post-Acute Care and Long-Term Services: Evolution to Value-Based Care


### Introduction

- [P0001] **HECHO** — LTC historically meant nursing homes; PAC did not exist except patient going home after lengthy hospital stay
- [P0002] **HECHO** — As hospital LOS shortened, need for transitional post-acute services grew
- [P0003] **HECHO** — Nursing homes dedicated some beds for post-acute services
- [P0004] **HECHO** — Quadruple Aim guides providers: reduce total cost of care, improve patient experience, improve population health outcomes, reduce caregiver burden
- [P0005] **HECHO** — Hospitals paid based on DRGs receive set payment regardless of LOS → incentivized to decrease LOS
- [P0006] **HECHO** — 73% of variation in total Medicare spending occurs in PAC; single-greatest driver of spending variation
- [P0007] **HECHO** — Per MedPAC March 2019 Report to Congress, PAC direct spending = 8.3% of Medicare FFS spending
- [P0008] **HECHO** — 1 of every 4 dollars spent by Medicare Advantage (MA) plan goes to PAC
- [P0009] **HECHO** — PAC payment shifting away from fee-for-service toward value-based care

### Financing of PAC and LTSS (Table 1)

- [P0010] **DEFINICIÓN** — PAC financing = Medicare Part A
- [P0011] **DEFINICIÓN** — LTSS financing = Medicaid + Private Pay
- [P0012] **DEFINICIÓN** — Nursing Home/SNF PAC setting = Subacute/SNF-short-term (ST)
- [P0013] **DEFINICIÓN** — Nursing Home/SNF LTSS setting = SNF-long-term (LT)
- [P0014] **DEFINICIÓN** — PAC alternative facilities: LTACH, IRF, homecare
- [P0015] **DEFINICIÓN** — LTSS alternative facilities: Assisted living facility (ALC), Continuing care retirement community (CCRC), homecare
- [P0016] **DEFINICIÓN** — PAC systems: SNF direct admissions, Hospital preferred SNF network, Home first promotion
- [P0017] **DEFINICIÓN** — LTSS systems: PACE, Special Needs Plans-Institutional (SNP-I)

### Long-Term Services and Supports

- [P0018] **DEFINICIÓN** — SNF eligibility criterion applies to admission to SNF, ALC (via Medicaid waiver), and PACE
- [P0019] **DEFINICIÓN** — Custodial care = care primarily for personal (not medical) needs, not specific therapy for illness/injury, not skilled care
- [P0020] **DEFINICIÓN** — Custodial care includes assistance with ADLs: walking, getting in/out of bed, bathing, dressing, feeding, toilet use, special diet preparation, medication supervision
- [P0021] **DEFINICIÓN** — Custodial care = maintenance care by health aides when individual reached maximum physical/mental function
- [P0022] **REGLA** — Determining custodial care considers level of care and medical supervision required/furnished, not diagnosis, condition type, functional limitation degree, or rehab potential

### Post-acute Facility-Based Care

- [P0023] **HECHO** — Medicare Part A covers PAC in 4 settings: Home Health, Subacute SNF, IRF, LTACH
- [P0024] **HECHO** — Cost increases with each step from Home → SNF → IRF → LTACH
- [P0025] **HECHO** — MedPAC proposed site-neutral payments: program should not pay more in one setting than another if care can safely/effectively be provided in lower-cost setting

### Comparison of PAC Venues (Table 2)

- [P0026] **HECHO** — Home health: part-time intermittent skilled nursing, therapy services, limited home health aide; recertified every 30 days; median cost $24/hr
- [P0027] **HECHO** — Subacute SNF: short-term rehab typically following 3-day inpatient stay; 1-1.5 hr daily therapy; avg LOS 27 days; avg cost/patient $11,000
- [P0028] **HECHO** — IRF: intensive rehab requiring ≥3 hr therapy ≥5 days/week; avg LOS 13 days; avg cost/patient $17,000; typical conditions: stroke, neurological disorder
- [P0029] **HECHO** — LTACH: continued hospital-level care; avg LOS 26 days; avg cost/patient $38,500; typical conditions: complex medical, complex wounds, ventilator weaning
- [P0030] **HECHO** — Bundle-based care programs (e.g., major joint replacement) reimburse based on 90-day costs, incentivizing discharge directly home

### Home Health Services

- [P0031] **HECHO** — Medicare Part A and/or Part B cover eligible home health services
- [P0032] **DEFINICIÓN** — Covered home health services: part-time/"intermittent" skilled nursing, PT, OT, speech-language pathology, medical social services, part-time home health aide, injectable osteoporosis drugs for women
- [P0033] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Medicare does not pay for: 24-hr/day home care, meals delivered to home, homemaker services when only care needed
- [P0034] **REQUISITO** — Patient must be under care of doctor with plan of care created/reviewed regularly by doctor
- [P0035] **REQUISITO** — Doctor must certify patient needs ≥1 of: intermittent skilled nursing (not just blood draws), PT, speech-language pathology, or continued OT
- [P0036] **REQUISITO** — Therapy services covered only when specific, safe, effective; amount/frequency/time must be reasonable; must be complex or require qualified therapist
- [P0037] **REGLA** — Therapy eligibility requires: (1) condition expected to improve in reasonable time, OR (2) skilled therapist needed for maintenance program, OR (3) skilled therapist needed for maintenance therapy
- [P0038] **REQUISITO** — Home health agency must be Medicare-certified
- [P0039] **REQUISITO** — Beneficiary must be homebound, certified by physician
- [P0040] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Patients not eligible for home health benefit if they need more than part-time/"intermittent" skilled nursing
- [P0041] **PERMISO** — Patients may leave home for medical treatment or short/infrequent nonmedical absences (e.g., religious services)
- [P0042] **PERMISO** — Patients can receive home health care while attending adult day care

### Inpatient Rehabilitation Facilities (IRF)

- [P0043] **DEFINICIÓN** — IRFs provide higher intensity of services than SNF, within hospital setting or freestanding facilities
- [P0044] **DEFINICIÓN** — Acute inpatient rehab = restoration of disabled person to self-sufficiency or maximal possible functional independence
- [P0045] **REQUISITO** — IRF qualification requires interdisciplinary coordinated team approach with minimum 3 daily hours of rehab services
- [P0046] **REQUISITO** — IRF continuation requires evidence of progress toward stated goals, documented by objective functional measurements
- [P0047] **REGLA** — CMS "60 Percent Rule": designated % of IRF admissions must fall within specific diagnostic categories to maintain accreditation
- [P0048] **REGLA** — IRF postoperative rehab may be medically necessary for >1 major joint replacement during single hospitalization; typically not for single joint replacement
- [P0049] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Single joint replacement → IRF typically not considered medically necessary unless serious comorbidity causing functional deficits
- [P0050] **HECHO** — CMS implemented Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement Model: bundled payment for complete episode of care for hip/knee replacement
- [P0051] **HECHO** — Joint Replacement Model holds hospitals, physicians, PAC providers financially accountable from surgery through recovery

### Long-Term Acute Care Hospital (LTACH)

- [P0052] **REQUISITO** — LTACH admissions require documentation of complicated recovery course requiring prolonged hospitalization
- [P0053] **REQUISITO** — LTACH criteria: ≥2 medically active conditions requiring: ≥3 interventions incl. IV meds, continuous IV fluids (not "keep vein open"), TPN/PPN + blood products, ≥1 physician visit/day, frequent diagnostic services, active therapy participation ≥5 days/week
- [P0054] **HECHO** — Two other major LTACH categories beyond complex medical: complex wound/burns and mechanical ventilation weaning

### Subacute/SNF-ST

- [P0055] **DEFINICIÓN** — SNF-ST = distinct health care service for transitioning patient home after qualifying acute-care hospitalization
- [P0056] **REGLA** — Qualifying hospitalization historically required minimum 3-day inpatient stay; MA and managed care plans increasingly granting waivers
- [P0057] **HECHO** — Subacute rehab patients typically need <3 treatment modalities, most often PT
- [P0058] **HECHO** — Subacute qualification beyond PT: observation, assessment, monitoring of complicated/unstable condition
- [P0059] **HECHO** — Additional subacute qualifications: complex teaching requiring 24-hr SNF vs intermittent home health, complex medication regimen, tube feeding initiation, active ventilator weaning, wound care (incl. decubitus/pressure ulcers)
- [P0060] **HECHO** — Medicare Part A covers up to 100 days subacute services per benefit period
- [P0061] **HECHO** — Medicare covers 100% of first 20 days; days 21-100 require $185.50/day coinsurance from beneficiary
- [P0062] **HECHO** — Average subacute stay = 21 days
- [P0063] **REQUISITO** — Daily documentation of patient progress/complications required to maintain subacute care coverage

### Direct Subacute Admissions

- [P0064] **HECHO** — Most hospital stays cost >$2000/day; SNF costs typically <25% of that
- [P0065] **HECHO** — "Home First" strategy: previously inpatient procedures (e.g., joint replacement) performed in outpatient settings → patient goes directly home
- [P0066] **REGLA** — CMS 3-Day Rule (1965): Medicare patients required medically necessary 3-consecutive-day inpatient hospital stay before SNF admission
- [P0067] **REGLA** — 3-day count excludes discharge day, pre-admission ER time, outpatient observation time
- [P0068] **PERMISO** — Medicare Advantage programs and risk-bearing ACOs can receive 3-Day Rule waiver
- [P0069] **HECHO** — 3-Day Rule remains in place for traditional FFS Medicare beneficiaries
- [P0070] **HECHO** — Temporary 3-Day Rule waiver issued 2020 for COVID-19 pandemic; expected reversed 2021
- [P0071] **REQUISITO** — COVID-19 temporary waiver conditions: (1) beneficiary not residing in nursing home/SNF for LT custodial care, (2) meets all other CMS criteria for SNF admission
- [P0072] **REQUISITO** — SNF direct admission requires: medically stable, confirmed diagnoses, no need for inpatient hospital evaluation/treatment, identified skilled nursing/rehab need not providable outpatient or via home health
- [P0073] **REQUISITO** — SNF must have/maintain ≥3 Stars in CMS 5-Star Quality Rating System to participate in SNF 3-Day Rule Waiver
- [P0074] **HECHO** — ≥1/3 hospitalized patients experience some ill effect not related to pathologic process; 9% have major untoward event
- [P0075] **HECHO** — RAbbIT program (Rapid Assessment + Initial Treatment) developed at Forest and Chestnut Hill Healthcare Center, Newark/Passaic NJ, for direct SNF admissions from ED
- [P0076] **REQUISITO** — SNF direct admissions require PCP to assess and establish treatment plan; can use virtual provider access or dedicated APN at facility

### Subacute/SNF-ST Reimbursement

- [P0077] **HECHO** — CMS final rule CMS-1696-F (July 31, 2018) outlined FY2019 Medicare payment updates for SNFs
- [P0078] **HECHO** — Three significant changes: (1) SNF PPS case-mix classification, (2) Quality Reporting Program (QRP), (3) Value-Based Purchasing Program (VBP)
- [P0079] **HECHO** — PDPM effective October 1, 2019; focuses on patient condition/care needs rather than amount of care provided
- [P0080] **DEFINICIÓN** — PDPM = case-mix reimbursement model paying SNFs based on ICD-10 diagnosis codes, patient characteristics, clinically relevant factors → `PDPM`
- [P0081] **HECHO** — PDPM replaced RUG-IV (Resource Utilization Group, Version IV) case-mix model
- [P0082] **RESTRICCIÓN** — PDPM places 25% limit on group/concurrent therapy; 75% must be individual therapy
- [P0083] **HECHO** — FY2019 aggregate impact estimated +$820 million in Medicare payments to SNFs (2.4% market basket update per Bipartisan Budget Act 2018)
- [P0084] **HECHO** — $820M increase = <$50,000 per SNF
- [P0085] **REGLA** — Under SNF QRP, SNFs failing to submit required quality data to CMS face 2% reduction in funding
- [P0086] **HECHO** — SNF VBP began October 1, 2018; applies positive/negative incentive payments based on readmissions measure
- [P0087] **HECHO** — SNF VBP uses single claims-based all-cause 30-day hospital readmissions measure
- [P0088] **HECHO** — SNF QRP measures satisfy IMPACT Act of 2014 requirements
- [P0089] **HECHO** — PDPM adjusts payments for non-therapy ancillary services (NTAS): drugs, medical supplies
- [P0090] **TENSIÓN** — PDPM shifts SNF patient mix from therapy-focused (stroke, joint replacement) → medically complex (COPD, CHF)

### Preferred SNF Network

- [P0091] **HECHO** — Health systems developing Preferred SNF Networks: higher quality scores, greater efficiency (lower 30-day readmission), better collaboration
- [P0092] **HECHO** — Study in Health Affairs: hospitals with formal SNF networks saw 4.5% relative reduction in readmission rates (2009-2013) vs hospitals without
- [P0093] **REQUISITO** — SNF must maintain ≥3 Stars in CMS 5-Star to participate in 3-Day Rule Waiver and provide direct SNF admissions → `CMS 5-Star`

### SNF Measures of Success (Table 3)

- [P0094] **REQUISITO** — CMS Star Rating must be >3 (overall + quality measures)
- [P0095] **HECHO** — HCQIS (QIO) data tracks: readmission rate during SNF stay, readmission rate after SNF stay, overall SNF readmission rate
- [P0096] **HECHO** — CMS Star Rating measures: % short-stay residents rehospitalized, % with outpatient ED visit, % successfully discharged to community
- [P0097] **HECHO** — SNF total cost of care measures: average SNF stay cost, average SNF total cost of care
- [P0098] **HECHO** — INTERACT tool used to identify opportunities to prevent avoidable ED/hospital utilization
- [P0099] **HECHO** — Health systems strongly recommend all unplanned ED/hospitalizations undergo analysis to prevent future occurrences

### Special Needs Plans

- [P0100] **DEFINICIÓN** — SNPs created by Congress 2003; Medicare coordinated care plans (CCPs) for targeted care to special needs individuals; responsible for total cost of care
- [P0101] **HECHO** — Three SNP types: D-SNP (dual eligible, 2,157,682 enrollees), C-SNP (chronic condition, 345,951 enrollees), I-SNP (institutional, 71,474 enrollees)
- [P0102] **DEFINICIÓN** — I-SNP restricts enrollment to MA-eligible individuals who for ≥90 days have had/are expected to need LTC/SNF-level services
- [P0103] **PERMISO** — I-SNP may operate single or multiple facilities; CMS may allow county-based service area if I-SNP includes ≥1 LTC facility accessible to county residents
- [P0104] **REQUISITO** — I-SNP community enrollment requires: (1) institutional LOC determination using state assessment tool (same tool as for institution residents), (2) LOC assessment administered by independent impartial party (not owned/controlled by I-SNP)
- [P0105] **HECHO** — Successful SNPs should focus on: wellness, caregivers, coordination, prescription/medication management, end-of-life care, social determinants of health, mental health

### Learnings from COVID-19

- [P0106] **HECHO** — First US COVID-19 case in LTC identified in King County, Washington, almost 1 month before pandemic declared
- [P0107] **HECHO** — As of June 2021: 4% of COVID-19 infections but 31% of deaths (≥184,000) reported in US nursing homes
- [P0108] **HECHO** — At one point 43% of COVID-19 deaths attributed to LTC facilities
- [P0109] **REQUISITO** — Nursing facilities required (as part of pandemic response) to designate ≥1 Infection Preventionist(s) (IPs) responsible for facility's IPCP

### The Interdisciplinary Team (Table 4)

- [P0110] **DEFINICIÓN** — CNA: works under nurse supervision, assists with daily living; requires HS diploma/GED + 6-12 week certificate program
- [P0111] **DEFINICIÓN** — LPN: provides personal-level patient care, reports to physicians/RNs; must pass NCLEX-PN after state-approved program
- [P0112] **HECHO** — LPN scope varies by state: some (not all) states allow LPNs to administer prescribed medications or start IV fluids
- [P0113] **DEFINICIÓN** — RN: primary point of contact between patient and healthcare; 3 educational paths: bachelor's, associate, diploma; must pass national licensing exam
- [P0114] **DEFINICIÓN** — RNAC: assists DON ensuring documentation meets federal/state/certification guidelines; coordinates RAI process (MDS, RAPS, Interdisciplinary Care Plan)
- [P0115] **DEFINICIÓN** — DON: oversees standards of nursing practices; participates in development of patient care programs, policies, procedures
- [P0116] **DEFINICIÓN** — Social worker: assists people coping with everyday issues; most states require 2 years (3000 hrs) supervised clinical experience for clinical licensure
- [P0117] **DEFINICIÓN** — Dietitian: plans food/nutrition programs, prevents/treats illnesses via dietary modifications; requires ≥ bachelor's degree
- [P0118] **DEFINICIÓN** — PT: helps recover/improve function; requires master's or doctoral degree
- [P0119] **DEFINICIÓN** — OT: helps improve ability to perform tasks in living/working environments; requires master's degree or higher
- [P0120] **DEFINICIÓN** — Recreational therapist: devises programs in art, music, dance, sports, games, crafts; requires bachelor's degree + training
- [P0121] **DEFINICIÓN** — Attending PCP: responsible for initial patient care, supports discharges/transfers, periodic on-site visits; requires 4 yrs medical school + 1-3 yr primary residency; some complete geriatric fellowship
- [P0122] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Nursing facilities required to have medical director per OBRA 87
- [P0123] **HECHO** — Maryland is the only state requiring Medical Directors to be Certified Medical Director (CMD) in LTC
- [P0124] **HECHO** — CMD established by American Medical Directors Association
- [P0125] **DEFINICIÓN** — NP: advanced practice nurse; diagnoses/treats wide range of health problems; entry-level = graduate degree (master's or doctoral); bachelor's (4 yrs) + graduate NP (2-4 yrs)
- [P0126] **DEFINICIÓN** — Consultant pharmacist: reviews/manages medication regimens especially in institutional settings; Pharm.D. is only professional pharmacy degree; typically 6 yrs post-secondary education
- [P0127] **DEFINICIÓN** — Nursing home administrator: managing officer responsible for planning/organizing/directing/controlling facility day-to-day; requires ~120 hr certificate program + bachelor's degree + preceptor training

## Home Health Care


### Introduction

- [P0128] **HECHO** — By 2050, US population age ≥65 projected to increase from 46M to 90M (fastest growing segment)
- [P0129] **HECHO** — Age ≥65 group = 16% of population but accounts for ~36% of all healthcare expenditures
- [P0130] **HECHO** — "Hospital at home" programs reduce complications while cutting cost of care by ≥30%
- [P0131] **HECHO** — Elderly often require ER evaluation for acute illness, unintended injuries, exacerbation of chronic conditions (CHF, COPD, diabetes)
- [P0132] **HECHO** — These patients experience shorter hospital LOS

### Home Care

- [P0133] **DEFINICIÓN** — Home care = provision of health care-related services and DME to patients at home for restoring/maintaining maximal function, independence, comfort, health
- [P0134] **HECHO** — Home care requires clinician-coordinated interdisciplinary approach with therapeutic, diagnostic, social support services
- [P0135] **HECHO** — Home care eligible for patients with intermittent skilled needs due to: chronic condition exacerbation, acute illness/injury, or transition from hospital/rehab/SNF
- [P0136] **HECHO** — Indications for home care include: acute care visit when patient unable to travel, ongoing chronic condition management, environmental/social assessment (recurrent falls, abuse suspicion), recurrent ER/hospital admissions, palliative/end-of-life care

### The Physician House Call

- [P0137] **HECHO** — Patients not required to be homebound for practitioner to provide house call and receive reimbursement
- [P0138] **REQUISITO** — Homebound requirements apply only for services by home health agency
- [P0139] **HECHO** — Veterans Hospital Administration has had HBPC program for >30 years
- [P0140] **HECHO** — HBPC shown to potentially reduce overall cost of care for patients with serious illness

### Preparing for the House Call

- [P0141] **REQUISITO** — Home visit requires advance notice to patient, family/caregivers, home health/hospice agency, community-based caseworker
- [P0142] **HECHO** — During home visit: address medical condition, review all medications (prescribed, OTC, herbal), assess functional status/memory/cognitive ability/independence (BADLs + IADLs)
- [P0143] **HECHO** — Assess need for adaptive/DME for safety/independence; identify home adaptation/remodeling needs; perform caregiver assessment; recognize caregiver burnout

### Billing for Services

- [P0144] **HECHO** — Any licensed physician, NP, or PA can perform house call and bill using appropriate CPT code
- [P0145] **REGLA** — Practitioners not allowed to bill for travel time associated with house calls
- [P0146] **HECHO** — Medicare Part B 2021 house call reimbursement: new patients 99341 ($51.98) to 99344 ($172.85); established patients 99347 ($52.24) to 99350 ($170.25)
- [P0147] **HECHO** — Reimbursement varies state to state

### Agency Home Health Care

- [P0148] **REQUISITO** — Medicare beneficiaries must have documented need for episodic care to be eligible for home health
- [P0149] **DEFINICIÓN** — Home health care team: skilled nurse, PT, speech therapist, OT, home health aide, social worker, case manager, wound care nurse, medical director (some agencies)
- [P0150] **HECHO** — Referral often made when physician/APP notes patient's decline placing patient at risk for falls, hospitalization, or institutionalization
- [P0151] **HECHO** — Home health can assist in palliative care for seriously ill patients ineligible for hospice or who chose not to enroll

### Skilled Nursing

- [P0152] **HECHO** — LPN or RN can provide skilled-level home care: patient/caregiver education, medication instruction, lab specimens, home X-ray/infusion/IV therapy coordination, wound care, vaccinations
- [P0153] **REQUISITO** — Nurse admission assessment must include functional, memory/cognitive status, medication reconciliation (prescribed + OTC)
- [P0154] **HECHO** — Most home health agencies use software to screen potential drug-drug interactions; report sent to physician
- [P0155] **HECHO** — Nurses usually provide services 1-2 times/week; can be more frequent at start of care (front-loaded visits)

### Physical Therapist

- [P0156] **HECHO** — Home health PTs improve lower extremity strength/conditioning; common referrals: stroke, lower extremity orthopedic surgery, fall history, post-hospitalization deconditioning
- [P0157] **HECHO** — PTs recommend DME (canes, walkers, shower chairs) and bathroom retrofitting

### Speech Therapist

- [P0158] **PERMISO** — Under Medicare home health guidelines, physician can request home health referral solely for speech therapy
- [P0159] **HECHO** — Speech therapist can perform initial swallowing screen → may necessitate formal swallowing study for dysphagia assessment

### Occupational Therapist

- [P0160] **HECHO** — Home health OTs work with PTs to promote upper extremity function and basic ADL self-care
- [P0161] **HECHO** — Some OTs have acquired skills in assessing swallowing

### Social Worker

- [P0162] **HECHO** — Medical social workers help identify caregivers (paid/informal), address caregiver stress, alleviate financial hardship

### Home Care Aide

- [P0163] **HECHO** — Home health agencies provide nurse aides for ADLs (bathing, dressing) and light housework
- [P0164] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Aide services contingent upon need for skilled nursing for patient

### Requirements for Agency Home Health Care

- [P0165] **REQUISITO** — As of April 2020, physician, APN, CNS, or PA must determine patient meets "homebound" status and has need for episodic skilled nursing, PT, or speech therapy
- [P0166] **DEFINICIÓN** — Homebound Criterion 1: patient must need supportive devices (crutches, canes, wheelchairs, walkers), special transportation, or assistance of another person to leave residence; OR leaving home is medically contraindicated
- [P0167] **DEFINICIÓN** — Homebound Criterion 2: (1) normal inability to leave home AND (2) leaving home requires considerable and taxing effort
- [P0168] **REQUISITO** — Initial home health certification requires ACA-mandated face-to-face encounter addressing medical condition for which home health ordered
- [P0169] **PLAZO** — Face-to-face encounter must occur within 90 days prior to start of home health OR within 30 days after start of care
- [P0170] **REGLA** — Face-to-face encounter required only for initial certification, not recertification
- [P0171] **PERMISO** — Face-to-face can be done via telemedicine
- [P0172] **PERMISO** — Face-to-face can be performed by resident physician in training under supervision of teaching physician
- [P0173] **REQUISITO** — Face-to-face documentation must include: certifying physician/provider name, date, patient homebound status, need for skilled services, signature + date
- [P0174] **HECHO** — Home health improves care transitions through medication reconciliation, patient/caregiver education, ensuring timely practitioner follow-up

### Physician Reimbursement for Home Health Care Services

- [P0175] **HECHO** — Physicians/APPs who order home health from Medicare agency eligible for payment for reviewing plan of care
- [P0176] **HECHO** — Initial certification form = Form 485; physician reviews, agrees with plan of care, signs and dates
- [P0177] **HECHO** — Billing codes: G0180 (initial certification), G0179 (recertification), G0181 (Care Plan Oversight)
- [P0178] **PLAZO** — Each certification period = 60 days
- [P0179] **REQUISITO** — Face-to-face necessity documentation only required for initial certification
- [P0180] **REQUISITO** — Care Plan Oversight (CPO) G0181: physician/APP must spend minimum 30 min in 30-day period providing oversight/directing services
- [P0181] **REQUISITO** — CPO time must be documented throughout 30-day period
- [P0182] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Surgeons not allowed to bill for CPO; already bundled into postoperative care fee
- [P0183] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Cannot concurrently bill Transitional Care Management or Chronic Care Coordination during CPO 30-day period (unless physician decides not to bill CPO)
- [P0184] **HECHO** — Resident doctors (in-training) cannot bill for initial certification, recertification, or CPO
- [P0185] **PERMISO** — Non-physician practitioners (NP/PA) can bill for initial certification, recertification, and CPO

### Summary: The Future of Home Health Care

- [P0186] **HECHO** — CMS exploring innovative/alternative healthcare delivery models to contain future costs
- [P0187] **HECHO** — Distance monitoring of vital signs (e.g., heart failure patients) used to identify exacerbations early, prevent hospitalization

## Assisted Living and Residential Care


### Introduction

- [P0188] **HECHO** — AL integral component of LTC for persons not requiring/choosing nursing home, in less institutionalized setting
- [P0189] **HECHO** — AL usually less expensive than LT nursing home care; cost varies within/between states
- [P0190] **HECHO** — AL aspires to optimize resident autonomy, privacy, dignity
- [P0191] **HECHO** — Due to aging in place desire + shorter hospital stays + more acute care in AL/RC, many AL residents eventually experience functional dependence similar to nursing home residents of 20-30 years ago

### History

- [P0192] **HECHO** — AL industry experienced rapid growth over past 15-20 years
- [P0193] **HECHO** — Core AL values: resident autonomy, choice, privacy, dignity, home-like environment
- [P0194] **HECHO** — AL availability increased due to: growing older adult population, need for supportive care, states' limits on certified nursing home beds
- [P0195] **HECHO** — AL ranges from small "mom and pop" homes (as few as 4 beds) to large communities (several hundred residents); many for-profit
- [P0196] **TENSIÓN** — Controversy whether AL is social or medical model of care; many stakeholders emphasize social model, oppose requirements for increased medical oversight/regulation
- [P0197] **HECHO** — National Assisted Living Workgroup established 2003 at request of US Senate Special Committee on Aging; submitted 110 recommendations
- [P0198] **HECHO** — CEAL (Center for Excellence in Assisted Living) established as informational clearinghouse promoting research, best practices, policy review
- [P0199] **DEFINICIÓN** — Person-centered care (CEAL) = comprehensive ongoing process of transforming entity's culture/operation into nurturing, empowering one promoting purpose/meaning/well-being in relationship-based home environment
- [P0200] **HECHO** — CEAL developed PC-PAL Toolkit (Person-Centered Practices in AL) using resident/staff questionnaires

### Definition

- [P0201] **DEFINICIÓN** — AL = state-regulated/monitored residential LTC option providing/coordinating oversight and services to meet residents' individualized scheduled/unscheduled needs based on assessments/service plans

### Required Services

- [P0202] **OBLIGACIÓN** — AL state-required services must include: 24-hr awake staff, personal/supportive services (ADL assistance), health-related services (e.g., medication management), social services, recreational activities, meals, housekeeping/laundry, transportation
- [P0203] **HECHO** — 65% of ALCs serve ≤25 residents; 31% have 26-100 beds; 4% have >100 beds
- [P0204] **HECHO** — >50% of residents dwell in ALCs of ≥26 beds
- [P0205] **HECHO** — 81% for-profit, 18% nonprofit, 1% government/other
- [P0206] **HECHO** — In 2020, largest AL chains: Brookdale Senior Living, Sunrise Senior Living, Five Star Senior Living, Enlivant, Atria Senior Living

### Community Characteristics

- [P0207] **HECHO** — No federal regulations for AL; each state develops own regulations
- [P0208] **TENSIÓN** — Some advocates propose potential benefit of federal oversight for uniform standards; industry resists

### Staffing

- [P0209] **HECHO** — No requirement for nurse on-site in AL, though >50% have RN or LPN on staff
- [P0210] **HECHO** — 50% of ALCs provide social work services; activities/recreational staff more frequent in larger communities
- [P0211] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Conditions usually prohibiting AL admission: ventilator dependency, stage IV pressure injuries, need for IV fluids, communicable airborne infections requiring isolation
- [P0212] **PERMISO** — ALCs may admit residents needing moderate physical assistance (e.g., wheelchair transfers)
- [P0213] **HECHO** — 40 states require special license/certification for dementia care in ALCs
- [P0214] **HECHO** — In 2016, 14% of ALCs had dementia care unit; 9% served only dementia residents

### Resident Characteristics

- [P0215] **HECHO** — In 2016, 29% of AL residents needed transfer assistance
- [P0216] **HECHO** — AL residents generally need assistance with ≥2 ADLs; median LOS = 22 months
- [P0217] **HECHO** — 42% of AL residents have Alzheimer/other age-related dementia; dementia prevalence 24-47%; cognitive impairment rates up to 90%
- [P0218] **HECHO** — AL residents typically non-Hispanic white, female, age ≥85
- [P0219] **HECHO** — Hypertension and dementia most prevalent conditions; other common: heart disease, depression, arthritis, osteoporosis
- [P0220] **HECHO** — AL residents take avg 12-14 medications; 78% require medication assistance
- [P0221] **HECHO** — >70% of AL residents move in from own home/apartment; 60% relocate within 10 miles of previous residence
- [P0222] **HECHO** — 60-90% of residents satisfied with care received
- [P0223] **HECHO** — ~60% of residents eventually transition to LT nursing facility after ~22 months stay

### Residents' Rights

- [P0224] **PERMISO** — AL residents eligible to receive home health services on intermittent basis for skilled nursing needs
- [P0225] **PERMISO** — AL residents entitled to receive hospice services if ALC can accommodate within community policies
- [P0226] **OBLIGACIÓN** — ALC must disclose all fees/services to resident/family before signing admission contract
- [P0227] **OBLIGACIÓN** — ALC must inform residents/families of transfer/discharge policies including appeals process
- [P0228] **HECHO** — Resident rights include: consideration/respect, dignity, autonomy, freedom from restraints, freedom from abuse/neglect, access to LTC ombudsman, manage own financial affairs, guide service plans, choose physicians/pharmacists, execute advance directives, form resident councils
- [P0229] **OBLIGACIÓN** — ALC responsibilities: maintain abuse-free environment, report resident welfare info to state agencies, establish house rules with resident council, obtain notification of third-party services

### Financing

- [P0230] **HECHO** — AL median annual cost private room = $51,600 vs $105,850 private nursing home room
- [P0231] **HECHO** — Annual AL costs lowest in Missouri ($36,000), highest in Delaware ($80,280)
- [P0232] **EXCLUSIÓN** — No Medicare reimbursement to ALCs
- [P0233] **HECHO** — 44 states have Medicaid waiver program (under CMS) for personal/skilled care for qualified low-income AL residents
- [P0234] **HECHO** — In 2016, Medicaid covered some services for 17% of AL residents
- [P0235] **REQUISITO** — Medicaid waiver requires resident to meet both state nursing home eligibility criteria AND Medicaid financial eligibility
- [P0236] **HECHO** — As of 2020, 25 states administer Medicaid through managed care
- [P0237] **HECHO** — Only 6% of AL residents move out due to financial reasons
- [P0238] **HECHO** — LTC insurance is infrequent AL payment source; costs usually include monthly rent, meals, basic services

### Staff Training

- [P0239] **OBLIGACIÓN** — 24-hr staffing must be available at ALC
- [P0240] **HECHO** — Most states require dementia care training for direct care staff
- [P0241] **HECHO** — End-of-life training for staff associated with greater hospice utilization and more engagement in advance care planning
- [P0242] **HECHO** — Most common AL deficiencies: medication administration (48%), staffing/staff qualifications (41%), services insufficient to meet needs (36%)
- [P0243] **HECHO** — Florida 2020 report: two most common deficiencies = staffing requirements + lack of staff in-service training

### Disaster Preparedness and COVID-19

- [P0244] **HECHO** — Most states require ALCs to have disaster plan, but not as prescriptive as nursing facility plans
- [P0245] **HECHO** — <10% of Florida ALCs had pandemic emergency plan separate from infection control plan
- [P0246] **HECHO** — LTC settings = <1% US population but 5% of COVID-19 cases
- [P0247] **HECHO** — As of March 2021 (Atlantic COVID-19 Tracker): 1 in 12 US COVID deaths occurred in LTC facility residents (incl. nursing homes + ALCs)
- [P0248] **HECHO** — Among 143 ALCs affected by hurricanes 2004-2005: 75% sheltered in place, 23% evacuated during ≥1 hurricane
- [P0249] **HECHO** — Most federal COVID-19 guidance related to nursing facilities, not considering unique ALC situation

### Medical Care

- [P0250] **HECHO** — AMDA (Society for Post-Acute and LTC Medicine) convened consensus conference identifying 4 AL concerns: medication management, clinician role, communication/care coordination, clinical direction
- [P0251] **REGLA** — Five "Rs" of geriatric prescribing: Right medication, Right time, Right dose, Right route, Right patient
- [P0252] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Unlike SNFs, no federal requirement for consultant pharmacist monthly medication review in ALCs
- [P0253] **HECHO** — Consultant pharmacists provide medication review/monitoring at 84% of ALCs
- [P0254] **HECHO** — Beers Criteria = useful guide for potentially inappropriate medications in older adults → `Beers Criteria`

### Medication Management

- [P0255] **HECHO** — Sufficiently trained medication aides in ALCs do not commit more errors than LPNs
- [P0256] **HECHO** — Unlike nursing homes, medical providers may not be notified when AL resident refuses medication

### Communication and Care Coordination

- [P0263] **HECHO** — Many clinicians unaware of differences in care/services between ALCs and nursing homes → problematic for transitions

### Care Coordination and Technology

### Clinical Direction (Medical Director or Not)

- [P0269] **HECHO** — No federal mandate requiring ALC to have Medical Director/Physician Advisor
- [P0270] **HECHO** — AL Workgroup did not reach majority consensus to recommend ALCs have Medical Director; agreed on "external professional consultant"
- [P0271] **HECHO** — Clinician confidence in AL staff increases in smaller ALCs, with increased nursing staff presence, and if facility has Medical Director position

### Clinical Direction and Hospice

- [P0272] **HECHO** — In 2016, hospice available in 81% of nursing homes and 68% of ALCs
- [P0273] **HECHO** — Medicare hospice benefit: 4 levels of care = (1) routine home care, (2) general inpatient care, (3) inpatient respite care, (4) continuous home care
- [P0274] **HECHO** — 60% of Medicare beneficiaries receiving hospice in ALCs had ill-defined condition, dementia, or Alzheimer's as terminal diagnosis
- [P0275] **HECHO** — Median hospice LOS per beneficiary: ALCs 98 days, nursing homes 50 days, SNF 30 days, home 45 days
- [P0276] **HECHO** — OIG 2015 report: hospices financially incentivized to target ALCs (greatest financial gain)
- [P0277] **HECHO** — Medicare no longer accepts nonspecific diagnoses (adult failure to thrive, senility without psychosis, unspecified debility) for hospice

### Practitioner Billing E/M Codes

- [P0278] **REGLA** — As of January 1, 2023, AL visits use home visit E/M codes: new patients 99341/99342/99344/99345; established 99347/99348/99349/99350
- [P0279] **HECHO** — AL uses Place of Service Code 13
- [P0280] **HECHO** — AL reimbursement generally higher than equivalent codes for office and nursing home

### Clinical Suggestions for Practitioners

- [P0281] **HECHO** — Nursing home residents required to be seen minimum every 60 days; recommendation for AL = on-site visits at least quarterly
- [P0282] **HECHO** — Use of consultant pharmacist for monthly/quarterly medication reviews in AL encouraged

### The Future of Assisted Living

- [P0283] **HECHO** — CEAL 2014 roundtable identified 3 main drivers of AL change: increased medical acuity, healthcare transitions to non-institutional settings via ACOs, increased baby boomer expectations
- [P0284] **HECHO** — CEAL identified 14 major themes; key areas: personnel, data, state regulations, affordability
- [P0285] **HECHO** — AHCA/NCAL Quality Initiative for AL: 4 core areas = safely reducing hospital readmissions, reducing off-label antipsychotic use, increasing staff stability, increasing customer satisfaction
- [P0286] **HECHO** — INTERACT adapted for AL (Assisted Living Version 1.0) for early identification/management/documentation of acute condition changes to prevent avoidable hospitalizations; available at interact2.net

- [P0288] **HECHO** — QAPI mandated in nursing homes; recommended for ALCs via physician leadership

### Advance Directives

- [P0289] **HECHO** — Many ALC residents have no advance healthcare directive or one not applicable in emergency
- [P0290] **HECHO** — POLST (Physician Orders for Life Sustaining Treatment) recommended; format differs state to state

### Medical Director Roles (Table 8)

## PACE


### Introduction

- [P0293] **DEFINICIÓN** — PACE = unique healthcare delivery system integrating full spectrum of services for frail persons ≥55 years old
- [P0294] **HECHO** — PACE organizations deliver primary, specialist, acute, long-term, home-based, and palliative care to enrollees
- [P0295] **HECHO** — Average PACE enrollee: 77 years old, avg 8 acute/chronic conditions, 3 ADL limitations
- [P0296] **HECHO** — >95% of PACE participants live in community
- [P0297] **HECHO** — PACE member population frailer than AL residents (who require ≥2 ADL assistance, avg 3 chronic conditions)

### PACE History

- [P0298] **HECHO** — PACE originated 1971, San Francisco Chinatown, $2000 federal grant to Chinatown-North Beach Health Care Planning and Development Corp (later On Lok Senior Health Services)
- [P0299] **HECHO** — Marie-Louise Ansak (Swiss social worker) developed LTC model for elderly Chinese population considering nursing home placement culturally unacceptable
- [P0300] **HECHO** — Based on British Day Hospital model offering therapeutic/minor medical services with patients returning home same day
- [P0301] **HECHO** — "On Lok" = Cantonese for "peaceful abode"
- [P0302] **HECHO** — By 1980s On Lok received Medicare/Medicaid waivers to pilot financing system: full medical services for fixed monthly per-enrollee payment
- [P0303] **HECHO** — 1986: federal legislation allowed other PACE sites
- [P0304] **HECHO** — 1997: PACE received permanent Medicare/Medicaid provider status under Balanced Budget Act
- [P0305] **HECHO** — Alexian Brothers Community Services (St. Louis) = first permanently recognized PACE site
- [P0306] **HECHO** — 1999 Federal Interim Regulation published: 30 programs in 19 states
- [P0307] **HECHO** — 2005 Deficit Reduction Act initiated PACE rural expansion with $7.5M funding
- [P0308] **HECHO** — Currently >137 PACE programs in 31 states with >53,000 enrollees
- [P0309] **HECHO** — NPA (National PACE Association) founded 1994 to support PACE programs

### Financing

- [P0310] **DEFINICIÓN** — PACE financing = capitated Medicare + Medicaid dollars per enrollee; once enrolled, PACE = sole source of Medicare/Medicaid-covered services incl. medications
- [P0311] **HECHO** — Most PACE participants dually eligible (Medicare + Medicaid); can pay privately if not
- [P0312] **HECHO** — Medicare rates calculated using risk-adjusted payment methodology similar to Medicare Advantage
- [P0313] **HECHO** — Medicaid rates negotiated between each PACE organization and state Medicaid agency
- [P0314] **HECHO** — PACE organizations assume all financial risk for Medicare Parts A, B, D and Medicaid services
- [P0315] **HECHO** — PACE = Medicare Advantage Programs funded under Medicare Part C
- [P0316] **HECHO** — PACE is both payor and provider → allows payment flexibility/creativity/innovation
- [P0317] **HECHO** — In 2015, HHS Secretary authorized for-profit entities to operate PACE (contingent on demonstrating comparable access, quality, cost-effectiveness to nonprofit PACE)

### Outcomes

- [P0318] **HECHO** — PACE shown to improve functional status, quality of life, and decrease mortality
- [P0319] **HECHO** — Very old (80-90 yrs), living alone, using ambulation aids, cognitively impaired, needing IADL assistance benefit most from PACE
- [P0320] **HECHO** — PACE hospitalization, readmission, and potentially avoidable hospitalization (PAH) rates lower than comparable Medicaid nursing home residents
- [P0321] **HECHO** — Variation in PACE outcomes across sites reflects heterogeneity in case mix, program longevity, IDT experience, transitional housing, local hospital/SNF contracts, local medical culture

### A Day at the PACE Day Health Center

- [P0322] **DEFINICIÓN** — PACE IDT members: PCP (physician/PA/NP per state law), RN, master's-level social worker, PT, OT, recreational therapist/activities coordinator, dietician, PACE center manager, home care coordinator, personal care attendant, driver
- [P0323] **OBLIGACIÓN** — IDT responsible for initial assessment, periodic reassessment, plan of care, coordination of 24-hr care delivery per 42 CFR:460.104
- [P0324] **HECHO** — PACE participants attend DHC avg 3 times/week
- [P0325] **HECHO** — DHC serves as main medical center and social services base
- [P0326] **HECHO** — Frontline staff, HHAs, drivers = "eyes and ears" of PACE, often identifying condition changes ahead of clinic staff
- [P0327] **HECHO** — Typical PCP panel ≤100 patients
- [P0328] **HECHO** — PACE in-house specialists may include podiatry, optometry, dentistry, behavioral health (varies by center)
- [P0329] **HECHO** — PACE schedules/provides transportation for all IDT-approved specialist appointments
- [P0330] **HECHO** — PACE admits participants to contracted nursing homes for respite; during hospital/SNF stays PACE clinical staff continue to follow participant
- [P0331] **HECHO** — PACE nurse and provider on call 24/7

### COVID-19 Impact on PACE

- [P0332] **HECHO** — Post-COVID, PACE service model likely to remain more home-based and less reliant on day center (North Carolina study)

### PACE Model Practice

- [P0333] **HECHO** — NPA Primary Care Committee began developing Model Practices in 2006 for key chronic conditions
- [P0334] **HECHO** — Model practices direct care along 3 pathways per participant directives: achieving longevity, maintaining function, receiving palliative care
- [P0335] **HECHO** — Current Model Practices: Atrial Fibrillation (2018), Diabetes Mellitus (rev 2014), Dementia (2014), CAD (2020), CHF (rev 2018), CKD (rev 2016), COPD (2013), Preventive Care Guideline (2010)
- [P0336] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Non-NPA members may not use/reproduce/modify model practices without written consent of NPA

### Role of the Medical Director in PACE

- [P0337] **OBLIGACIÓN** — 42 CFR:460.60: PACE organization must employ medical director responsible for participant care delivery, clinical outcomes, and QA/performance program implementation/oversight
- [P0338] **HECHO** — PACE Medical Director operates as both director of clinical services and administrator of health plan
- [P0339] **HECHO** — Typical PACE program = ~300 participants; many Medical Directors also function as PCP
- [P0340] **HECHO** — New PACE PCP usually requires ~6 months to become proficient in IDT role
- [P0341] **HECHO** — PACE sites also use NPs and PAs as PCPs/IDT members
- [P0342] **HECHO** — Medical Director must recruit/contract provider network: specialists, hospitals, SNFs, pharmacy, ancillary services (home care, DME suppliers)

### PACE in the Nursing Home

- [P0343] **HECHO** — ~10% of PACE participants require skilled or custodial nursing care despite community-living goal
- [P0344] **REQUISITO** — CMS requires comprehensive medical record at PACE site even if participant resides in nursing facility
- [P0345] **HECHO** — PACE and nursing facility teams must collaborate to integrate documentation/care plans

### QAPI in PACE

- [P0346] **HECHO** — PACE QAPI includes: utilization of services, caregiver/participant satisfaction, safety, clinical outcome measures, wound care, infections, falls, staff competency, participant grievances

### The Future of PACE

- [P0347] **TENSIÓN** — Operational challenges limit PACE growth: substantial human/financial resources needed to run small health plan
- [P0348] **HECHO** — Significant back-office overhead for Part A/B claims, Part D data, federal/state reporting → less dollars for patient care
- [P0349] **TENSIÓN** — Expanding PACE limited by federal regulation not keeping up with modern innovations
- [P0350] **HECHO** — NPA working with Congress for more operational flexibility, growth, innovation
- [P0351] **HECHO** — Issues to explore: expansion of service areas, revision of age and NF eligibility requirement, utilization of alternative care settings, unbundling of services

## Department of Veterans Affairs Options for Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P0352] **HECHO** — VA developed LTC options ranging from traditional institutional care → innovative community-based long-term/post-acute care programs
- [P0353] **HECHO** — VA focused on cost-effective care using community-based services aimed at reducing hospitalizations and preventing institutionalization (parallel to CMS Triple Aim)
- [P0354] **HECHO** — VA services include Hospital in Home, Home-Based Primary Care, Clinical Video Telehealth (CVT), Telehealth, Homemaker Home Health Aide Care
- [P0355] **PERMISO** — End-of-life care including hospice may be available to Veterans based on clinical need
- [P0356] **REQUISITO** — Veterans may receive nursing facility (NF) care based on military service-connected status, level of disability, and income requirements
- [P0357] **DEFINICIÓN** — VA inpatient facility = Community Living Center (CLC); alternative = VA contracted nursing facility

### Community-Based Care

- [P0358] **HECHO** — Hospital in Home operational in ~8 VA Medical Centers with ongoing expansion
- [P0359] **HECHO** — Hospital in Home provides hospital-level services at home including parenteral infusions
- [P0360] **HECHO** — Veterans receive daily visits from medical practitioner/clinician + skilled nursing + PT/nurse aides as needed
- [P0361] **HECHO** — Veterans can be directly transferred home from Emergency Department avoiding hospitalization under Hospital in Home
- [P0362] **HECHO** — Most Hospital in Home programs staffed solely by VA employees
- [P0363] **HECHO** — "Partnership" model: VA provides medical care + DME (e.g., oxygen); partner home health agency + infusion pharmacy provide skilled nursing + infusion services
- [P0364] **HECHO** — Small study demonstrated reduction in cost of care and NF 30-day admission rate post hospital discharge → `Cai et al. 2018`
- [P0365] **HECHO** — HBPC Veterans receive avg 2-3 contacts/month from team + primary care visit every 4-6 weeks
- [P0366] **REGLA** — If Veteran lives outside HBPC coverage area (often designated by driving time) or HBPC cannot provide needed care → VA may contract with community-based agency
- [P0367] **PERMISO** — Veteran may receive additional skilled services through Medicare if Medicare beneficiary
- [P0368] **HECHO** — >10% overall reduction shown in Veterans dually eligible for VA and Medicare benefits → `Edes et al. 2014`
- [P0369] **HECHO** — HBPC enrollment associated with decreased hospital bed days, decreased NF bed days, decreased hospital readmission rates → `Leff et al. 2015`
- [P0370] **DEFINICIÓN** — Home-Based Primary Care (HBPC) = comprehensive program serving veterans with chronic, disabling conditions and complex psychosocial needs
- [P0371] **ALCANCE** — HBPC goal: maximize Veteran independence at home, reduce preventable ER visits, hospitalizations, LTC facility admissions
- [P0372] **HECHO** — VA interdisciplinary team (IDT) includes physician, nurse, social worker, rehab therapist, dietitian, psychologist, pharmacist
- [P0373] **HECHO** — IDT may also include NP, PA, chaplain, respiratory therapist, recreational therapist
- [P0374] **HECHO** — HBPC provides primary care in homes of Veterans for whom clinic-based care has been ineffective
- [P0375] **HECHO** — HBPC team may also coordinate care, provide palliative care, rehabilitation services, disease management
- [P0376] **HECHO** — Fiscal 2019: >909,000 Veterans received portion of VA care through telehealth
- [P0377] **HECHO** — >60% of VA primary/mental health care practitioners provided ≥1 CVT appointment to Veteran's home (fiscal 2019)
- [P0378] **HECHO** — Telehealth technologies collect/send Veteran health data (vital signs) to VA care team for remote management
- [P0379] **HECHO** — Nurse usually serves as telehealth point of contact
- [P0380] **HECHO** — VA provides veteran training on home telehealth system use
- [P0381] **HECHO** — Telehealth study showed reduction in hospital LOS, reduction in cost of care, increase in patient satisfaction → `Messina 2016`
- [P0382] **DEFINICIÓN** — Community Living Centers (CLCs) = nursing facilities owned and operated by VA; structure/services comparable to community NFs
- [P0383] **REQUISITO** — CLC eligibility based on service-connected status, level of disability, and income
- [P0384] **DEFINICIÓN** — Homemaker Home Health Aide program provides homemakers/home health aide services to Veterans needing assistance with BADLs and/or IADLs
- [P0385] **PERMISO** — VA allows homemaker/home health aide services concurrently with other community-based services to avoid/delay NF admission

### Nursing Facility Care

- [P0386] **HECHO** — VA contracts with community nursing facilities to provide care to eligible Veterans
- [P0387] **REQUISITO** — VA funding for NF care based on service-connected status, level of disability, and income
- [P0388] **REGLA** — If Veteran does not meet VA NF funding requirements → private pay or Medicaid enrollment if indigent

### End-of-Life Care

- [P0389] **OBLIGACIÓN** — VA policy requires interdisciplinary Palliative Care consult team at each VA facility
- [P0390] **HECHO** — Hospice and Palliative Care services = part of Veterans Health Administration Standard Medical Benefits Package
- [P0391] **PERMISO** — All enrolled Veterans eligible for hospice/palliative services if they meet clinical need
- [P0392] **PERMISO** — Veteran has choice between VA benefit or Medicare benefit to fund hospice services
- [P0393] **PERMISO** — No VA policy prevents Veterans from receiving hospice services in addition to other palliative care (e.g., whole brain irradiation, radiation for painful/bleeding metastatic disease, palliative chemotherapy/immunotherapy) if VA not funding duplicate care
- [P0394] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Dual model of hospice + palliative care subject to acceptance by community-based hospice

## The Role of Practitioners and the Medical Director


### Introduction

- [P0395] **HECHO** — Nursing facilities now admit medically complex patients discharged from acute care and specialty hospitals, beyond traditional custodial care
- [P0396] **HECHO** — Both LTC residents and post-acute patients can be medically complex with multi-morbidities and risk factors for geriatric syndromes (pressure injuries, anorexia, falls)
- [P0397] **DEFINICIÓN** — Persons living in nursing facilities = "residents"; those admitted for short-term medical/skilled care = "patients"
- [P0398] **HECHO** — LTC residents often need acute care in addition to ongoing chronic medical care
- [P0399] **HECHO** — Short-stay patients often have chronic conditions + need for acute and post-acute medical care; some may have extended stay or transition to LTC bed
- [P0400] **HECHO** — Attending physicians primarily responsible for managing individual patient medical care + facilitating IDT collaboration
- [P0401] **REGLA** — Medical director roles/functions are separate from attending physician roles, even when same person serves both
- [P0402] **DEFINICIÓN** — Attending physician provides direct resident care; medical director oversees, coordinates, helps improve facility's overall medical care/services

### The Role of the Medical Practitioner

- [P0403] **OBLIGACIÓN** — OBRA '87 required every NF resident/patient to have attending physician to supervise and coordinate medical care → `OBRA 1987`
- [P0404] **DEFINICIÓN** — "Supervising the care" = participating in assessment/management, monitoring changes in medical status, providing consultation/treatment when needed
- [P0405] **HECHO** — Supervising care includes performing regulatory visits and possibly supervising NPs or PAs
- [P0406] **PERMISO** — NPs currently permitted to serve as "attending practitioner" for hospice patients
- [P0407] **HECHO** — AMDA developed curriculum + certification program for medical directors and curriculum for attending physicians covering diverse competency areas
- [P0408] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Physician must approve resident admission (e.g., giving/approving orders upon admission) → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0409] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Physician must be familiar with and contribute to patient assessment/care planning → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0410] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Physician must take active role in supervising patients' care: aware of impact of others' medical actions, ensure coordination → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0411] **OBLIGACIÓN** — At each visit: review total program of care including medications/treatments by other disciplines → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0412] **OBLIGACIÓN** — At each visit: write, sign, date progress note → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0413] **OBLIGACIÓN** — At each visit: sign and date all orders (except immunization orders that may be periodic without new order) → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0414] **OBLIGACIÓN** — At each visit: evaluate resident condition + continued appropriateness of current medical regimen → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0415] **REGLA** — Visit frequency: ≥1× every 30 days for first 90 days after admission; ≥1× every 60 days thereafter → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0416] **REGLA** — Next scheduled regulatory visit date determined by admission date, not actual date of last visit → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0417] **REGLA** — Visit is timely if occurs ≤10 days after required date → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0418] **PERMISO** — Daily visits allowed but must be medically necessary → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0419] **REGLA** — After initial visit, required visits may alternate between attending physician visits and PA/NP/CNS visits under physician supervision → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0420] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Physician must respond appropriately and timely when notified of acute change of condition → `Table 1 OBRA'87`
- [P0421] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Physician must ensure backup medical coverage (individual physician, physician group, or APN) if attending unavailable; virtual visits may be allowable → `Table 1 OBRA'87`

## Common Clinical Conditions in Post-Acute and Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P0568] **HECHO** — Chapter authored by Naushira Pandya, Dept of Geriatrics, Nova Southeastern University, Kiran C Patel College of Osteopathic Medicine
- [P0569] **HECHO** — Management of medical conditions in frail elderly in PALTC is challenging due to multimorbidity, progressive functional decline, psychosocial issues, reduced life expectancy

### Hypertension

- [P0571] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACC/AHA 2017: normal BP <120/<80 mmHg; HTN defined as BP >130/80 mmHg
- [P0572] **HECHO** — HTN is most prevalent and modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease and death
- [P0573] **HECHO** — NHANES prevalence of HTN in >65 yr: 63.6% women, 65.8% men
- [P0574] **HECHO** — HTN prevalence in >75 yr: 73.4% women, 81.2% men
- [P0575] **HECHO** — Age-adjusted HTN prevalence highest in non-Hispanic black men and women
- [P0576] **HECHO** — Up to 2/3 of nursing home patients have HTN
- [P0577] **HECHO** — HTN increases risk for atrial fibrillation, CHF, PAD, CKD, cognitive impairment
- [P0578] **HECHO** — Systolic HTN is more prevalent than diastolic HTN and more closely related to CV risk
- [P0579] **HECHO** — Physiologic aging changes contributing to elevated BP: increased arterial stiffness, increased sympathetic nervous system activity, increased peripheral resistance, reduced vascular compliance/vasodilation
- [P0580] **HECHO** — Changes in renin-angiotensin system and kidney function lead to increased salt sensitivity with aging
- [P0581] **DEFINICIÓN** — JNC-8: pre-HTN 120-139/80-89; Stage 1: 140-159/90-99; Stage 2: >160/>100
- [P0582] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACC/AHA 2017: elevated SBP 120-129; Stage 1: 130-139/80-89; Stage 2: >=140/>=90
- [P0583] **REGLA** — JNC-8 BP treatment threshold: >=60 yr >150/90; <60 yr with DM or CKD >140/90
- [P0584] **REGLA** — ACC/AHA 2017 BP treatment threshold: adults with CVD or ASCVD risk >10% treat >130/80; without CVD and risk <10% treat >140/90
- [P0585] **REGLA** — JNC-8 BP targets: >60 yr 150/90; <60 yr 140/90
- [P0586] **REGLA** — ACC/AHA 2017 BP target: <130/80 for all
- [P0587] **REGLA** — Non-Black adults first-line HTN therapy: ACEi/ARB, CCB, thiazides
- [P0588] **REGLA** — Black adults first-line HTN therapy: thiazides or CCB
- [P0589] **REGLA** — Adults with CKD first-line therapy: ACEi/ARB (JNC-8)
- [P0590] **HECHO** — Post-prandial hypotension occurs in 1/3 of PALTC patients; risk factor for falls, syncope, stroke, mortality
- [P0591] **HECHO** — In PALTC, BP measurements may vary with higher readings in morning before breakfast and drops after meals
- [P0592] **HECHO** — Cochrane Review (15 trials, 24,055 subjects >=60 yr): reduction in total mortality RR 0.90, total CV morbidity/mortality RR 0.72
- [P0593] **HECHO** — In very elderly >=80 yr: reduction in total CV mortality/morbidity RR 0.75, but NO reduction in total mortality RR 1.01
- [P0594] **HECHO** — HYVET trial: 3845 healthy community individuals >80 yr with sustained SBP >=160 mmHg randomized to indapamide/placebo + perindopril/placebo, SBP goal 150 mmHg
- [P0595] **HECHO** — HYVET results: 30% reduction in fatal/nonfatal stroke, 39% reduction in fatal stroke, 21% reduction in all-cause mortality; benefits apparent at 1 yr, increased at 2 yr
- [P0596] **EXCLUSIÓN** — HYVET excluded individuals with immobility, cognitive impairment, and nursing facility residents
- [P0597] **HECHO** — SPRINT: 9361 ambulatory subjects, mean age 68 yr, baseline SBP >130 mmHg; goal <120 vs <140 mmHg
- [P0598] **HECHO** — SPRINT results: CV events HR 0.77, all-cause mortality HR 0.73 in intensive group
- [P0599] **HECHO** — SPRINT-MIND: 19% reduction in MCI but not in probable dementia
- [P0600] **EXCLUSIÓN** — SPRINT excluded nursing facility residents, Type 2 DM, HF, dementia
- [P0601] **TENSIÓN** — PARTAGE study: 1127 NH subjects >80 yr in Europe; SBP <130 mmHg + >=2 BP-lowering meds associated with higher mortality risk (adjusted HR 1.78; 95% CI 1.34-2.37, p<0.001)
- [P0602] **HECHO** — Prior HTN trials (SHEP, STOP, Sys-Eur, Syst-China): 32-47% reduction in stroke, 13-30% reduction in coronary disease, 29-58% reduction in HF
- [P0603] **REGLA** — BP diagnosis requires average of 2 readings on >=2 occasions; appropriate cuff size; seated position at rest
- [P0604] **HECHO** — >90% of elderly with elevated BP have essential HTN; majority with new onset HTN have systolic HTN
- [P0605] **HECHO** — NSAIDs (including COX-2), erythropoietin, alcohol, corticosteroids, uncontrolled pain often implicated in elevating BP
- [P0606] **DEFINICIÓN** — Pulse pressure = SBP minus DBP; increases with age
- [P0607] **REGLA** — Evaluate HTN patients for DM, CKD, sleep apnea as contributing comorbidities
- [P0608] **REGLA** — Diastolic HTN or treatment-resistant HTN should be evaluated for renovascular cause
- [P0609] **REGLA** — Unexplained hypokalemia + metabolic alkalosis → evaluate for hyperaldosteronism
- [P0610] **REGLA** — ACC/AHA recommends SBP goal <130 mmHg for non-institutionalized mobile community-dwelling older adults >65 yr
- [P0611] **TENSIÓN** — For older adults >65 yr with high disease burden and limited life expectancy, HTN goals differ and need individualization based on functional/cognitive status, fall risk, patient preference
- [P0612] **REGLA** — Initiate HTN treatment with half usual dose to minimize side effects
- [P0613] **REGLA** — Prefer once-daily long-acting medication for HTN
- [P0614] **REGLA** — Lower BP gradually over weeks to months (not emergent) due to impaired baroreceptor responses and cerebral autoregulation in elderly
- [P0615] **REGLA** — Measure supine and standing BP before initiating systolic HTN treatment; 20% have orthostatic and/or postprandial hypotension
- [P0616] **HECHO** — Degree of BP reduction is major determinant of CV risk reduction, not choice of drug
- [P0617] **REGLA** — Stage 2 HTN usually requires 2 drugs (e.g., thiazide + ACEi)
- [P0618] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Combining ACEi and ARB increases risk of hypotension, syncope, hyperkalemia, renal dysfunction without improving CV outcomes
- [P0619] **HECHO** — Thiazide diuretics: reduce mortality, stroke, coronary events; disadvantages include hypokalemia, hyponatremia, gout
- [P0620] **HECHO** — ACEi: no CNS side effects, preserve renal function, reduce proteinuria; disadvantages include cough, hyperkalemia
- [P0621] **HECHO** — CCAs: effective in reducing stroke, no CNS/metabolic side effects; disadvantages include constipation, ankle edema
- [P0622] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Avoid short-acting CCAs for HTN treatment
- [P0623] **HECHO** — Beta blockers not recommended as monotherapy for HTN
- [P0624] **HECHO** — Alpha blockers: improve urinary symptoms in BPH; disadvantage is increased CHF hospitalization; not approved first-line for HTN

### Resistant Hypertension

- [P0625] **DEFINICIÓN** — Resistant HTN = poor control despite 3 complementary antihypertensives (including diuretic), or control requires >=4 medications
- [P0626] **HECHO** — Renal artery stenosis present in 25% of people with serum creatinine >2 mg/dL
- [P0627] **HECHO** — Renal artery stenosis can contribute to HF, cardiac ischemia, "flash" pulmonary edema (especially if bilateral)
- [P0628] **HECHO** — Two large studies in subjects >69 yr failed to show benefit of renal artery stenting on CV or renal outcomes
- [P0629] **REGLA** — Resistant HTN approach: exclude pseudoresistance, address lifestyle factors, discontinue contributing meds (sympathomimetics, NSAIDs, erythropoietin, corticosteroids), screen for secondary causes
- [P0630] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Screening for renal artery stenosis in PALTC with existing renal insufficiency is not recommended
- [P0631] **TENSIÓN** — Benefit of lowering BP in LTC population >85 yr, frail, or with multiple comorbidities is not clearly established; this population excluded from clinical studies

### Anemia

- [P0632] **HECHO** — Aging: decreased hematopoietic reserve, reduced absorption of essential nutrients, decline in GFR and EPO secretion, increased cytokine concentrations
- [P0633] **HECHO** — Aging alters myeloid-lymphoid precursor differentiation: decline in lymphopoiesis, increase in myeloid clones (which can mutate → myeloid neoplasms)
- [P0634] **HECHO** — Anemia is NOT considered normal part of aging
- [P0635] **HECHO** — Anemia may be independent risk factor for adverse outcomes when DM, CKD, or CVD is present
- [P0636] **HECHO** — Anemia associated with: reduced exercise tolerance, LVH, renal function decline, dizziness, weakness/sarcopenia, osteoporosis, frailty, falls, cognitive dysfunction, mortality
- [P0637] **DEFINICIÓN** — WHO defines anemia as Hb <12 g/dL in adult women, <13 g/dL in adult men
- [P0638] **RESTRICCIÓN** — WHO cutoffs based on population data excluding >65 yr; do not account for race/ethnicity
- [P0639] **HECHO** — NHANES III prevalence of anemia in US community-dwelling >65 yr: 11% men, 10.2% women
- [P0640] **HECHO** — Proposed new lower limits of normal Hb for older white men/women: 13.2/12.2 g/dL; older black men/women: 12.7/11.5 g/dL
- [P0641] **HECHO** — In nursing home studies (USA and Turkey), 56% of residents were anemic; risk higher in malnourished
- [P0642] **HECHO** — Signs/symptoms of anemia in LTC: anorexia, nausea, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, tachycardia, cold intolerance, decreased activity, depression, ecchymoses, gingival bleeding, falls, confusion, jaundice, pallor
- [P0643] **HECHO** — In community-dwelling >65 yr: ~1/3 anemias due to nutrient deficiencies, ~1/3 disease-related, ~1/3 unexplained
- [P0644] **DEFINICIÓN** — Iron deficiency: MCV decreased, serum ferritin <30 ng/mL and/or TSAT <20%
- [P0645] **DEFINICIÓN** — Possible iron deficiency: serum ferritin 30-100 ng/mL and/or TSAT <20%
- [P0646] **DEFINICIÓN** — EPO deficiency (CKD): eGFR <60, no abnormal cells on smear
- [P0647] **DEFINICIÓN** — B12 deficiency anemia: MCV increased, RDW increased, low B12, increased homocysteine and MMA
- [P0648] **DEFINICIÓN** — Myelodysplastic syndrome: MCV increased, normal RDW, B12 and folate normal; dysplastic neutrophils on smear
- [P0649] **HECHO** — Soluble transferrin receptor + iron studies + ferritin helps differentiate iron deficiency anemia from anemia of chronic inflammation
- [P0650] **HECHO** — Serum EPO increases with severity of anemia but response may be blunted
- [P0651] **REGLA** — Bone marrow biopsy indicated if morphological/lab evaluation not diagnostic and multiple cell lines abnormal (e.g., pancytopenia)

### Anemia of CKD

- [P0652] **HECHO** — Anemia of CKD is typically normochromic, normocytic, primarily due to EPO deficiency
- [P0653] **HECHO** — Hb decline especially noticeable as GFR trends below 60 mL/min/1.73m2
- [P0654] **HECHO** — Usually no deficiency of iron, B12, or folate when GFR <30 mL/min/1.73m2
- [P0655] **HECHO** — ~50% of NH residents have GFR <60 mL/min/1.73m2; in one study 60% of residents with Stage III CKD were anemic

### Acute or Chronic Immune Activation (ACI)

- [P0656] **HECHO** — ACI causes disturbance of iron homeostasis limiting iron availability for erythropoiesis via impaired release from macrophages
- [P0657] **HECHO** — ACI: ferritin may be increased while other iron studies are low; mediated by proinflammatory cytokines and hepcidin
- [P0658] **HECHO** — Hepcidin decreases duodenal absorption of iron
- [P0659] **REGLA** — Chronic disease patients with Hb <10 g/dL should be evaluated for additional causes: blood loss, malnutrition, hemolysis

### Treatment of Anemia

- [P0660] **REGLA** — Iron deficiency treatment: ferrous sulfate 325 mg daily (65 mg elemental iron) or ferrous gluconate 300 mg daily (36 mg elemental iron)
- [P0661] **REGLA** — Parenteral iron indicated in cancer, CKD, or intolerance of oral iron
- [P0662] **REGLA** — Vitamin C source needed if achlorhydria present with iron deficiency
- [P0663] **REGLA** — B12 deficiency treatment: 1000 mcg IM weekly x 1 month, then monthly if malabsorption or neurological complications; oral B12 1000 mcg daily alternative
- [P0664] **REGLA** — Folate deficiency: folate 1 mg orally daily; 5 mg daily if severe malabsorption
- [P0665] **REGLA** — Anemia of CKD: epoetin alfa or darbepoetin alfa SC; maintain Hb 10-11 g/dL; weekly Hb till stable then monthly; monitor BP
- [P0666] **REGLA** — Blood transfusion for acute blood loss with hypotension/CV compromise; for chronic anemia if Hb <7 g/dL, Hct <21%, or presence of angina/HF/dyspnea/tachycardia/hypotension

### Heart Failure

- [P0667] **HECHO** — HF affects 20% of nursing facility residents
- [P0668] **HECHO** — HTN is major risk factor for HF, especially HFpEF
- [P0669] **HECHO** — HFpEF present in 50% of NH residents diagnosed with HF
- [P0670] **HECHO** — HFpEF incidence increases significantly in older adults with DM, obesity, female gender, HTN, dyslipidemia, atrial arrhythmias
- [P0671] **HECHO** — SNF rehospitalization rates for HF: 27-43%
- [P0672] **HECHO** — 1-year mortality in LTC residents with HF >50%; hospitalization >50% annually
- [P0673] **HECHO** — HF intrinsic causes: MI, myocarditis, valvular heart disease, congenital heart disease, nonischemic dilated cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, restrictive cardiomyopathy (amyloidosis), pericarditis, cardiac tamponade
- [P0674] **HECHO** — HF extrinsic causes: systemic HTN, COPD, PE, anemia, thyrotoxicosis, drug toxicity, excess blood volume/polycythemia
- [P0675] **DEFINICIÓN** — HFpEF: LVEF >50%
- [P0676] **DEFINICIÓN** — HFmEF: LVEF 41-49%
- [P0677] **DEFINICIÓN** — HFrEF: LVEF <40%
- [P0678] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACC/AHA HF Stage A: at risk for HF without structural heart disease or symptoms
- [P0679] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACC/AHA HF Stage B: structural heart disease without signs/symptoms of HF
- [P0680] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACC/AHA HF Stage C: structural heart disease with prior or current HF symptoms
- [P0681] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACC/AHA HF Stage D: refractory HF requiring specialized interventions
- [P0682] **DEFINICIÓN** — NYHA Class I: no limitation of physical activity; ordinary activity does not cause HF symptoms
- [P0683] **DEFINICIÓN** — NYHA Class II: slight limitation; comfortable at rest; ordinary activity causes symptoms; can walk 2 blocks or climb 2 flights
- [P0684] **DEFINICIÓN** — NYHA Class III: marked limitation; comfortable at rest; less than ordinary activity causes symptoms
- [P0685] **DEFINICIÓN** — NYHA Class IV: unable to perform any physical activity (including ADLs) without HF symptoms; or symptoms at rest
- [P0686] **HECHO** — HF manifestations may be atypical in LTC: fatigue, malaise, lethargy, declining function, confusion, restlessness, sleep disturbance, orthopnea, GI symptoms
- [P0687] **HECHO** — Can be up to 8 lbs of fluid weight gain before peripheral edema develops
- [P0688] **HECHO** — HFpEF patients: more often female, S4, sustained PMI, absence of JVD, absence of peripheral edema, normal heart size on CXR, LVH on EKG
- [P0689] **HECHO** — HFrEF patients: more often male, S3, displaced PMI, JVD, pitting edema, Q waves on EKG
- [P0690] **REGLA** — Hypokalemia from diuretics may increase risk of fatal arrhythmias; many with hypokalemia also have hypomagnesemia → inadequate response to K+ supplementation
- [P0691] **HECHO** — Hyperkalemia associated with ACEi, ARBs, ARNI, worsening renal function
- [P0692] **HECHO** — Hyponatremia may indicate HF disease progression; associated with reduced survival
- [P0693] **HECHO** — For age >75 yr, NT-proBNP >1800 pg/mL consistent with HF
- [P0694] **REGLA** — BNP <100 pg/mL → HF unlikely; BNP >500 pg/mL → HF likely
- [P0695] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Serial BNP/NT-proBNP measurements not recommended
- [P0696] **HECHO** — NT-proBNP/BNP increase with age, in women, with renal impairment
- [P0697] **HECHO** — Urine microalbumin indicates risk for developing HF
- [P0698] **HECHO** — CXR: may show pulmonary congestion (may be absent in HF), effusion; serial CXR not recommended
- [P0699] **HECHO** — EKG evaluates LVH, ischemia, prior MI, atrial fibrillation; does not diagnose HF
- [P0700] **REGLA** — Echocardiogram with Doppler determines EF, ventricular size/function, pericardium, valvular disease; may be repeated in 3-6 months

### Common Precipitants of Heart Failure

- [P0701] **HECHO** — Cardiac precipitants of HF: MI/ischemia, poorly controlled HTN, excess dietary sodium, medication nonadherence, excess fluid intake
- [P0702] **HECHO** — Arrhythmia precipitants of HF: SVT (especially Afib with rapid rate), bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome
- [P0703] **HECHO** — Associated medical conditions precipitating HF: PE, hypoxia from chronic lung disease, infection (pneumonia, viral, sepsis), anemia, hyperthyroidism, CKD (eGFR <30 mL/min)
- [P0704] **HECHO** — Medications precipitating HF: alcohol, beta blockers (including ophthalmic), CCBs, NSAIDs, glucocorticosteroids, mineralocorticoids, antiarrhythmics

### Disease Management and Care Considerations in HF

- [P0705] **REGLA** — Close observation for symptoms/signs that may precede acute HF episode by several days
- [P0706] **REGLA** — Weigh HF patients 3x/week, before breakfast, on same scale
- [P0707] **REGLA** — Patients with both frailty and HF likely to have higher morbidity → optimize nutrition and rehabilitation
- [P0708] **REGLA** — Consult cardiologist or HF nurse specialist for patients responding poorly or with repeated exacerbations/hospitalizations

### Management of HF

- [P0709] **HECHO** — Mortality significantly increases with cardiac EF <30-35%
- [P0710] **REGLA** — HF nonpharmacologic: smoking cessation, moderate sodium restriction <3 g/day (unless hyponatremic), avoid excessive fluid intake (restriction not usually necessary)
- [P0711] **TENSIÓN** — Low sodium diets associated with worse outcomes in HF
- [P0712] **REGLA** — HF: evaluate for anemia and thyroid dysfunction
- [P0713] **HECHO** — ARNIs, SGLT2 inhibitors, and ivabradine shown to improve HF symptoms, reduce hospitalizations, prolong survival
- [P0714] **HECHO** — SGLT2 inhibitors beneficial in HF patients with and without diabetes
- [P0715] **REGLA** — After initial HFrEF diagnosis, medications should be adjusted every 2 weeks to achieve guideline-directed therapy
- [P0716] **REGLA** — HFrEF pharmacotherapy sequence: ARNI/ARB/ACEi (ARNI preferred) + beta blocker + diuretic if needed → add aldosterone antagonist → add SGLT2 inhibitor → titrate diuretic if fluid overload → add hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate for Black patients → add ivabradine if HR >70 on max tolerated beta blockers
- [P0717] **REGLA** — ACEi: indicated when ARNI not possible; contraindicated if K >5.5 mEq/L, SBP <80 mmHg, estimated CrCl <30 mL/min
- [P0718] **REGLA** — ACEi/ARB: check BP, renal function, K level weekly when starting and titrating to max tolerated dose
- [P0719] **REGLA** — Aldosterone antagonists (MRA): indicated NYHA III-IV with EF <35%; contraindicated if creatinine >2.5 mg/dL or K >5.0 mEq/L
- [P0720] **REGLA** — MRA: check renal function and K 2-3 days after initiation, at day 7, then monthly x 3 months
- [P0721] **HECHO** — Eplerenone beneficial in HF following MI
- [P0722] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Combining ACEi and ARB not beneficial in HF; increases adverse events
- [P0723] **REGLA** — Beta blockers: indicated for newly diagnosed HFrEF Stage C; contraindicated in severe decompensated HF, HR <45-50/min, active bronchospastic lung disease, significant heart block, SBP <90-100 mmHg
- [P0724] **HECHO** — Beta blocker benefit in HF only demonstrated with carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol
- [P0725] **HECHO** — Beta blockers: greatest impact when resting HR <70/min and patients are "dry"
- [P0726] **REGLA** — ARNI (valsartan/sacubitril): indicated for newly diagnosed HFrEF Stage C, NYHA II-III; contraindicated if hypotension or history of angioedema
- [P0727] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Avoid starting ARNI within 36 h of last ACEi dose
- [P0728] **REGLA** — Digoxin: optimal serum levels 0.5-0.9 ng/mL; lower dose 0.125 mg/day recommended; QOD dosing in renal impairment
- [P0729] **REGLA** — Digoxin: 50% dose reduction if on verapamil, amiodarone, or quinidine
- [P0730] **HECHO** — Digoxin reduces HF hospitalizations but not mortality; "therapeutic concentrations" may cause toxicity and mortality
- [P0731] **REGLA** — Diuretics: if furosemide dose >80 mg BID → consider different loop diuretic (e.g., bumetanide) or add thiazide (e.g., metolazone)
- [P0732] **REGLA** — Hydralazine + nitrates: for Black patients with persistent symptoms despite ACEi/ARBs + beta blockers; also for HF patients unable to take ACEi/ARBs
- [P0733] **REGLA** — Ivabradine: indicated NYHA II-III HFrEF in sinus rhythm with persistent HR >70/min on max tolerated beta blockers
- [P0734] **HECHO** — Ivabradine reduced hospitalizations for HF in NYHA II-III HFrEF; adverse effect: transient brightness in visual fields
- [P0735] **REGLA** — SGLT2 inhibitors: consider in HFrEF NYHA II-IV; improved hospitalization and CV mortality; also reduces decline in kidney function in HFrEF and CKD
- [P0736] **HECHO** — Dapagliflozin is current FDA-approved SGLT2 inhibitor for HFrEF
- [P0737] **HECHO** — SGLT2 inhibitor adverse effects: genital infections, Fournier gangrene, lower extremity complications, AKI (uncommon)
- [P0738] **REGLA** — Routine anticoagulation in HF patients without Afib is NOT recommended
- [P0739] **REGLA** — Long-term anticoagulation indicated in most HF patients with comorbid Afib; warfarin or DOAC may be used

### Rehospitalization for HF

- [P0740] **HECHO** — 20% of SNF/NF patients with HF readmitted to hospital within 30 days; up to 50% within 6 months
- [P0741] **HECHO** — Not all HF rehospitalizations due to HF; COPD, pneumonia, sepsis, other cardiac events, arrhythmias contribute
- [P0742] **HECHO** — % of short stay and long stay patients rehospitalized after NH admission are CMS quality measures
- [P0743] **HECHO** — Contributing factors for HF rehospitalization: severity of HF, comorbidities, suboptimal evidence-based therapy, inconsistent practitioner availability, NSAID use, sodium restriction nonadherence, medication reconciliation errors

### Refractory HF

- [P0744] **HECHO** — Older adults with HF discharged to SNF: 14.4% mortality within 30 days, 53.5% within 1 year
- [P0745] **REGLA** — Refer to advanced HF specialist if: required IV inotropes, >=2 ER visits/hospitalizations in past 12 months for HF, persistent fatigue/edema with rising BNP/NT-proBNP, creatinine >1.8 mg/dL or BUN >43 mg/dL, inability to tolerate beta blockers/ACEi/ARB/ARNI/MRA, Afib, ventricular arrhythmias, repeated ICD shocks
- [P0746] **REGLA** — Palliative care consultation appropriate before ICD or LVAD placement
- [P0747] **REGLA** — ICD should be inactivated upon hospice admission if consistent with goals of care
- [P0748] **HECHO** — HF 5-year mortality: 74% with normal LVEF, 92% with reduced LVEF

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

- [P0749] **DEFINICIÓN** — COPD: insidious progressive lung disease characterized by airflow obstruction not fully reversible
- [P0750] **HECHO** — COPD is 3rd leading cause of death in older adults
- [P0751] **HECHO** — 1 in 6 patients admitted to NH may have COPD or emphysema
- [P0752] **HECHO** — COPD responsible for 34% probability of death in 180 days post hospital discharge to nursing facilities
- [P0753] **HECHO** — Smoking accounts for 90% of COPD cases
- [P0754] **HECHO** — Other COPD risk factors: advanced age, repeated pulmonary infections, prior TB, biomass fuel exposure, air pollution, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
- [P0755] **HECHO** — 50% of lung function lost by time mild exertional dyspnea occurs; only 30% remains when dyspnea at rest
- [P0756] **HECHO** — COPD clinical indicators: dyspnea (progressive, worse with exertion), abnormal pulse oximetry, cough, chronic sputum, wheezing, chest tightness, ADL avoidance, smoking history, recurrent pulmonary infections, occupational exposure, weight loss, fatigue, anxiety, cognitive impairment, sleep disorders
- [P0757] **HECHO** — Advanced COPD exam findings: barrel chest, prolonged expiration, accessory muscle use, wheezing, rhonchi, distant heart sounds
- [P0758] **DEFINICIÓN** — Cor pulmonale signs: JVD, hepatic congestion, pedal edema
- [P0759] **DEFINICIÓN** — Normal spirometry: FEV1/FVC >=70% or FEV1 >=80% predicted
- [P0760] **DEFINICIÓN** — COPD spirometry: FEV1/FVC <=70%
- [P0761] **DEFINICIÓN** — Restrictive lung disease spirometry: FEV1/FVC >=90%
- [P0762] **HECHO** — FEV1/FVC ratio decreases with aging → can lead to overdiagnosis of COPD
- [P0763] **HECHO** — Spirometry not usually practical in PALTC; not feasible in frail or cognitively impaired residents
- [P0764] **HECHO** — COPD differential diagnosis: asthma, HF, bronchiectasis, recurrent aspiration, ACEi-induced cough, vocal cord dysfunction, PE, respiratory tract tumors
- [P0765] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD 1 Mild: FEV1 >=80% predicted (post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <70%)
- [P0766] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD 2 Moderate: 50% <= FEV1 <80% predicted
- [P0767] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD 3 Severe: 30% <= FEV1 <50% predicted
- [P0768] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD 4 Very severe: FEV1 <30% predicted
- [P0769] **DEFINICIÓN** — Chronic respiratory failure: PaO2 <60 mmHg +/- PaCO2 >50 mmHg breathing air at sea level
- [P0770] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD A: fewer symptoms, <2 exacerbations/yr
- [P0771] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD B: more symptoms, <2 exacerbations/yr
- [P0772] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD C: fewer symptoms, >2 exacerbations/yr
- [P0773] **DEFINICIÓN** — GOLD D: more symptoms, >2 exacerbations/yr
- [P0774] **REGLA** — COPD Group A: bronchodilator (short or long acting)
- [P0775] **REGLA** — COPD Group B: long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA)
- [P0776] **REGLA** — COPD Group C: LAMA
- [P0777] **REGLA** — COPD Group D: LAMA, or LAMA+LABA (if highly symptomatic), or ICS+LABA (if eosinophils >300/mcL)
- [P0778] **HECHO** — No COPD treatment has been shown to modify progressive decline in lung function
- [P0779] **HECHO** — Long-acting bronchodilators more effective than short-acting bronchodilators/anticholinergics (underutilized)
- [P0780] **HECHO** — Combination short-acting beta-agonists + muscarinic agonists > either alone in FEV1 improvement
- [P0781] **HECHO** — LAMA+LABA combination improves lung function, symptoms, reduces exacerbations vs monotherapy
- [P0782] **HECHO** — LAMAs more effective than LABAs in reducing COPD exacerbations and hospitalizations
- [P0783] **HECHO** — Theophylline: modest benefit, small effect on bronchodilation
- [P0784] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Routine antibiotics not recommended for COPD other than acute exacerbations
- [P0785] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Mucolytics not recommended for COPD (may consider for viscous mucous)
- [P0786] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Prolonged oral glucocorticoids should be avoided in COPD
- [P0787] **HECHO** — Statins and vasodilators show no benefit in COPD

### COPD Pharmacologic Agents

- [P0788] **REGLA** — Albuterol: 2 puffs q6h or 2.5-5 mg nebulized q4-6h; may be used for acute bronchospasm
- [P0789] **REGLA** — Levalbuterol: 0.63-1.25 mg nebulized q6-8h; may be used for acute bronchospasm
- [P0790] **REGLA** — Formoterol (LABA): 20 mcg nebulized q12h; NOT for acute bronchospasm
- [P0791] **REGLA** — Salmeterol (LABA): 1 puff q12h
- [P0792] **REGLA** — Ipratropium bromide: 2 puffs q6h or 0.5 mg nebulized q6-8h; may be used for acute exacerbation
- [P0793] **REGLA** — Tiotropium: 2 puffs q24h or 1 inhalation q24h; maintenance only, not for acute bronchospasm; caution with BPH and glaucoma
- [P0794] **REGLA** — ICS (beclomethasone, fluticasone, budesonide, mometasone): for severe COPD with repeated exacerbations; added to routine bronchodilator therapy
- [P0795] **REGLA** — Oral prednisone for COPD exacerbation: 30-40 mg/day x 5 days; monitor glucose in DM; risk of osteoporosis, myopathy, cataracts
- [P0796] **REGLA** — Theophylline ER: 400 mg/day; toxicity and multiple drug interactions; caution with liver and cardiac disease; check levels
- [P0797] **REGLA** — Roflumilast (PDE4 inhibitor): 500 mcg q24h; adjuvant therapy for severe COPD with exacerbations
- [P0798] **REGLA** — Long-term O2 therapy: if resting PaO2 <55 mmHg; use >15 h/day improves survival, exercise tolerance, cognition

### Acute COPD Exacerbations

- [P0799] **HECHO** — Majority of acute COPD exacerbations may be due to tracheobronchitis; 1/3 may be noninfectious
- [P0800] **REGLA** — COPD exacerbation protocol: increase dose/frequency beta agonists, use spacers/nebulizers, oral steroids (prednisolone 30-40 mg/day x 5 days), antibiotics if increased sputum volume/purulence (5 days), O2 supplementation (target SpO2 88-92%), CBC/CXR/EKG
- [P0801] **REGLA** — Consider hospitalization if response poor and respirations >28/min
- [P0802] **REGLA** — Hospitalize COPD if: fails to respond, tachypnea >28 breaths/min, persistent hypoxia, delirium, high fever, difficulty sleeping/eating, serious comorbidities (HF, DM, CKD, liver disease)
- [P0803] **HECHO** — COPD is major cause of 30-day hospital readmissions
- [P0804] **HECHO** — Non-pulmonary complications of COPD: CV and skeletal muscle changes, reduced BMI, frailty (increases mortality), sarcopenia, male hypogonadism, vitamin D deficiency, osteoporosis
- [P0805] **HECHO** — COPD patients: anxiety, depression, malnutrition, sleep disturbance, decreased awareness of hypoxia/hypercapnia, cognitive dysfunction
- [P0806] **HECHO** — 6-min walk distance <350 m predictive of future COPD exacerbations and death

### Diabetes

- [P0807] **HECHO** — Diabetes affects >30% of LTC residents
- [P0808] **HECHO** — DM associated with reduced life expectancy, multiple complications, higher risk of geriatric syndromes, independent predictor of LTC placement
- [P0809] **HECHO** — Annual nursing home expenditures for DM estimated $18.6 billion
- [P0810] **HECHO** — DM residents have higher prevalence of CVD, infections, lower extremity complications, pain, pressure ulcers, urinary incontinence, falls, poor dentition, cognitive dysfunction, functional dependency
- [P0811] **HECHO** — DM complications in elderly: atherosclerosis (MI, stroke), weight changes, confusion, dehydration, depression, skin problems, visual impairment, falls, foot ulcers/gangrene, frequent infections, neuropathy, nonketotic hyperosmolar coma, oral health problems, polypharmacy, urinary symptoms
- [P0812] **HECHO** — Medications causing hyperglycemia: glucocorticoids, atypical antipsychotics, beta-adrenergic agonists, thiazides, megesterol acetate
- [P0813] **HECHO** — Unique DM syndromes: diabetic neuropathic cachexia, diabetic neuropathy, renal papillary necrosis, diabetic amyotrophy, diabetic bullae, DKA (possibly caused by antipsychotics or SGLT2 inhibitors)

### Type 1 Diabetes in PALTC

- [P0814] **HECHO** — Type 1 DM may not be recognized due to incomplete records, care transitions, cognitive impairment, lack of social support
- [P0815] **HECHO** — DKA can be mistaken for sepsis, organ failure, or metabolic acidosis from other causes

### Key Recommendations for Managing Diabetes in PALTC

- [P0816] **REGLA** — Hypoglycemia risk is important factor in determining glucose control goals
- [P0817] **REGLA** — Simplified treatment plan preferred; sliding scale insulin as sole treatment should be avoided
- [P0818] **REGLA** — Use liberal diets to promote adequate food/fluid intake; less risk than restrictive diets
- [P0819] **REGLA** — Physical activity/exercise important; depend on current functional abilities

### Treatment Selection (Diabetes)

- [P0820] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Dietary restriction not recommended in LTC; food enjoyment, quality of life, weight loss prevention take precedence
- [P0821] **REGLA** — Metformin: first-line if no contraindications; safe if eGFR >30 mL/min; low hypoglycemia risk; may cause GI disturbances, weight loss, B12 deficiency
- [P0822] **REGLA** — Sulfonylureas: low cost but hypoglycemia risk; use short-acting glipizide to reduce hypoglycemia; avoid glyburide (renal elimination)
- [P0823] **REGLA** — Meglitinides: useful if variable eating habits; skip dose if skipped meal; useful with one large meal for postprandial control
- [P0824] **RESTRICCIÓN** — TZDs: low hypoglycemia risk, can use in CKD; contraindicated due to edema/HF, bone loss/fracture risk, bladder cancer concerns
- [P0825] **REGLA** — GLP1-RA: consider if overweight or glucose targets not met; low hypoglycemia risk; can use in CKD; adverse effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea; weekly dosing convenient
- [P0826] **REGLA** — SGLT2i: low hypoglycemia risk; ASCVD/HF benefit; decreases renal disease progression; avoid if frail/hydration issues; genital yeast infections/UTI risk
- [P0827] **REGLA** — Stepwise: Metformin first → if high CV risk/ASCVD/CKD/HF → add GLP1-RA or SGLT2i → if no high CV risk → DPP-4i, GLP1-RA, SGLT2i, or TZD
- [P0828] **RESTRICCIÓN** — DPP-4i and GLP1-RA cannot be used simultaneously
- [P0829] **REGLA** — SGLT2i with reduced LVEF <45% if HF present
- [P0830] **REGLA** — SGLT2i primary evidence of reducing CKD progression if diabetic kidney disease + albuminuria present

### Insulin

- [P0831] **REGLA** — Basal insulin initiation: 10 units daily or 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day, weekly increases of 2-3 units until desired fasting glucose
- [P0832] **REGLA** — Insulin indicated acutely for: symptomatic hyperglycemia despite max oral agents, catabolism (weight loss, ketosis, DKA), A1C >10%, BG persistently >300 mg/dL
- [P0833] **HECHO** — Basal analogs (U100 glargine, detemir) and longer-acting (U300 glargine, degludec) may have lower risk of hypoglycemia and nocturnal hypoglycemia
- [P0834] **REGLA** — Rapid acting insulin: administer 10-15 min before meal; insulin glulisine 15 min before to 20 min after meal; insulin lispro 15 min before or immediately after meal
- [P0835] **REGLA** — Prandial insulin: 4 units or 0.1 units/kg added to main meal when fasting BG controlled but A1C remains elevated

### Sliding-Scale Insulin

- [P0836] **RESTRICCIÓN** — SSI not recommended by any guideline as primary/sole treatment; on Beers criteria for potentially inappropriate medication use in older adults
- [P0837] **HECHO** — SSI results in increased injections, ~60% unnecessary fingersticks, medication errors, increased patient discomfort, increased nursing time
- [P0838] **REGLA** — SSI may be useful as correctional scale supplement to scheduled oral/insulin therapy when BG highly variable and patient medically unstable

### Goals of Treatment (Diabetes)

- [P0839] **REGLA** — Less stringent A1C goals (7.5-8.5%) appropriate for LTC residents with history of hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy, or multimorbidities
- [P0840] **REGLA** — Skilled rehabilitation: A1C unreliable due to recent acute illness; fasting/premeal glucose target 100-200 mg/dL
- [P0841] **REGLA** — LTC resident: avoid relying on A1C; interpret with caution; fasting/premeal glucose target 100-200 mg/dL; intensive glycemic control of limited benefit
- [P0842] **REGLA** — End-of-life resident: no role for A1C; avoid symptomatic hypo-/hyperglycemia; monitor periodically
- [P0843] **REGLA** — A1C should be monitored every 6 months if at goal, every 3 months if suboptimally controlled

### Medication Management (Diabetes Safety)

- [P0844] **REGLA** — Administer basal insulin in morning instead of night
- [P0845] **HECHO** — Linagliptin vs basal insulin: similar glycemic control, lower hypoglycemia rates, no renal dose adjustment needed
- [P0846] **REGLA** — If sulfonylurea used: avoid glyburide; use glimepiride or glipizide (hepatic elimination); monitor for hypoglycemia
- [P0847] **REGLA** — Consider SGLT2i (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin) if HF/CKD/albuminuria + adequate fluid intake + no recurrent UTIs (eGFR >45 or >60 mL/min/1.73m2 respectively)
- [P0848] **REGLA** — Simplify insulin: titrate basal to BG 90-150 mg/dL; if mealtime dose <10 U consider discontinuing + add non-insulin agent; if >10 U decrease by 50% + add non-insulin agent
- [P0849] **REGLA** — Second-generation basal insulins (degludec 200 U/mL, glargine 300 U/mL): for high-dose basal insulin or wide glucose fluctuations; similar control + lower severe hypoglycemia

### Hypoglycemia

- [P0850] **DEFINICIÓN** — Hypoglycemia Level 1: glucose <70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) and >54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L)
- [P0851] **DEFINICIÓN** — Hypoglycemia Level 2: glucose <54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L)
- [P0852] **DEFINICIÓN** — Hypoglycemia Level 3: severe event with altered mental/physical status requiring treatment
- [P0853] **HECHO** — Hypoglycemia symptoms in elderly may be atypical: disorientation, lethargy, weakness, falls, aggression, altered behavior
- [P0854] **HECHO** — Older adults have lower glucose threshold for developing symptoms; repeated episodes worsen dementia
- [P0855] **REGLA** — Treat hypoglycemia with 15 g glucose/carbohydrate; recheck in 15 min; repeat if still below target
- [P0856] **REGLA** — Obtunded patients: SC/IM glucagon 1 mg (or 1 unit) or 50% dextrose IV (usually 50 mL)
- [P0857] **HECHO** — Glucagon available as nasal spray in prefilled syringe (no mixing required)
- [P0858] **HECHO** — Risk factors for severe hypoglycemia: age, Black race, poor nutrition/variable intake, cognitive/functional impairment, loss of counter-regulation, unawareness, high insulin/SU doses, recent hospitalization, liver/renal/CV disease, endocrine deficiency, alcohol

### Monitoring (Diabetes)

- [P0859] **REGLA** — Multiple insulin injections/pump: pre-meal and bedtime glucose checks
- [P0860] **REGLA** — Simpler insulin regimen (1-2 doses/day): twice daily glucose monitoring >=3-4 days/week
- [P0861] **REGLA** — Oral agents or less frequent insulin: glucose monitoring 2x/day for 1-2 weeks after admission, then 1-2x/week
- [P0862] **HECHO** — A1C increased by: age, race (African American/Hispanic), hypothyroidism, splenectomy, aplastic anemia, polycythemia, Hb variants, iron deficiency, metabolic acidosis/uremia
- [P0863] **HECHO** — A1C decreased by: hemolytic anemia, blood loss, transfusions, hemodialysis, Hct <30%, liver disease, EPO therapy
- [P0864] **REGLA** — Avoid changing medication regimen based on isolated hyperglycemic episode

### Improving Interprofessional Communication

- [P0865] **REGLA** — Facility-wide DM protocols require admin buy-in; "diabetes nurse" or champion helps ensure implementation

### Optimizing Transitions of Care (Diabetes)

- [P0866] **HECHO** — Rehospitalization risk factors: poor health literacy, inadequate social support, Black race, age >75 yr, male, difficulty managing medications, high comorbidity burden
- [P0867] **REGLA** — Hospital to LTC transfer must include: H&P, progress notes, consultation reports, accurate diagnosis list, lab/imaging reports, current med list (reconciled), time of last basal insulin dose, hypoglycemia episodes, meal consumption

### Thyroid Disorders

- [P0869] **HECHO** — Normal aging: progressive fibrosis/atrophy of thyroid gland but HPT axis remains intact
- [P0870] **HECHO** — With aging: decline in TSH → reduced T4/T3 secretion; but T4 levels remain normal due to reduced renal clearance; T3 declines in advanced age; reverse T3 increases
- [P0871] **HECHO** — Mean TSH levels increase with age; TPO antibodies increase with age
- [P0872] **HECHO** — Thyroid disorders more prevalent in women, lower in Blacks than Whites
- [P0873] **HECHO** — Hypothyroidism risk factors: female, iodine deficiency, autoimmune conditions, selenium deficiency, medication, syndromic conditions (e.g., Down's)
- [P0874] **HECHO** — Hyperthyroidism risk factors: female, iodine deficiency, autoimmune conditions, smoking, selenium deficiency, medication
- [P0875] **HECHO** — Colorado Thyroid Disease Prevalence Study: 16% women, 21% men >74 yr had elevated TSH
- [P0876] **HECHO** — Adults >60 have 2-10% prevalence of hypothyroidism
- [P0877] **REGLA** — Serum TSH is first-line test for thyroid dysfunction
- [P0878] **DEFINICIÓN** — Normal TSH reference range: 0.4-4.1 mU/L; in >80 yr normal TSH can be up to 7.9 mU/L
- [P0879] **TENSIÓN** — TSH values above 4.0-5.0 mU/L may be normal in elderly → overdiagnosis of hypothyroidism can result in iatrogenic hyperthyroidism
- [P0880] **HECHO** — Conditions associated with thyroid disease: biological agents, CKD, cognitive impairment, Down's/Turner's, amiodarone, lithium, hyperlipidemia, head/neck irradiation, pituitary surgery, Type 1 DM, severe head injury, unexplained depression/weight loss
- [P0881] **HECHO** — Drugs causing hypothyroidism: lithium, iodine (all forms), amiodarone, interferon alpha
- [P0882] **HECHO** — Drugs causing hyperthyroidism: amiodarone, iodine, interleukin-2, interferon alpha
- [P0883] **HECHO** — Drugs reducing T4→T3 conversion: glucocorticoids, iodine, PTU, propranolol, amiodarone
- [P0884] **HECHO** — Drugs suppressing TSH: dopamine, dobutamine, glucocorticoids, phenytoin, bromocriptine, somatostatin analogs, metformin, mitotane
- [P0885] **HECHO** — Drugs increasing T4 clearance: carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampin, phenobarbital
- [P0886] **HECHO** — Drugs reducing T4 binding to TBG: phenytoin, carbamazepine, salsalate, NSAIDs, furosemide, heparin
- [P0887] **HECHO** — Drugs influencing thyroxine absorption: PPIs, cholestyramine, aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate, ferrous sulfate, sucralfate
- [P0888] **HECHO** — Non-thyroidal illness: low T3, normal TSH/FT4/T4; severely ill may have low T4 + low T3; TSH can decline with severe illness; transient high TSH during recovery
- [P0889] **REGLA** — Repeat thyroid function tests once patient is medically stable before initiating thyroid replacement

### Hypothyroidism

- [P0890] **DEFINICIÓN** — Overt hypothyroidism: serum FT4 below normal with hypothyroid symptoms
- [P0891] **DEFINICIÓN** — Subclinical hypothyroidism: elevated TSH with normal FT4
- [P0892] **HECHO** — Majority of hypothyroidism is primary (thyroid gland failure); central/secondary <1% of cases
- [P0893] **HECHO** — Primary hypothyroidism causes: Hashimoto's thyroiditis, post-131I treatment, thyroidectomy, head/neck radiation, drugs
- [P0894] **HECHO** — Central hypothyroidism causes: hypothalamic/pituitary tumors/infiltrative lesions, pituitary surgery, head injury, cranial radiation, stroke
- [P0895] **HECHO** — Fatigue in 68% and weakness in 53% of hypothyroid older adults; classical symptoms (weight gain, cold intolerance, paresthesias, muscle cramps) less frequent in elderly
- [P0896] **HECHO** — Hypothyroidism symptoms: fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, dysphagia, exertional dyspnea, lack of concentration, memory loss/delusions/psychosis, hearing loss, depression, generalized weakness/muscle cramps
- [P0897] **HECHO** — Hypothyroidism signs: alopecia, xerosis, hoarseness, weight gain, bradycardia, diastolic HTN, worsened CHF, anemia, hyperlipidemia, elevated CPK, myxedema, neuropathy, slowed reflexes
- [P0898] **REGLA** — Goal of hypothyroidism treatment: normalize TSH to achieve euthyroid state
- [P0899] **REGLA** — Synthetic thyroid preparations preferred over thyroid extracts (longer half-life, more constant serum level)
- [P0900] **REGLA** — Initial levothyroxine dose: 25-50 mcg/day; in significant cardiac comorbidities start 12.5-25 mcg/day; adjust by similar amount every 3-6 weeks until TSH normalized
- [P0901] **REGLA** — After TSH normalized, check TSH every 6-12 months
- [P0902] **REGLA** — In primary hypothyroidism: TSH alone monitors treatment; in central hypothyroidism: use FT4 level
- [P0903] **HECHO** — Full replacement dose of levothyroxine (no residual thyroid function): 1.6 mcg/kg/day (typically 100-150 mcg)
- [P0904] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Low-normal or subnormal TSH levels should be avoided; thyroxine can be held days-weeks and restarted at lower dose once stable
- [P0905] **HECHO** — Linear changes in T4 concentration correspond to logarithmic changes in serum TSH
- [P0906] **REGLA** — After inadvertent discontinuation of levothyroxine during care transition: resume at prior documented dose; measure FT4 may be helpful

### Subclinical Hypothyroidism

- [P0907] **DEFINICIÓN** — Subclinical hypothyroidism: elevated TSH with normal FT4; anti-TPO antibodies positive in 67% of NH residents with this condition
- [P0908] **HECHO** — Prevalence in 80-year-olds: 14-20%
- [P0909] **HECHO** — Etiology: usually chronic autoimmune thyroiditis; also poor levothyroxine compliance, suboptimal treatment, recovery from severe illness, thyroiditis, meds affecting thyroid function
- [P0910] **REGLA** — Repeat TSH in 1-3 months to confirm diagnosis
- [P0911] **HECHO** — Subclinical hypothyroidism progresses to overt hypothyroidism in 33-55% of individuals
- [P0912] **HECHO** — Risk factors for progression: TSH >12 mU/L, autoantibodies, goiter, advanced age, history of radiation therapy
- [P0913] **HECHO** — Current evidence does not support treating subclinical hypothyroidism to improve cardiac function or neuropsychiatric symptoms
- [P0914] **REGLA** — TSH 4.5-10 mU/L without symptoms → repeat TSH in 6 months, yearly if asymptomatic
- [P0915] **REGLA** — TSH upper limit 4.5-6.9 mU/L → no treatment in >65-70 yr (age appropriate); avoid treatment in >80 yr
- [P0916] **REGLA** — TSH 7.0-9.9 mU/L → treat if hypothyroid symptoms present; goal TSH 3-6 mU/L
- [P0917] **REGLA** — TSH >10 mU/L → administer thyroid treatment in most circumstances

### Hyperthyroidism

- [P0918] **HECHO** — Primary and subclinical hyperthyroidism reported up to 2% in >60 yr
- [P0919] **HECHO** — Hyperthyroidism risks: cardiac arrhythmias, worsening osteoporosis, increased fracture incidence
- [P0920] **HECHO** — Endogenous causes: toxic multinodular goiter (40%), toxic adenoma (30%), Graves' disease (15%), thyroiditis (5%)
- [P0921] **HECHO** — Exogenous causes: suppressive levothyroxine dosing for thyroid carcinoma (10%), excessive levothyroxine dosing (5%)
- [P0922] **HECHO** — T3 toxicosis: low TSH with isolated T3 elevation; usually from thyroid nodule or goiter
- [P0923] **HECHO** — Amiodarone-related thyrotoxicosis more frequent in older adults with Afib; amiodarone more often causes hypothyroidism
- [P0924] **HECHO** — Hyperthyroidism signs/symptoms in elderly: Afib (up to 20%), weight loss, depression/cognitive decline/delirium, lethargy, fatigue, heat intolerance, tremor, severe osteoporosis
- [P0925] **HECHO** — Atypical signs in elderly: vomiting, constipation, depression, fatigue; classic signs (heat intolerance, anxiety, tachycardia, tremor) may be lacking
- [P0926] **HECHO** — Thyroid storm: severe hypermetabolic state, fever, neurocognitive changes, possibly HF; most common cause in elderly is infection
- [P0927] **DEFINICIÓN** — Overt primary hyperthyroidism: low TSH + elevated total and FT4
- [P0928] **DEFINICIÓN** — Subclinical hyperthyroidism: low TSH + normal total or FT4
- [P0929] **REGLA** — Positive TSI or thyroid antibodies help confirm Graves' disease diagnosis
- [P0930] **REGLA** — Subacute thyroiditis: beta blockers for hyperadrenergic symptoms; glucocorticoids if refractory; low-dose NSAIDs for pain
- [P0931] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Beta blockers: avoid in decompensated HF, asthma, Raynaud's disease
- [P0932] **REGLA** — Graves' disease: antithyroid meds (methimazole, PTU) + radioiodine therapy; radioiodine favored over long-term meds or surgery
- [P0933] **REGLA** — PTU: elevated risk of agranulocytosis during first 90 days; monitor LFTs regularly; use short course before radioiodine
- [P0934] **REGLA** — Amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis Type 1: high-dose methimazole (40-60 mg/day) or PTU (600-800 mg/day)
- [P0935] **REGLA** — Amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis Type 2: long course glucocorticoids; glucocorticoids for relapse
- [P0936] **REGLA** — Thyroid storm treatment: fluid/electrolyte stabilization, high-dose methimazole (40-80 mg/day) or PTU (200 mg q6h), beta blocker, stress-dose glucocorticoids, potassium iodide 1 h after antithyroid medication
- [P0937] **REGLA** — Subclinical hyperthyroidism: treat same as overt when TSH <0.1 mIU/L

### Thyroid Nodules and Thyroid Cancer

- [P0938] **HECHO** — Over age 65, 50% have thyroid nodules detected by ultrasound; 5% of thyroid nodules may be malignant
- [P0939] **HECHO** — Thyroid cancer prevalence higher in: >60-65 yr, history of head/neck irradiation, family history thyroid cancer, total body irradiation for BMT, MEN2, familial adenomatous polyposis, Cowden syndrome

### Follow-Up of Patients with History of Thyroid Cancer

- [P0940] **REGLA** — TSH suppression with levothyroxine may not be necessary if cancer was low risk without recurrence, or patient has cardiac arrhythmias or low bone density
- [P0941] **REGLA** — Thyroglobulin should be undetectable with TSH stimulation post-treatment
- [P0942] **REGLA** — If anti-Tg antibodies present, serum Tg alone cannot be used as marker for recurrent/persistent thyroid cancer → further imaging needed
- [P0943] **REGLA** — Neck ultrasound identifies malignant cervical lymph nodes (common recurrence site for papillary thyroid cancer)
- [P0944] **REGLA** — Patients treated with thyroidectomy + radioiodine ablation or thyroid cancer should be referred to endocrinologist/oncologist
- [P0945] **HECHO** — Unexplained hypocalcemia may be due to hypoparathyroidism from partial parathyroidectomy during prior thyroid surgery

### Vitamin B12 Deficiency

- [P0946] **HECHO** — B12 deficiency prevalence in older adults: 10-15%; hospitalized elderly from community/NH: 5% with low B12 (<200 pg/mL), 20% with marginal B12 (200-349 pg/mL)
- [P0947] **HECHO** — Elderly may have "subtle B12 deficiency" with only biochemical deficiency
- [P0948] **HECHO** — Older age and more established symptoms associated with lower likelihood of neurologic improvement when treatment delayed
- [P0949] **HECHO** — B12 deficiency associated with low bone mineral density and implicated in pre-frailty
- [P0950] **HECHO** — Dietary cobalamin deficiency responsible for 40-50% of B12 deficiency; pernicious anemia ~10%
- [P0951] **HECHO** — Stomach acid needed to release B12 from food → binds haptocorrin → pancreatic enzyme degradation → binds intrinsic factor → absorbed in terminal ileum → binds transcobalamin for transport
- [P0952] **HECHO** — B12 deficiency causes: atrophic gastritis/hypochlorhydria, chronic antacid use (H2 blockers, PPIs), gastrectomy, ileal resection, small intestine disorders (Crohn's, sprue, malabsorption), pancreatic insufficiency, H. pylori, bacterial overgrowth, strict vegetarian diet, pernicious anemia, AIDS/zidovudine, metformin
- [P0953] **HECHO** — B12 deficiency signs: hematologic (macrocytosis, anemia, neutrophil hypersegmentation, pancytopenia), neurologic (peripheral neuropathy, paresthesias, loss of vibration/position sense, ataxia, orthostatic hypotension), neuropsychiatric (delirium, slow thinking, depression, confusion, memory loss), glossitis, pallor
- [P0954] **HECHO** — B12 deficiency often has NO associated anemia or macrocytosis
- [P0955] **REGLA** — Measure B12 in frail elderly, macrocytic anemia, hypersegmented neutrophils, gait disorders, peripheral neuropathy, neuropsychiatric symptoms without obvious cause
- [P0956] **DEFINICIÓN** — Serum B12 <200 pg/mL (150 pmol/L) = B12 deficiency diagnosis
- [P0957] **HECHO** — Falsely low B12: AIDS, multiple myeloma, folate deficiency, excessive vitamin C
- [P0958] **HECHO** — Falsely normal/high B12: transcobalamin II deficiency, myeloproliferative disorders (CML), liver disease, intestinal bacterial overgrowth
- [P0959] **DEFINICIÓN** — Serum B12 200-350 pg/mL (150-260 pmol/L) = borderline B12 deficiency; can be treated by supplementation
- [P0960] **REGLA** — MMA (serum or urine) is specific for B12 deficiency; consider in borderline B12 levels to confirm clinically significant deficiency; MMA normalizes with treatment
- [P0961] **HECHO** — Falsely elevated MMA in hypovolemia and renal insufficiency
- [P0962] **REGLA** — Consider H. pylori testing when evaluating B12 deficiency
- [P0963] **REGLA** — Folate deficiency can coexist with B12 deficiency; check folate levels when B12 deficiency diagnosed
- [P0964] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Failure to treat folate deficiency before starting B12 replacement can result in spinal cord demyelination
- [P0965] **HECHO** — Schilling test or gastric function tests rarely of added benefit; bone marrow biopsy rarely needed but consider in pancytopenia
- [P0966] **REGLA** — Symptomatic B12 deficiency treatment: cyanocobalamin 1000 mcg IM weekly x 3 weeks, then monthly or every other month
- [P0967] **REGLA** — Incidental B12 deficiency: oral replacement; crystalline oral B12 >=500-1000 mcg/day required to reverse biochemical disorder
- [P0968] **REGLA** — Impaired B12 absorption: higher oral doses 1000-2000 mcg daily if lack of intrinsic factor or terminal ileum pathology
- [P0969] **HECHO** — Sublingual, transdermal, intranasal B12 more costly and less rigorously tested
- [P0970] **HECHO** — No evidence for supplemental B12 as tonic to improve well-being
- [P0971] **REGLA** — B12 deficiency with anemia: check reticulocyte count 1 week after starting treatment; Hb in 1-2 months
- [P0972] **REGLA** — B12 treatment may unmask folate and iron deficiency as red cell production increases → supplementation may be required

- [P0974] **DEFINICIÓN** — Norwegian (crusted) scabies: severe atypical infection with extremely pruritic crusty lesions, thousands-millions of live mites

- [P0978] **DEFINICIÓN** — Scabies outbreak: >=1 confirmed case within finite time in defined facility location

- [P0985] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Lindane no longer recommended for scabies due to mite resistance and neurotoxicity
- [P0986] **REGLA** — Permethrin 5% cream: apply head to toe, leave on 8-14 h; 90% effective after first application; second application may be needed 7-10 days later
- [P0987] **REGLA** — All symptomatic patients + close contacts including staff treated with permethrin in same 24-48 h period
- [P0988] **REGLA** — Permethrin applied entire skin hairline to feet including palms, soles, under finger/toenails; scalp rarely unless infested
- [P0989] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Topical steroid creams/antihistamines for pruritus should not be applied until scabicide removed
- [P0990] **REGLA** — Oral ivermectin (Stromectol): effective alternative to topical scabicides; single dose 200 mcg/kg (standard 6 mg for 70 kg adult); not FDA-approved for scabies
- [P0991] **REGLA** — Second scabies treatment needed in 1-2 weeks after eggs mature (therapies not effective against mite eggs)

### Herpes Zoster

- [P0992] **DEFINICIÓN** — Herpes zoster (HZ): reactivation of varicella zoster virus (VZV) latent in sensory ganglia
- [P0993] **HECHO** — 2/3 of HZ cases diagnosed in >50-year-olds
- [P0994] **HECHO** — HZ characterized by pruritic maculopapular vesicular rash evolving into noninfectious dried crusts over 5-6 days
- [P0995] **HECHO** — HZ triggered by decline in cell-mediated immunity
- [P0996] **HECHO** — HZ lesions: "dew drops on rose petal," clustered in dermatomal distribution, not crossing midline
- [P0997] **REGLA** — HZ diagnosis: clinical; lab via PCR (preferred if available) or DFA testing
- [P0998] **HECHO** — ~20% of affected older persons develop post-herpetic neuralgia (PHN)
- [P0999] **HECHO** — HZ complications: ophthalmic branch trigeminal nerve (uveitis, keratitis, blindness), nasociliary branch (pharynx/nose tip vesicles), Ramsay Hunt syndrome (ear vesicles, deafness, facial palsy, vertigo)
- [P1000] **HECHO** — HZ not contagious to those with history of chickenpox or adequate IgG titers to varicella
- [P1001] **REGLA** — HZ skin lesions: keep clean/dry; avoid antibiotic ointments and adhesive dressings (delay healing)
- [P1002] **REGLA** — Ophthalmic zoster and immunocompromised patients must be treated with antivirals
- [P1003] **REGLA** — Zoster vaccination recommended for >=50 yr regardless of prior HZ history (unless contraindicated); not for immunocompromised
- [P1004] **HECHO** — Zostavax: 51% fewer HZ episodes, 66% less PHN overall; but only 18% and 26% respectively in >=80 yr
- [P1005] **HECHO** — Shingrix (released 2019): 97% effective in preventing HZ in >50 yr; given 2 doses, 2-6 months apart
- [P1006] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Topical antiviral treatment for HZ is not efficacious
- [P1007] **REGLA** — Systemic antiviral for HZ: if >=50 yr, moderate-severe pain/rash, or non-truncal involvement within 72 h of rash onset; begin immediately if neurologic/ocular signs, severe pain, or rash
- [P1008] **REGLA** — Acyclovir for HZ: 800 mg 5x daily (q4-5h) x 7-10 days (less preferred due to dosing schedule)
- [P1009] **REGLA** — Famciclovir for HZ: 500 mg TID x 7 days
- [P1010] **REGLA** — Valacyclovir for HZ: 1000 mg TID x 7 days
- [P1011] **REGLA** — Monitor renal function in HZ patients with renal insufficiency on antivirals
- [P1012] **REGLA** — HZ pain adjuncts: acetaminophen/opioids/tramadol; gabapentin (max 3600 mg/day), pregabalin (75 mg BID), or low-dose TCA (nortriptyline 25 mg hs, up to 150 mg/day)
- [P1013] **HECHO** — Oral corticosteroids for HZ: common but lack evidence for improving QoL or reducing PHN incidence
- [P1014] **REGLA** — Refer to pain specialist for neural blockade evaluation if analgesics/adjuncts/corticosteroids ineffective for PHN

### Clostridioides difficile Infection

- [P1015] **HECHO** — C. difficile: 500,000 infections annually nationwide
- [P1016] **HECHO** — 1 in 6 CDI patients experience recurrence in 2-8 weeks; 1 in 11 adults >65 yr with healthcare-associated CDI die within 1 month
- [P1017] **HECHO** — C. difficile colonizes gut after normal flora altered by antibiotic treatment
- [P1018] **HECHO** — NAP1/BI/027 strain implicated in CDI outbreaks; capable of increased toxin A and B production
- [P1019] **HECHO** — ~8-10% of PA/LTC residents are C. difficile carriers
- [P1020] **HECHO** — CDI risk factors: advanced age, frequent hospitalization, recurrent antibiotics (especially clindamycin, 3rd-gen cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones), comorbidities, PPI/H2RA use, immunosuppression
- [P1021] **HECHO** — CDI symptoms: watery diarrhea, abdominal cramps, constipation/ileus, fever (may be afebrile); onset during or up to 8 weeks after antibiotics
- [P1022] **HECHO** — CDI in PA/LTC may present as fever, confusion, abdominal pain, anorexia, nausea, leukocytosis (often >20,000 WBC), hypoalbuminemia, distinctive fecal odor
- [P1023] **REGLA** — CDI diagnosis: test diarrheal stool; EIA for toxins A/B; PCR for toxins A/B (superior, 1 h; beware false positives); EIA for GDH (screening, cannot distinguish toxigenic strains)
- [P1024] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Repeat CDI testing to confirm cure NOT recommended
- [P1025] **REGLA** — Discontinue inciting antibiotics; avoid antiperistaltic agents; consider discontinuing PPIs
- [P1026] **REGLA** — Mild-moderate CDI: metronidazole 500 mg PO TID x 10-14 days; vancomycin 125 mg PO QID if unable to take metronidazole or no improvement in 5-7 days
- [P1027] **DEFINICIÓN** — Severe CDI: albumin <3 g/dL + WBC >15,000 or abdominal tenderness
- [P1028] **REGLA** — Severe CDI: vancomycin 125 mg PO QID
- [P1029] **DEFINICIÓN** — Severe complicated CDI: ICU admission, hypotension, fever, ileus/abdominal distension, confusion, WBC >35,000 or <2,000, lactate >2 mmol/L
- [P1030] **REGLA** — Severe complicated CDI: vancomycin 500 mg PO QID; vancomycin enema (500 mg in 500 mL NS QID) + metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h if oral not tolerated
- [P1031] **HECHO** — CDI recurrence rate: 10-20% within 8 weeks
- [P1032] **REGLA** — Recurrent CDI: confirm diagnosis, conservative for mild symptoms, same regimen as initial episode, "pulsed vancomycin," fidaxomicin 200 mg BID x 10 days, or fecal microbiota transplantation
- [P1033] **REGLA** — CDI infection control: private rooms, antibiotic stewardship, hand hygiene, contact precautions (gowns/gloves), sporicidal disinfectant for environmental surfaces
- [P1034] **HECHO** — No conclusive evidence supporting probiotics for CDI treatment or prevention

### Acute Kidney Injury

- [P1035] **HECHO** — Age-related renal changes: decreased GFR, decreased urine concentrating ability (→ nocturia, hypernatremia, poor hypovolemia compensation), reduced Na conservation, reduced Na excretion, decreased K excretion
- [P1036] **DEFINICIÓN** — AKI: acute increase in creatinine or decrease in urine output to <0.5 mL/kg/h for >=6 h
- [P1037] **HECHO** — AKI incidence increases with older age, male gender, Black race, chronic inflammation, poor nutritional status
- [P1038] **HECHO** — Due to low muscle mass from aging/frailty, serum creatinine may be normal/near-normal despite severe renal impairment
- [P1039] **DEFINICIÓN** — AKI classification: prerenal, intrinsic renal (glomerular, tubulointerstitial, vascular), postrenal
- [P1040] **HECHO** — Prerenal AKI causes: volume depletion, low arterial volume (decompensated HF), renal hypoperfusion (bilateral renal artery stenosis), medications (ACEi, ARBs, NSAIDs, diuretics, SGLT2i)
- [P1041] **REGLA** — Prerenal AKI treatment: stop/reduce offending medications, restore intravascular volume with oral hydration or IV fluids
- [P1042] **HECHO** — Obstructive uropathy causes: BPH, bladder carcinoma, urethral stricture, anticholinergic medications, spinal cord injury, diabetic autonomic neuropathy, ureteral obstruction
- [P1043] **HECHO** — ATN causes: ischemia, sepsis, nephrotoxins (cisplatin, vancomycin, aminoglycosides, rhabdomyolysis, iodinated contrast agents)
- [P1044] **HECHO** — Acute interstitial nephritis: allergic response to NSAIDs, PPIs, antibiotics; also infections and rheumatological disorders
- [P1045] **REGLA** — ATN urine findings: epithelial cells and granular casts; FENa >2% (usually <1% in prerenal)
- [P1046] **HECHO** — Medications contributing to hyperkalemia in AKI: ACEi, ARBs, renin inhibitors, NSAIDs, spironolactone, amiloride, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, TMP-SMX, pentamidine

### Conclusion

- [P1047] **HECHO** — HF 5-year mortality: 74% with normal LVEF, 92% with reduced LVEF (repeated for emphasis in conclusion)
- [P1048] **HECHO** — HTN treatment in >80 yr results in significant reduction in stroke and all-cause mortality within 1-2 yr

## Preventing Hospital Admissions and Readmissions


### Introduction

- [P1049] **HECHO** — National average readmission rate from NF/SNF ≈18% for FY 2019 per CMS
- [P1050] **REGLA** — Affordable Care Act established Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP) starting October 1, 2012
- [P1051] **DEFINICIÓN** — HRRP goal: encourage hospitals improve communication/care coordination to reduce avoidable readmissions
- [P1052] **DEFINICIÓN** — Excess Readmission Ratio (ERR) = ratio of predicted-to-expected readmission rates; measures hospital performance vs regional/national rates
- [P1053] **HECHO** — CMS evaluates 6 diagnoses via ERR: heart failure, acute MI, COPD, pneumonia, CABG surgery, elective primary total hip/knee arthroplasty
- [P1054] **REGLA** — HRRP reports 30-day risk-standardized unplanned readmission rate including readmissions to same or another acute care hospital for any reason
- [P1055] **REGLA** — Protecting Access to Medicare Act 2014 mandated penalties for NF/SNF starting October 1, 2018 for excessive readmissions → `HRRP`
- [P1056] **HECHO** — Patient Driven Payment Model (PDPM) replaced fee-for-service in FY 2020
- [P1057] **HECHO** — CMS provided NF/SNF quarterly readmission reports starting October 2016
- [P1058] **HECHO** — FY 2019: facilities could obtain 1.6% Medicare Part A payment bonus or 2% payment reduction based on readmission data
- [P1059] **HECHO** — 3% of SNFs received maximum bonus 1.6%; ≈20% of SNFs got maximum cut 2%
- [P1060] **HECHO** — Adverse hospital outcomes for frail elders include: distress/discomfort, delirium (environment change), polypharmacy/drug errors in transitions, falls/fractures, incontinence/improper catheter use, HAI, weight loss/poor nutrition, immobility/de-conditioning/pressure wounds (Ouslander 2016)

### Transitions of Care

- [P1061] **HECHO** — Care transitions primarily involve 3 systems: NF/SNF, hospital, primary care in community
- [P1062] **HECHO** — Most transitions: hospital→NF/SNF, NF/SNF→hospital, NF/SNF→community
- [P1063] **REQUISITO** — Successful transfers require: (1) patient-centered/family-oriented care, (2) effective communication between all care systems
- [P1064] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Imperative to review plan of care with healthcare proxy, POA, and/or family caregivers as well as patient
- [P1065] **OBLIGACIÓN** — During care transition, goals of care must be reviewed with patient (if capacity) or proxy/surrogate decision maker
- [P1066] **HECHO** — Care transitions = opportune time to clarify wishes re: future hospitalizations, ER visits, NF/SNF care
- [P1067] **REGLA** — Goals of care should include discussions on CPR, DNR, and if appropriate "do not transfer to ER" / "do not admit to hospital"
- [P1068] **DEFINICIÓN** — Geriatrics 4M model (2017, IHI + AHA): Mind, Mobility, Medications, Matters Most
- [P1069] **HECHO** — Mind = dementia/delirium evaluation; Mobility = fall prevention/evaluation; Medications = polypharmacy review/de-prescribing; Matters Most = advance care planning/serious illness conversation
- [P1070] **HECHO** — Some organizations added 5th M: multi-morbidity (multi-complexity) as additional factor in reducing avoidable hospitalizations

### Effective Communication

- [P1071] **REQUISITO** — Discharge summary should be sent with patient at time of discharge + copy to PCP
- [P1072] **REQUISITO** — Discharge summary must include: updated medication list + reasons for changes, discharge instructions, conditions to monitor, contact person at discharging facility, pertinent consultations/ER visits/follow-up visits, copies of advance directives and goals of care including DNR status
- [P1073] **REQUISITO** — Follow-up phone call to discharged patients/caregivers should discuss: medication issues (reconciliation), planned follow-up visits, follow-up lab work/testing
- [P1074] **REQUISITO** — To reduce avoidable admissions: (1) practitioners must acquire/maintain excellent geriatric competencies, (2) NF/SNF must ensure timely follow-up upon admission or acute change

### PCP Fax Transitional Care Summary: SNF to Home

- [P1075] **HECHO** — NF discharge summaries frequently not completed by day of discharge → delay in information transfer to PCP
- [P1076] **OBLIGACIÓN** — PCP Fax Summary should be completed and faxed on day of discharge to receiving PCP
- [P1077] **RESTRICCIÓN** — PCP Fax should not be faxed before day of transfer (may be incomplete) nor after (may be forgotten)
- [P1078] **REGLA** — PCP Fax does not require signature as it would delay completion
- [P1079] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Do not give PCP Fax copy to patient in lieu of faxing; PCP may not receive it timely

### Accountable Care Organizations (ACO)

- [P1080] **DEFINICIÓN** — CMS defines ACOs as groups of doctors/hospitals/providers who voluntarily coordinate high-quality care to Medicare patients
- [P1081] **REGLA** — Medicare incentivizes ACOs by sharing cost savings while placing ACO at risk for financial losses if quality standards not met
- [P1082] **HECHO** — 3 diagnoses consistently common reasons for hospitalization from NF: septicemia, CHF, pneumonia (per Medicare/AHRQ data)
- [P1083] **HECHO** — 60-75% of hospital admissions for septicemia/CHF/pneumonia from NF/SNF are potentially avoidable
- [P1084] **HECHO** — ACOs can refer frail adults with acute illness directly to NF/SNF rather than hospital admission
- [P1085] **HECHO** — INTERACT II (Ouslander et al.) when implemented in nursing homes demonstrated improved quality of care and reduction of avoidable hospitalizations

### Acute Changes in Condition

- [P1086] **REQUISITO** — Physicians/NPs/PAs must be available to assess patients within 24-48h when acute change in condition occurs
- [P1087] **HECHO** — Common acute changes requiring licensed staff knowledge: change in mental status, chest pain, CHF, dehydration, acute aggressive behavior, fever of unknown origin, UTI/asymptomatic bacteriuria, pneumonia

### Change in Mental Status

- [P1088] **HECHO** — Frequent causes of acute mental status change in frail older adults: medication side effects, new-onset infections, cognitive decline progression, pain
- [P1089] **HECHO** — Prevalence of memory loss in NF patients: 50-80%
- [P1090] **REQUISITO** — Critical to obtain baseline cognitive assessment within first few days after admission to NF or assisted living
- [P1091] **REGLA** — When recently admitted patient diagnosed with delirium/depression, cognition baseline should be reevaluated after resolution of acute change

### Chest Pain

- [P1092] **TENSIÓN** — Chest pain is emergency but calling 911 may not be best response depending on patient goals of care
- [P1093] **REGLA** — For patients with "comfort" goal of care: treat chest pain with aspirin, SL nitroglycerin, SL morphine sulfate as appropriate (especially if on hospice)

### Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)

- [P1094] **HECHO** — CHF is one of most common diagnoses for NF→hospital transfers; many are avoidable
- [P1095] **OBLIGACIÓN** — During monthly/bimonthly visits, residents' weights should be reviewed
- [P1096] **REGLA** — Except for rare situations (e.g., ruptured heart valve), PCPs should treat CHF at facility and avoid hospitalization unless refractory pulmonary edema develops

### Dehydration

- [P1097] **HECHO** — Dehydration frequently caused by protracted vomiting/diarrhea without adequate fluid replacement
- [P1098] **REGLA** — Early interventions include: antiemetic medication, holding/reducing dose of medications causing adverse effects (warfarin, diuretics, insulin, oral diabetics, GI meds), IV fluids or increased oral intake
- [P1099] **HECHO** — >60% LTCF residents have significant cognitive impairment → may need family member present during IV therapy
- [P1100] **HECHO** — No psychotropic medications FDA-approved for treating behavioral/psychological symptoms of dementia

### Behavioral Problems

- [P1101] **HECHO** — Hospitalizing residents with serious behavioral problems has little benefit other than temporary removal from NF
- [P1102] **REGLA** — As last resort, off-label medications can be used for disruptive behaviors; PCP/staff must share risks/benefits with POA including antipsychotic "black box warning"

### Acute Onset of Fever

- [P1103] **REGLA** — NFs should avoid standing orders of antipyretics without notifying PCP of temperature change
- [P1104] **HECHO** — Castle et al.: repeated oral temps >99 degrees F (37.2 C) = sensitivity 80%, specificity 89% for fever
- [P1105] **REGLA** — Significant fever criteria (Castle): single oral temp >100 degrees F (37.8 C) OR single temp 2 degrees F (1.1 C) above baseline OR repeated oral temps >99 degrees F (37.2 C) → report to PCP
- [P1106] **HECHO** — CMS reports pneumonia and urosepsis = 2 common diagnoses among NF/SNF residents leading to hospitalization
- [P1107] **HECHO** — >40% urine specimens of frail older adult females in NFs will have bacteriuria
- [P1108] **HECHO** — If resident has only mental status change without other UTI signs/symptoms, only 11% will have UTI

### Asymptomatic Bacteriuria and Urinary Tract Infections

- [P1109] **DEFINICIÓN** — SHEA criteria for UTI in LTC residents without indwelling catheter require both: (1) ≥1 sign/symptom subcriteria AND (2) microbiologic subcriteria
- [P1110] **REGLA** — Without catheter, sign/symptom subcriteria (i): acute dysuria or acute pain/swelling/tenderness of testes/epididymis/prostate
- [P1111] **REGLA** — Without catheter, sign/symptom subcriteria (ii): fever or leukocytosis (neutrophilia >14,000 or left shift >6% bands or ≥1500 bands/mm3) AND ≥1 localizing urinary subcriteria (costovertebral angle pain/tenderness, suprapubic pain, gross hematuria, new/marked increase in urgency/incontinence/frequency)
- [P1112] **REGLA** — Without catheter, in absence of fever/leukocytosis: ≥2 localizing urinary subcriteria required (suprapubic pain, gross hematuria, increased incontinence/urgency/frequency)
- [P1113] **REGLA** — Without catheter, microbiologic subcriteria: ≥100,000 cfu/mL of ≤2 species in voided sample OR ≥100 cfu/mL of any organisms in in-and-out catheter specimen
- [P1114] **REGLA** — With indwelling catheter, sign/symptom subcriteria include: fever/rigors/new-onset hypotension (no alternate infection site); acute mental status change or functional decline with leukocytosis (no alternate diagnosis); new-onset suprapubic/costovertebral pain; purulent discharge around catheter or acute testicular/epididymal/prostatic pain/swelling
- [P1115] **REGLA** — With indwelling catheter, microbiologic: catheter specimen culture ≥100,000 cfu/mL of any organism(s)
- [P1116] **REGLA** — IDSA recommends changing indwelling catheter before collecting urine specimen for C&S
- [P1117] **REGLA** — For catheterized resident with urosepsis (fever, chills, hypotension, delirium), obtain blood cultures; treat with broad-spectrum antibiotic while awaiting results
- [P1118] **HECHO** — Within 1 month of inserting indwelling catheter, 75-95% chance of developing bacteriuria
- [P1119] **HECHO** — Bacteriuria risk 18-40% 1 month after suprapubic catheter replacement
- [P1120] **HECHO** — Condom catheter reduces bacteriuria risk to 12%
- [P1121] **HECHO** — Bacteriuria risk lowest with proper hygiene and self-catheterization: 1-3% in 1 month

### Nursing Home Acquired Pneumonia

- [P1122] **HECHO** — Incidence rates of pneumonia in NF: 0.3-2.5 infections/1000 resident care-days
- [P1123] **HECHO** — Some residents inappropriately diagnosed with bacterial pneumonia when they have aspiration (chemical) pneumonitis; antibiotics often not indicated for aspiration pneumonitis
- [P1124] **HECHO** — Tenfold increase in pneumonia incidence in LTC residents vs age-matched community dwellers
- [P1125] **HECHO** — Increased bacterial load in mouth due to poor oral care increases pneumonia risk
- [P1126] **REGLA** — SHEA pneumonia criteria for LTCF: all 3 must be met: (1) chest X-ray showing pneumonia/new infiltrate, (2) ≥1 respiratory subcriteria, (3) ≥1 constitutional criteria
- [P1127] **REGLA** — Respiratory subcriteria: new/increased cough, new/increased sputum, O2 sat <94% on room air or ≥3% reduction from baseline, new/changed lung exam, pleuritic chest pain, respiratory rate ≥25/min
- [P1128] **REGLA** — Constitutional criteria: fever (single oral >37.8 C or repeated oral >37.2 C or rectal >37.5 C or single temp >1.1 C above baseline) OR leukocytosis (>14,000/mm3 or ≥6% bands or ≥1500 bands/mm3) OR acute mental status change from baseline (CAM criteria) OR acute functional decline (new 3-point increase in ADL score range 0-28)
- [P1129] **REGLA** — CAM criteria for delirium: acute onset + fluctuating course + inattention + (disorganized thinking OR altered consciousness)
- [P1130] **DEFINICIÓN** — Acute functional decline = new 3-point increase in total ADL score (0-28) based on 6 items each scored 0-4: bed mobility, transfer, locomotion within LTCF, dressing, toilet use, personal hygiene
- [P1131] **HECHO** — Undesirable risks of hospitalization for pneumonia: pressure ulcers, colonization with virulent/drug-resistant organisms, delirium (especially with dementia), difficult behaviors
- [P1132] **HECHO** — Risk factors for hospital readmission/mortality after pneumonia admission: higher number comorbidities, increased number medications, prior admissions to healthcare facilities

### Physician Order for Life Sustaining Treatment (POLST) and Advance Directives

- [P1133] **HECHO** — During crisis, 3 fundamental patient questions: "What is happening and why?", "What does this mean for my future?", "What can be done and how will it change my future?"
- [P1134] **DEFINICIÓN** — Serious Illness Conversation Guide (SICG) developed by Ariadne Labs; helps clinicians discuss values/goals/preferences
- [P1135] **HECHO** — SICG shown in multiple clinical trials to result in more/better/earlier serious illness conversations, positive patient impact, cost reductions in last 6 months of life
- [P1136] **HECHO** — POLST adopted by hospital systems, EMS, and NFs in many US states; portable document communicating medical care wishes for seriously ill patients
- [P1137] **OBLIGACIÓN** — PCP must have conversation with patient/POA upon every admission to NF/SNF
- [P1138] **HECHO** — Most families desire highest quality of life for ill member and have less interest in maintaining life as long as possible
- [P1139] **REQUISITO** — Newly admitted residents should be seen within 2-5 days of admission or within 12-24h when significant change in condition occurs
- [P1140] **REQUISITO** — Goals of care should be discussed during every care transition (e.g., using SICG)
- [P1141] **REQUISITO** — NF/SNF should have effective antibiotic stewardship program using updated LTCF criteria by SHEA

### Summary

- [P1142] **REGLA** — When resident with memory loss shows increased behavioral problems without meeting UTI criteria, only 11% will have UTI
- [P1143] **REGLA** — Do not treat residents with urinary catheters for UTI unless they meet SHEA criteria for UTI/urosepsis
- [P1144] **REGLA** — If urosepsis suspected, draw blood cultures before initiating broad-spectrum antibiotics
- [P1145] **REGLA** — Aspiration pneumonitis without pneumonia does not require antibiotic treatment

## Goals of Care and Prevention


### Introduction

- [P1146] **HECHO** — One of 4 components of age-friendly care (IHI) = asking patient "what matters most" in day-to-day life
- [P1147] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Documenting practitioner discussion on goals of care and determining patient capacity for informed decisions is vital

### Goals of Care

- [P1148] **HECHO** — No nationwide or state database exists for persons to document care wishes/preferences
- [P1149] **HECHO** — Many states use POLST or MOLST to document patient wishes on CPR, airway intubation, feeding tubes, IV therapy, antibiotics
- [P1150] **HECHO** — VA uses "life sustaining treatment orders" to document veteran wishes
- [P1151] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Practitioners/staff of NF/assisted living should meet with patients and family to review expectations on admission and establish overall goals of care
- [P1152] **HECHO** — Goals of care frequently change over illness trajectory; changes must be acknowledged and incorporated into plan of care
- [P1153] **HECHO** — COVID-19 pandemic created urgent need for more advance care planning discussions
- [P1154] **DEFINICIÓN** — Age-friendly care "4Ms" (variant naming): Mobility, Mentation, Medication, "what Matters" to patient

### Fall Prevention

- [P1155] **HECHO** — 2010 AGS Clinical Practice Guideline: "Prevention of Falls in Older Persons" provides screening/assessment recommendations
- [P1156] **REQUISITO** — All patients should be questioned about fall history and circumstances
- [P1157] **REQUISITO** — Multifactorial fall risk assessment required on NF/assisted living admission including physical exam with gait/balance evaluation
- [P1158] **HECHO** — Fall risk factors: diabetic neuropathy, urinary urgency, antihypertensive medication, depression, psychotropic medication, foot deformities, orthostasis, muscle weakness, vision impairment, cognitive impairment, delirium, vestibular dysfunction
- [P1159] **HECHO** — Comorbidities increasing fall risk: Parkinson's, anemia, osteoarthritis, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, cardiovascular disease, bladder incontinence, dementia, chronic pain
- [P1160] **HECHO** — CDC 2018: 27.5% of people >65 reported ≥1 fall in past year; 10.2% reported fall-related injury
- [P1161] **HECHO** — 21% of newly admitted NF residents had fallen within 30 days of admission
- [P1162] **REGLA** — After fall, huddle should occur reviewing possible contributing factors; multicomponent intervention by multidisciplinary team may reduce falls in LTC (per AMDA guideline)
- [P1163] **HECHO** — Useful fall assessment tests: "get up and go", functional reach test, Berg balance test, POMA (performance oriented mobility assessment)
- [P1164] **REGLA** — Exercise programs (yoga, Tai Chi, weight-bearing) recommended to maintain strength/mobility and decrease fall risk
- [P1165] **REGLA** — Supplement residents with ≥800 IU vitamin D daily for those with vitamin D deficiency and/or impaired balance; patients with vitamin D <20 ng/mL may have decreased fall risk with supplementation
- [P1166] **REGLA** — Consider dose reduction of medications causing orthostasis or increasing fall risk: psychotropics (especially benzodiazepines, sedatives, anxiolytics), multiple antihypertensives, opioids
- [P1167] **REGLA** — Consider osteoporosis treatment in residents tolerating pharmacologic therapy with life expectancy 5-10 years, particularly those with prior wrist/vertebral/hip fracture
- [P1168] **HECHO** — Bed/chair alarms do not prevent falls; only alert staff. Most NFs now alarm-free
- [P1169] **REGLA** — Restraints (chemical and physical) should be avoided at all times; they neither prevent falls nor injury and instead increase falls/injuries
- [P1170] **HECHO** — Double side rails considered a restraint; side rail injuries linked to older beds allowing entrapment
- [P1171] **HECHO** — Efficacy of hip protectors controversial; limited evidence of effectiveness

### Immunizations

- [P1172] **HECHO** — Vaccines recommended for elderly: influenza, pneumococcal, Shingrix (herpes zoster), Tdap, COVID-19
- [P1173] **HECHO** — Staff vaccination rates of 60-70% may decrease likelihood of facility outbreak among patients and staff

### Influenza

- [P1174] **DEFINICIÓN** — NF influenza outbreak = 2 residents sick with flu-like symptoms within 72h AND 1 confirmed by viral testing
- [P1175] **REGLA** — When outbreak occurs, all residents should be treated/prophylaxed with oseltamivir or zanamivir for 5 days
- [P1176] **REGLA** — During outbreak: limit transit between units; standard/droplet precautions for suspected/confirmed influenza; dining/activities may need to occur in patient rooms
- [P1177] **HECHO** — Amantadine and rimantadine no longer effective due to resistance development; should not be used for treatment or prophylaxis

### Pneumococcus

- [P1178] **HECHO** — Common pneumococcal disease presentations in older adults: pneumonia, sepsis, meningitis
- [P1179] **REGLA** — Adults 19-64 at higher risk: receive PCV 20 or PCV 15 followed by PPS 23 in 1 year
- [P1180] **REGLA** — All patients >65 recommended pneumococcal vaccination: PCV 15 followed by PPS 23 in 1 year, OR one-time dose PCV 20

### Shingles (Herpes Zoster)

- [P1181] **HECHO** — Shingrix prevents 90% herpes zoster and 89% post-herpetic neuralgia; effective ≥4 years; approved 2017
- [P1182] **REGLA** — Shingrix = 2-dose series; second dose 2 months after first
- [P1183] **HECHO** — Tdap given IM every 10 years; prevents tetanus and pertussis

### COVID-19 Vaccination

- [P1184] **HECHO** — COVID-19 vaccines (approved 2020) effective preventing infection in NF/assisted living residents and reducing illness severity; ongoing boosters needed

### Nutrition and Vitamin and Mineral Supplementation

- [P1185] **HECHO** — Being underweight increases risk: infections, pressure ulcers, physical decline, cognitive decline, death
- [P1186] **HECHO** — Being overweight increases risk: metabolic syndrome, hypertension, diabetes, increased need for ADL assistance
- [P1187] **HECHO** — Prevalence of malnutrition in NF ≈20%
- [P1188] **REQUISITO** — Nutritional/dietary plan required for all residents to ensure nutrient/calorie needs met
- [P1189] **PERMISO** — Resident rights allow patients to decline prescribed diet and eat what they choose even if it compromises medical condition
- [P1190] **HECHO** — Choosing Wisely guidelines (AGS/AMDA) strongly advise against feeding tube insertion in dementia patients
- [P1191] **HECHO** — Most common electrolyte abnormalities in NF residents: hypokalemia (diuretics) and sodium abnormalities (fluid balance)
- [P1192] **HECHO** — Many residents deficient in vitamin D; associated with falls, fractures, musculoskeletal pain, muscle weakness
- [P1193] **REGLA** — Optimal 25-hydroxyvitamin D level ≥40-50; supplementation range 800-4000 IU/day
- [P1194] **HECHO** — Highest risk vitamin D deficiency: dark pigmented skin, obesity, malabsorption, medications accelerating vitamin D breakdown (phenytoin, phenobarbital) or binding it (cholestyramine)
- [P1195] **REGLA** — Consider calcium supplement 1000-1200 mg daily with vitamin D if dietary intake poor and consistent with goals of care; note calcium can be constipating
- [P1196] **HECHO** — Magnesium often deficient in residents on diuretics or PPIs for extended periods and in diabetes mellitus
- [P1197] **HECHO** — Oral zinc replacement beneficial in patients with zinc deficiency and wound; correlation between adequate zinc levels and cognition/depression

### Screening for Osteoporosis

- [P1198] **HECHO** — USPSTF has not made specific recommendation for osteoporosis screening in NFs; recommends screening women >65; inadequate evidence for men
- [P1199] **HECHO** — National Osteoporosis Foundation recommends screening women >65 and men >70
- [P1200] **HECHO** — Screening options: DEXA (lumbar spine/hip), quantitative ultrasound (calcaneum), FRAX risk calculation
- [P1201] **HECHO** — Osteoporotic fracture or DEXA T-score > -2.5 [sic, should be < -2.5 per convention] may warrant treatment
- [P1202] **TENSIÓN** — Bisphosphonates may be appropriate but burdensome; adverse effects include GERD, osteonecrosis, atypical fractures; lag effect means treatment not prioritized at end of life

### Tuberculosis Screening

- [P1203] **REGLA** — TB screening recommended on admission to NF; initial testing = two-step TST or IGRA blood test
- [P1204] **HECHO** — Many older people anergic (immunosenescence) → false negative skin testing
- [P1205] **REGLA** — No longer recommended to do annual TB skin test or IGRA if facility TB incidence is low; many NFs use annual symptom screen instead

### Cancer Screening

- [P1206] **REGLA** — Cervical cancer screening rarely if ever needed in NF residents
- [P1207] **REGLA** — Colon cancer screening not recommended >75 by USPSTF; only if patient willing/able to undergo major surgery
- [P1208] **REGLA** — Lung cancer screening with low-dose CT limited to patients <75 with ≥40 pack-years smoking willing/able to undergo treatment; intended for community-dwelling healthy patients, not frail NF elderly
- [P1209] **REGLA** — Prostate cancer screening with PSA NOT recommended for most male NF residents; no mortality benefit, may increase morbidity/mortality from workup/treatment
- [P1210] **REGLA** — AMDA Choosing Wisely: do not screen for breast/colorectal/prostate cancer unless life expectancy >10 years; for most LTC residents >75, screening burdens likely outweigh benefits

### Exercise

- [P1211] **HECHO** — Exercise has positive effects on mood and helps maintain function in Alzheimer's patients
- [P1212] **HECHO** — Best exercise benefits: walking + strength training + balance + flexibility
- [P1213] **HECHO** — Yoga, Tai Chi, stretching adapted for chairbound; exercise with music effective in Parkinson's disease
- [P1214] **REGLA** — Liberal diets appropriate for most frail older patients; restrictive diets should be avoided unless medically necessary

## Integrating Palliative Care into Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P1215] **HECHO** — CAPC reports up to 80% of NF residents could benefit from palliative care
- [P1216] **REQUISITO** — As many facilities lack on-site specialist palliative care consultation, practitioners must acquire competency to integrate palliative care into daily practice
- [P1217] **HECHO** — Core palliative care challenge areas: advance care planning (prognostication/values clarification), symptom management, psychosocial support (Kristler, Sloane, Zimmerman)
- [P1218] **REGLA** — Palliative care can supplement traditional medical care; disease-modifying treatments not necessarily abandoned if congruent with patient values/preferences/goals
- [P1219] **HECHO** — F-Tag 684 = Quality of Care (hospice/palliative/terminally ill); F-Tag 697 = Pain Management; F-Tag 849 = Hospice Services; F-Tag 744 = Care of residents with dementia; F-Tag 757 = Unnecessary Drug Use
- [P1220] **DEFINICIÓN** — CMS Quadruple Aim: improve health of patient populations, enhance patient/family experience, reduce healthcare costs, improve job satisfaction

### Guiding Principles of Palliative Care

- [P1221] **DEFINICIÓN** — Core principles of palliative care: reduce symptom burden/suffering; address physical/psycho-emotional/social/spiritual dimensions; provide goal-congruent medical treatment; promote interdisciplinary care; assist with ethical/legal issues
- [P1222] **DEFINICIÓN** — Primary palliative care goals for patient/family: highest practical quality of life, autonomy/dignity, relieve suffering, alleviate family burden, mend estranged relationships

### Illness Trajectories and Prognostication

- [P1223] **HECHO** — Leading causes of death in US: heart disease, cancer, stroke, lung disease, Alzheimer's, influenza/pneumonia, kidney disease, unintentional injuries, diabetes, septicemia, COVID-19
- [P1224] **HECHO** — Common NF death causes: 30-60% cardiovascular, 1-23% pulmonary, 36% Alzheimer's/dementia (10% of all dementia deaths in NFs), 7-9% cancer, 2-3% ESRD
- [P1225] **HECHO** — As of June 2021: CMS reported 657,457 resident COVID-19 confirmed cases and 133,210 resident deaths in NFs; 586,899 staff confirmed cases and 1,949 staff deaths
- [P1226] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 illness trajectories leading to death: (1) short rapid decline weeks/months (cancer), (2) prolonged dwindling over years (dementia/Parkinson's/MS/ALS), (3) progressive functional decline 2-5 years with intermittent acute episodes/partial recovery (cardiac/pulmonary), (4) acute catastrophic event death within 7-14 days (stroke/MI/organ failure)
- [P1227] **HECHO** — Flacker Mortality Score provides % probability of death in 1 year in newly admitted NF residents ≥65
- [P1228] **HECHO** — MDS-based prognostic characteristics: significant weight loss, swallowing problems, BMI <22, age >88, CHF, shortness of breath, male sex
- [P1229] **HECHO** — Palliative Prognostic Score may predict 30-day survival; delirium = significant prognostic factor
- [P1230] **HECHO** — Symptom prevalence in advanced illness: fatigue 72-74% cancer/76% non-cancer; pain 71-74% cancer/67% non-cancer; breathlessness 36% cancer and non-cancer/60-95% COPD/heart/renal; anxiety/depression 40% cancer/57% non-cancer

### General Indicators and Clinical Parameters of Serious Illness

- [P1231] **HECHO** — Indicators patient may die within 6-12 months: repeated ER visits/frequent hospitalizations, recurrent life-threatening infections, PPS <50-70% or increasing dependency in ≥2 BADLs
- [P1232] **HECHO** — Additional indicators: progressive unavoidable weight loss 5% in 3 months or 10% in 6 months, serum albumin <2.5 gm/dL, ≥1 stage 3/4 pressure ulcers, BMI <22 (especially <20), age >88, severe refractory symptoms (fatigue, dyspnea, peripheral edema, bowel obstruction)
- [P1233] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Important to determine patient/surrogate decision-making capacity for healthcare, practical, and financial matters
- [P1234] **HECHO** — Advanced healthcare directives may be state-specific and may not apply in state where patient now resides
- [P1235] **DEFINICIÓN** — Ethical principles of healthcare: autonomy (self-determination), beneficence (do good), nonmaleficence (avoid harm), justice (treat fairly/equally/equitably), fidelity (truthfulness, dignity, non-abandonment)

### Symptom Assessment and Management

- [P1236] **HECHO** — Non-pain symptoms often more prevalent than pain even in cancer patients
- [P1237] **REQUISITO** — Symptom assessment steps: establish intensity/temporal pattern/exacerbating-relieving factors/location/effect on function; determine acute/chronic/intermittent; identify associated symptoms; assess QOL/ADL/cognition/sleep/mood impact; review treatments; focused physical exam; ascertain pathophysiology; check if medication-caused; identify reversible causes; least invasive testing; evaluate psycho-emotional/spiritual/social factors; initiate treatment based on illness phase/prognosis/preferences
- [P1238] **HECHO** — Assessment scales by symptom: Anorexia=FAACT; Anxiety=HAM-A; Cognition=MMSE/COGNISTAT/MOCA; Constipation=Modified Constipation Assessment Scale; Delirium=CAM/DRS; Depression=Beck/GDS/Zung/Cornell/CES-D; Dyspnea=NAS/VAS; Pain=NAS/VAS/Wong-Baker/FLACC/BPI/PAINAD; Spiritual=FICA/Herth Hope Index
- [P1239] **REGLA** — Interventions to relieve symptoms can lead to unintended consequences (double effect); reasonable to continue first-line treatment while initiating treatment for adverse effects
- [P1240] **REGLA** — Palliative sedation indicated for imminently dying patients with intractable suffering unresponsive to other palliative interventions and intolerable to patient (NHPCO 2010)
- [P1241] **HECHO** — ESAS (Edmonton Symptom Assessment Survey) assesses presence/intensity of multiple symptoms tracked over time

### Anorexia/Cachexia

- [P1242] **DEFINICIÓN** — Anorexia = lack of appetite; cachexia = catabolic state with severe muscle loss ± loss of adipose tissue; occur in late-stage progressive illness
- [P1243] **REGLA** — Appetite stimulants: corticosteroid (prednisone 5-20 mg/d or dexamethasone 4-8 mg/d), progestin (megestrol max 400-800 mg/d, trial 4-8 weeks), mirtazapine (off-label, also mood enhancer, give at bedtime)
- [P1244] **HECHO** — Corticosteroid appetite effect often decreases after several weeks; megestrol can cause lower limb edema and increase thrombophlebitis/thromboembolism risk
- [P1245] **HECHO** — Insufficient evidence to recommend cannabinoids (dronabinol), cyproheptadine, oxandrolone, or thalidomide for anorexia
- [P1246] **REGLA** — Prefer lactose-free nutritional supplements especially in elderly/African Americans (higher lactose intolerance prevalence)

### Dyspnea

- [P1247] **DEFINICIÓN** — Dyspnea = discomfort in breathing including breathlessness/shortness of breath/increased work of breathing; often not associated with tachypnea or hypoxemia
- [P1248] **REGLA** — First-line pharmacotherapy for dyspnea palliation: immediate-release opioid q3-4h; opioid-naive start morphine 2.5-5 mg PO or OME equivalent; titrate upward 25-50% q12-24h
- [P1249] **DEFINICIÓN** — Opioid-tolerant patient = taking consecutive oral daily MME ≥60 mg for 7-10 days
- [P1250] **REGLA** — For excessive oral/respiratory secretions: hyoscyamine PO/SL, glycopyrrolate PO, atropine ophthalmic SL, scopolamine SL or patch q3 days; guaifenesin for thick secretions
- [P1251] **REGLA** — Consider low-dose benzodiazepine (preferably lorazepam) for breakthrough/refractory dyspnea as anxiety often contributing factor
- [P1252] **HECHO** — Scheduled opioid (± benzodiazepine) better than PRN for dyspnea; opioids effective for COPD dyspnea but may be less effective for cancer/heart failure dyspnea
- [P1253] **REGLA** — When using opioid for dyspnea, aim for respiratory rate 14-20
- [P1254] **HECHO** — Oxygen may reverse hypoxemia but dyspnea may not improve; potential for oxygen to cause hypercapnia/obtundation/respiratory arrest

### Nausea and Vomiting

- [P1255] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 main pathophysiologic mechanisms of emetic reflex: cortical, vestibular, chemoreceptor trigger zone, gastrointestinal → `Table 3`
- [P1256] **REGLA** — Cortical N/V: tumor/increased ICP → dexamethasone; anxiety → benzodiazepine; pain → opioid/adjuvants
- [P1257] **REGLA** — Vestibular N/V → meclizine, scopolamine, dimenhydrinate
- [P1258] **REGLA** — CTZ-mediated N/V: medications → decrease/stop; metabolic (kidney/liver failure) → haloperidol/olanzapine; hyponatremia → NaCl/demeclocycline; hypercalcemia → bisphosphonate/dexamethasone
- [P1259] **REGLA** — GI N/V: drug-related → stop drug/consider PPI; tumor → promethazine/metoclopramide/octreotide; constipation → bowel regimen; cough-induced → opioid/anticholinergic
- [P1260] **HECHO** — Metoclopramide can cause EPS, dystonia, tardive dyskinesia
- [P1261] **REGLA** — Low-dose haloperidol (0.5-2 mg) or olanzapine (2.5-7.5 mg) may alleviate N/V
- [P1262] **HECHO** — Dronabinol antiemetic: start 2.5 mg BID, max 20 mg/day; FDA-approved for refractory chemo N/V; adverse effects include somnolence, paranoia, delirium

### Constipation

- [P1263] **HECHO** — Constipating drugs include opioids, anticholinergics, iron, calcium, calcium channel blockers, diuretics, clonidine
- [P1264] **REGLA** — Stepwise constipation regimen ("laxative ladder"): (1) senna ± docusate 1-2 tabs qd-bid, (2) senna 3-4 tabs bid, (3) add sorbitol/lactulose 30cc qd-bid or PEG 17g in 8oz water qd-bid, (4) glycerin ± bisacodyl suppository qd-qod, (5) mineral oil or soap suds enema, (6) digital dis-impaction for rectal impaction
- [P1265] **REGLA** — Stimulant laxatives (senna) most effective for opioid-induced constipation; as opioid dose increases, laxative regimen must also increase
- [P1266] **REGLA** — Bulk-forming laxatives NOT recommended; can exacerbate constipation in underhydrated/less mobile patients
- [P1267] **HECHO** — Rectal fecal impaction can cause "paradoxical" diarrhea or urinary retention
- [P1268] **REGLA** — Life-threatening causes to identify: malignant bowel obstruction, narcotic bowel syndrome

### Delirium

- [P1269] **DEFINICIÓN** — Delirium = acute confusional state with fluctuating course, inattention, disorganized thinking/speech; can be hyperactive, hypoactive, or mixed
- [P1270] **REGLA** — Any acute illness or any medication (regardless of when started) can precipitate delirium; gradual dose reduction/discontinuation of opioid/benzodiazepine/alcohol can also precipitate delirium
- [P1271] **REGLA** — First-line delirium medication: low-dose haloperidol (≤2-3 mg total/day) in divided doses; start low, go slow
- [P1272] **REGLA** — Second-line: low-dose lorazepam 0.5-1 mg PO/SL q6-8h; or valproic acid 125-250 mg q6-12h
- [P1273] **HECHO** — Haloperidol and lorazepam can cause paradoxical agitation → decrease (not increase) dose or discontinue
- [P1274] **HECHO** — Antipsychotics in dementia patients: increased sensitivity to adverse effects (sedation, EPS); "black box" warning = increased risk sudden death and cerebrovascular events

### Fatigue/Weakness/Lack of Energy

- [P1275] **HECHO** — Primary fatigue pathophysiology: reticular formation dysfunction, proinflammatory cytokines, HPA axis deregulation, skeletal muscle metabolism alteration
- [P1276] **HECHO** — Secondary fatigue causes: anemia, infection/fever, dehydration/electrolytes, hypothyroidism, vitamin D/B12 deficiency, depression, poor sleep, chronic pain, medication side effects, chemo/radiation
- [P1277] **REGLA** — Methylphenidate for fatigue: start 2.5 mg BID (early + late morning); may increase to 5 mg BID max; discontinue if no improvement in 1-2 weeks; use caution in heart disease/hypertension
- [P1278] **REGLA** — Corticosteroids for fatigue: prednisone 5-10 mg/d or dexamethasone 8-12 mg/d divided; beware confusion/delirium risk especially in elderly

### Depression/Anxiety

- [P1279] **HECHO** — Factors contributing to EOL depression/anxiety: symptom burden, loss of connectedness, loss of dignity, search for meaning, forgiveness, legacy
- [P1280] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Imperative to address severity of depression, suicidal ideation, and access to means; remove accessibility; psychiatric consultation may be necessary
- [P1281] **REGLA** — Residents with prognosis <6 months: psychostimulants may be preferred as first-line depression treatment over SSRIs
- [P1282] **REGLA** — SSRI discontinuation syndrome (headache, malaise, flu-like, agitation, anxiety, insomnia) most likely with paroxetine (short half-life); no need to taper fluoxetine (long half-life); anticipate at EOL when swallowing ceases

### Pain Management

- [P1283] **DEFINICIÓN** — Goals of pain control: relieve pain, alleviate suffering, prevent/minimize disability, optimize function, preserve decision-making capacity
- [P1284] **DEFINICIÓN** — "Total pain" = physical + psycho-emotional + social + spiritual aspects of pain
- [P1285] **DEFINICIÓN** — Pain classification: nociceptive (somatic=sharp/tender, visceral=dull/cramping, bone=throbbing/aching), neuropathic (burning/tingling/stabbing/shooting), inflammatory (pleuritic/rebound/inflamed joints)
- [P1286] **REGLA** — WHO 3-step analgesic ladder: Step 1 (mild) = ASA/acetaminophen/NSAIDs; Step 2 (moderate) = acetaminophen+codeine/hydrocodone/oxycodone, tramadol; Step 3 (severe) = morphine/hydromorphone/methadone/levorphanol/fentanyl/oxycodone; all ± adjuvants
- [P1287] **REGLA** — Analgesic administration principles: by mouth (oral preferred; avoid IM), around-the-clock (scheduled for continuous pain), according to ladder (severity-based), adapted to individual, with attention to detail
- [P1288] **HECHO** — In up to 90% of persons with pain, practitioners can adequately control pain with oral medication guided by WHO ladder
- [P1289] **REGLA** — Max acetaminophen: 3000-4000 mg/day; 2000 mg/day if renal/hepatic insufficiency or older adults
- [P1290] **REGLA** — Max tramadol: 300 mg/24h; may cause confusion, seizures, serotonin syndrome; lower dose in older adults
- [P1291] **REGLA** — Conventional NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) only short-term (days to ≤3-4 weeks); risks: GI bleeding, renal impairment, platelet dysfunction, edema, increased BP, worsened heart failure
- [P1292] **REGLA** — Never prescribe NSAID when patient taking warfarin; high GI bleed risk
- [P1293] **REGLA** — Lidocaine patch: apply 12h on / 12h off; no more than 3 patches at once
- [P1294] **REGLA** — Avoid meperidine: CNS toxicity risk from toxic metabolite normeperidine causing confusion/seizures
- [P1295] **REGLA** — Opioid partial agonists (butorphanol, pentazocine, buprenorphine, nalbuphine) NOT recommended: analgesic ceiling effects and can counteract pure agonist opioids → withdrawal pain crisis
- [P1296] **REGLA** — First-line analgesic by pain type: nociceptive = WHO Step 1-2; neuropathic = TCAs/anticonvulsants; bone = NSAIDs/corticosteroids; intracranial = corticosteroids; visceral = anticholinergic/opioid

### Opioid Analgesics

- [P1297] **HECHO** — Opioids appropriate for moderate-severe acute/chronic pain; judicious prescribing = low risk psychologic dependency/addiction in PALTC
- [P1298] **REGLA** — Acute pain: start immediate-release (IR) opioid; chronic pain: start low-dose sustained-release (LA/ER) with IR for breakthrough, OR start IR then convert to equianalgesic sustained-release
- [P1299] **HECHO** — IR morphine duration of analgesia 3-4h for all routes (PO, SL, SC, IV); peak analgesic effect: oral/SL 45-60 min, SC 15-30 min, IV 5-15 min
- [P1300] **REGLA** — Breakthrough pain dose = 10-15% total daily opioid dose q3-4h; for severe pain crisis can give q1-2h
- [P1301] **REGLA** — Opioid uptitration: 25-50% for mild-moderate pain; 50-100% for moderate-severe pain in opioid-tolerant patient
- [P1302] **REGLA** — ALWAYS start bowel regimen when starting opioid; stimulant/osmotic agent preferred
- [P1303] **HECHO** — Common opioid side effects: constipation (almost always), somnolence, N/V, dizziness, sweating, dry mouth. Less common: hypotension, urinary retention, confusion/delirium, bradycardia, seizures, respiratory depression, paralytic ileus
- [P1304] **REGLA** — NOT recommended to use ≥3 different opioids due to potential adverse drug-drug/opioid-receptor interactions
- [P1305] **HECHO** — Oral MME conversion table: morphine 30/10, oxycodone 20-30/N/A, oxymorphone 10/1, hydromorphone 7.5/1.5, meperidine 300/100, hydrocodone 30/N/A, codeine 200/100; fentanyl 25mcg patch = 50mg oral morphine/24h
- [P1306] **REGLA** — When rotating opioids, decrease new opioid total daily dose by 25-50% for incomplete cross-tolerance; convert over 2-3 days (downtitrate old + uptitrate new)
- [P1307] **REGLA** — Opioid-naive frail/elderly starting doses: morphine 2mg PO/SL, oxycodone 2.5mg, hydromorphone 0.5mg PO, hydrocodone 2.5mg PO
- [P1308] **REGLA** — Fentanyl patch NOT advised for initiating opioid treatment in frail elderly opioid-naive; 12mcg/h ≈25mg OME, 25mcg/h ≈50mg OME per 24h → excess sedation/fall risk; ineffective in thin patients <105 lbs (inadequate subcutaneous fat)
- [P1309] **REGLA** — Morphine/oxycodone/hydromorphone: use cautiously if GFR 30-50 mL/min or less (neurotoxic metabolite accumulation)
- [P1310] **REGLA** — Methadone and fentanyl safe in advanced renal failure/dialysis (not dialyzable); acetaminophen and tramadol (max 50-100mg BID) also safe in renal failure
- [P1311] **REGLA** — Morphine/codeine doses may need reduction in liver disease/cirrhosis; fentanyl may be optimal unless inadequate subcutaneous tissue
- [P1312] **HECHO** — Methadone: multiple mechanisms of action, hepatic metabolism, minimal renal excretion, cheap; half-life 45-180h; steady state ≈10 days; associated with QT prolongation and opioid-related deaths
- [P1313] **REGLA** — Methadone should only be used by/in consultation with experienced physician; frail elders start 2.5-5mg BID-TID; never use for acute/breakthrough pain (high respiratory depression risk)
- [P1314] **REGLA** — Transmucosal oral fentanyl (Actiq) only for severe breakthrough cancer pain; avoid in PALTC setting

### Eligibility Guidelines for Hospice

- [P1315] **HECHO** — Hospice underutilized; 35% of hospice admissions = stays ≤7 days; long stays >180 days = 10% of admissions
- [P1316] **REGLA** — OIG periodically audits hospice agencies with high number of long-stay patients for continued eligibility
- [P1317] **REGLA** — Hospice certification requires 2 physicians (usually hospice medical director + attending) certifying prognosis ≤6 months if illness follows natural progression
- [P1318] **REGLA** — MHB benefit periods: first 2 periods = 90 days each; subsequent periods = 60 days, unlimited; recertification requires narrative by hospice medical director
- [P1319] **REGLA** — After 6 months, every 60-day recertification requires face-to-face visit by hospice medical director or contracted NP
- [P1320] **PERMISO** — Patient can revoke hospice benefit at any time to seek life-sustaining/curative treatment; can be readmitted to hospice if guidelines still met
- [P1321] **REGLA** — Cancer hospice criteria: widespread metastasis, PPS ≤70%, no longer seeking curative care
- [P1322] **REGLA** — Dementia hospice criteria: inability to ambulate (FAST 7c), no consistent meaningful speech, life-threatening infections or multiple stage 3/4 ulcers, inability to maintain fluid/calorie intake
- [P1323] **REGLA** — Heart disease hospice criteria: poor response/intolerant to optimal treatment, NYHA Class IV CHF, EF ≤20% (helpful not required), cardiac-related syncope
- [P1324] **REGLA** — HIV/AIDS hospice criteria: CD4 <25, persistent viral load >100,000/mL, major AIDS-defining refractory infections, significant functional decline in ADLs
- [P1325] **REGLA** — Neurologic disease hospice criteria (PD/ALS/MS/MD/myasthenia gravis): rapid progression + critical nutritional state, life-threatening infections in preceding 12 months, stage 3-4 decubitus, critically impaired breathing + declines ventilator
- [P1326] **REGLA** — Pulmonary disease hospice criteria: disabling dyspnea at rest/minimal exertion, increasing ER visits/hospitalizations, hypoxemia on room air <88%, hypercapnia pCO2 >50mmHg, FEV1 <30% (helpful not required)
- [P1327] **REGLA** — Renal failure hospice criteria: not seeking/candidate for dialysis or refusing further dialysis, CrCl <10 (<15 for diabetics), creatinine >8 (>6 for diabetics)
- [P1328] **REGLA** — Stroke hospice criteria: coma (acute phase), dysphagia with insufficient intake, post-stroke dementia (apply dementia criteria)
- [P1329] **REGLA** — Liver disease hospice criteria: INR >1.5 (not on warfarin), albumin <2.5, refractory ascites, prior spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, hepatorenal syndrome, recurrent variceal bleeding
- [P1330] **HECHO** — General hospice eligibility indicators: frequent ER transfers, more frequent hospitalizations, weight loss 5%/1 month or 10%/6 months, multiple stage 3-4 ulcers, albumin <2.5, recurrent life-threatening infections, KPS <70% or increasing dependency ≥2 BADLs
- [P1331] **DEFINICIÓN** — FAST scale: 1-7 scale for functional staging in dementia; 1=no difficulty, 7=significant loss of speech/ambulation/smile/head control
- [P1332] **HECHO** — "Surprise question" has excellent predictive value: "Would you be surprised if this patient died in next 12 months?" If not surprised → consider hospice
- [P1333] **REGLA** — Diagnoses of failure to thrive, generalized debility, nonspecific terminal illness NO LONGER accepted as terminal condition under MHB
- [P1334] **HECHO** — When disease-specific hospice criteria not fully met, comorbid conditions and/or psychosocial factors may support eligibility

### End of Life Care

- [P1335] **HECHO** — 10% of dying patients may experience agitated delirium during last days of life
- [P1336] **REGLA** — Reduced cardiac output/intravascular depletion → tachycardia, hypotension, peripheral cooling, cyanosis, mottling; urine output diminishes → anuria; neurologic dysfunction → decreased consciousness → coma
- [P1337] **REGLA** — End-of-life practical comfort: periodic repositioning, decrease food/fluid to prevent choking/aspiration, maintain moist oral mucosa, moisture/lubricant to conjunctiva/lips
- [P1338] **REGLA** — Discontinue nonessential drugs at EOL (aspirin, multivitamins, calcium, lipid-lowering); consider risks/benefits of continuing antidepressants, antihypertensives, warfarin, antiarrhythmics, thyroid replacement; reduce/discontinue diuretics, ACE inhibitors, hypoglycemics, insulin
- [P1339] **HECHO** — Reduced hepatic function and renal perfusion can precipitate opioid-induced terminal delirium → reduce opioid dose while ensuring pain control + low-dose antipsychotic
- [P1340] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Last days of life: medication reconciliation essential to avoid polypharmacy as comfort medications administered
- [P1341] **REGLA** — EOL tachypnea/breathlessness: low-dose IR opioid ± benzodiazepine SL/transbuccal
- [P1342] **REGLA** — EOL excessive secretions: anticholinergic SL (hyoscyamine, atropine ophthalmic) or transdermal scopolamine; minimize suction as it paradoxically stimulates more secretions
- [P1343] **REGLA** — EOL pain: concentrated oral morphine (Roxanol 20mg/cc) or oxycodone (Oxyfast 20mg/cc) SL/transbuccal; avoid IM/SC if possible
- [P1344] **REGLA** — EOL anxiety/agitation: benzodiazepine, opioid, or antipsychotic; any can cause paradoxical agitation
- [P1345] **REGLA** — EOL restlessness/delirium: rule out fecal impaction and urinary retention first; then consider antipsychotic ± benzodiazepine
- [P1346] **REGLA** — EOL fever: scheduled acetaminophen PO/PR/PEG if distressing
- [P1347] **HECHO** — F-Tag 757 requires each resident's drug regimen be free from unnecessary drugs; opioid or antipsychotic for anxiety could be construed as unnecessary
- [P1348] **REGLA** — Consider REMS education on opioid analgesic use
- [P1349] **REGLA** — Integrate palliative care into traditional care throughout PALTC continuum irrespective of whether patient continues disease-directed/curative therapies

## Weight and Nutrition in Post-Acute and Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P1350] **HECHO** — Optimal nutrition improves health, function, QoL; reduces risk of osteoporosis, weakness, pressure sores, frailty, sarcopenia, infection
- [P1351] **HECHO** — Weight loss = facility quality measure + risk factor for poor outcomes
- [P1352] **HECHO** — Strong correlation between low BMI, low serum albumin, and increased mortality for months/years after hospital discharge
- [P1353] **HECHO** — Dementia, depression, GI/neurological/musculoskeletal/psychiatric disorders challenge maintaining nutrition/hydration/weight

### Introduction (Table 1 — Recommended intakes)

- [P1354] **REQUISITO** — Recommended caloric intake for older adults: ≥1800 calories/day, varies by body size/activity
- [P1355] **REQUISITO** — Protein RDI: 46 g/day females >18, 56 g/day males >18 (0.36-0.66 g/kg/day); 10-35% of calories
- [P1356] **REQUISITO** — Increased protein 1-1.2 g/kg/day suggested for geriatric LTC patients
- [P1357] **REQUISITO** — Fat intake: 20-35% of calories
- [P1358] **REQUISITO** — Carbohydrate intake: 130 g/day, 45-65% of calories
- [P1359] **REQUISITO** — Water intake: 2.7-3.7 L/day (includes total water, all beverages, average temperate climate)
- [P1360] **HECHO** — Vitamin A: 700-900 mcg/day; higher doses → increased toxicity/mortality
- [P1361] **HECHO** — Vitamin B12: 2.4 mcg/day; common deficiency in elderly; often malabsorbed requiring supplements
- [P1362] **HECHO** — Vitamin C: 75-90 mg; Zinc: 11 mg; larger doses for pressure sores not evidence-based
- [P1363] **REQUISITO** — Vitamin D: 800 IU (20 mcg) for adults >70 (2011 IOM Update)
- [P1364] **HECHO** — Vitamin E: 15 mg (22.5 IU); supplementation >200 IU no longer recommended due to meta-analysis showing increased mortality
- [P1365] **REQUISITO** — Calcium: 1200 mg/day; calcium + vitamin D supplements for all older adults at risk for osteoporosis
- [P1366] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Iron: 8 mg/day; doses >325 mg/day ineffective, cause increased constipation

### Risk Factors

- [P1367] **HECHO** — Older patients experience less hunger than younger patients after underfeeding period
- [P1368] **HECHO** — Inadequate food/fluid intake increases risk of dehydration, constipation, slow gastric emptying, regurgitation, cognitive impairment, aspiration, infection, decubiti, sepsis
- [P1369] **HECHO** — PALTC nutritional risk factors: history weight loss/appetite loss, oral/dental problems, reduced mobility, skin breakdown, depression/dementia/chronic illness, fluid retention/edema/nausea/vomiting/changed bowel habits, multiple medications

### Medication

- [P1370] **HECHO** — Drugs causing GI disturbance/anorexia/weight loss: NSAIDs, opioids, anticholinergics, metformin, digoxin, cholinesterase inhibitors
- [P1371] **HECHO** — TCAs cause worsened cognition, poor oral intake, constipation
- [P1372] **HECHO** — ACE inhibitors and antibiotics may distort normal smell/taste
- [P1373] **HECHO** — PPIs reduce absorption of calcium and vitamin B12
- [P1374] **HECHO** — Opioids and loperamide decrease peristalsis → potential toxic megacolon
- [P1375] **HECHO** — Antidiabetic agents and antipsychotics cause weight gain
- [P1376] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Admitting practitioner must review/verify medication list, discontinue unnecessary medications, ensure safe transition between settings

### Disease

- [P1377] **HECHO** — Disease-related weight/appetite/GI changes: hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism, diabetes, Parkinson's, dementia, depression, alcohol/substance abuse, oral/dental problems, achalasia, malabsorption, peptic ulcers, IBD, scleroderma, CHF, AIDS, cancer, infection
- [P1378] **HECHO** — Admission to LTC facility with 3 meals/day may improve nutrition and reverse weight loss in community-dwelling elderly

### Assessment of Nutritional Status

- [P1379] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Monitoring for significant weight loss required for all LTC patients; part of MDS and CMS Quality Measures
- [P1380] **DEFINICIÓN** — Significant weight loss per OBRA: 5% weight loss over past 30 days OR >10% weight loss over past 6 months
- [P1381] **HECHO** — Brief nutritional assessment tools: Subjective Global Assessment, Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA), MNA-SF (Short Form)
- [P1382] **HECHO** — Weight loss alone = one of best indicators of nutritional compromise; proven risk factor for increased morbidity/mortality
- [P1383] **DEFINICIÓN** — Instant Nutritional Assessment (INA): serum albumin <3.5 mg/dL abnormal; total lymphocyte count <1500/mm3 abnormal
- [P1384] **HECHO** — Low serum albumin = most commonly used indicator of protein malnutrition; correlated with increased mortality in older persons
- [P1385] **HECHO** — Depression may be most common cause of weight loss; one study: depression = 30% of weight loss cases, followed by cancer
- [P1386] **HECHO** — Mirtazapine and nortriptyline increase appetite/weight gain more than other antidepressants
- [P1387] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Antidepressant use solely for appetite/weight gain without depression = not validated

### Weight Management

- [P1388] **HECHO** — Optimal BMI range for older adults including LTC residents: 25 ± 5 (i.e., 20-30)
- [P1389] **HECHO** — Lowest overall mortality rates at "normal" BMI 22.5-25 kg/m2
- [P1390] **HECHO** — Canadian longitudinal study: lowest mortality in moderately overweight (BMI 25-30); underweight (BMI <20) → increased illness/mortality risk
- [P1391] **HECHO** — Patients losing ≥5% body weight = 5-10x more likely to die; even those who regain weight have increased mortality
- [P1392] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Too restrictive diet may decrease QoL and lead to nutritional deficiencies
- [P1393] **HECHO** — Calorie-dense supplements may suppress appetite at meals → failure to gain weight/further weight loss
- [P1394] **HECHO** — AMDA CPG Altered Nutritional Status: >20 steps to evaluate/treat nutritional issues in LTC
- [P1395] **HECHO** — "feed.ME" Global Study Group Nutritional Care Pathway: "screen, intervene, supervene" for all patients in healthcare facilities

### Nutritional Interventions — Fluids

- [P1396] **REQUISITO** — Recommended fluid intake: ~3.7 L/day males, ~2.7 L/day females (temperate climate, includes all beverages/food water content)
- [P1397] **HECHO** — Hypodermoclysis (subcutaneous infusion isotonic fluids) = short-term hydration alternative
- [P1398] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Lab abnormalities of electrolytes/renal function do not necessarily indicate true dehydration
- [P1399] **HECHO** — Dehydration diagnosis = sentinel event by regulatory agencies; should not be diagnosed without caution

### Nutritional Interventions — Supplements

- [P1400] **REGLA** — "Food first" — nutritious food always preferable to any artificial formula/supplement
- [P1401] **REQUISITO** — Increasing daily protein intake to 1-1.2 g/kg/day recommended for older adults to maximize muscle/bone health
- [P1402] **HECHO** — One can Ensure Plus = 350 calories ≈ half average modest meal
- [P1403] **REGLA** — Supplements should be given between meals to minimize appetite suppression
- [P1404] **REQUISITO** — Total daily calcium intake target: 1200-1500 mg for most women and many men
- [P1405] **HECHO** — Calcium citrate preferred over calcium carbonate in elderly due to better absorption with hypochlorhydria
- [P1406] **HECHO** — Three meta-analyses: vitamin D ≥800 IU/day benefits falls, fractures, total mortality
- [P1407] **DEFINICIÓN** — Vitamin D sufficiency defined as ≥30 ng/mL (25-OH-vitamin D)
- [P1408] **TENSIÓN** — Latest studies (2022) questioned value of vitamin D measurement/supplementation

### Appetite Stimulants

- [P1409] **OBLIGACIÓN** — All patients with difficulty eating → evaluation by dietitian + speech therapist for swallowing problems
- [P1410] **HECHO** — Megestrol acetate: expensive (~$500/month), questionable efficacy, safety concerns (fluid retention, edema, DVT risk up to 32%)
- [P1411] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Orexigenic drugs FDA-approved only for AIDS and cancer; off-label in dementia/failure to thrive/endstage disease
- [P1412] **HECHO** — Megestrol and cyproheptadine included in AGS Beers list of potentially inappropriate medication in older adults
- [P1413] **HECHO** — Mirtazapine causes weight gain (1-6 lbs) in depressed patients; little/no evidence effective for weight gain without depression
- [P1414] **HECHO** — Dronabinol (Marinol): cannabinoid, FDA-approved for anorexia/nausea in cancer/AIDS; can cause somnolence, seizures, delirium
- [P1415] **REGLA** — AGS Choosing Wisely: avoid prescription appetite stimulants/high-calorie supplements for anorexia/cachexia in older adults; instead optimize social supports, feeding assistance, clarify patient goals

### Indications and Use (or Not) of Feeding Tubes

- [P1416] **HECHO** — Feeding tube indications: esophageal obstruction, post-op head/neck cancer or GI surgery, severe protein/calorie depletion, moderate-severe weight loss, hypermetabolic state, inability to swallow safely
- [P1417] **HECHO** — Nasoenteric/oroenteric tubes = short-term temporary GI access; gastrostomy/jejunostomy for enteral support >few weeks
- [P1418] **HECHO** — ~5% demented NH residents had feeding tube inserted over 1-year period; median survival 56 days; 1-year post-insertion mortality 64%
- [P1419] **REGLA** — Feeding tube insertion only considered when reasonable expectation of recovery from temporary condition (e.g., acute stroke)
- [P1420] **REGLA** — AMDA + AGS (Choosing Wisely): "Don't insert percutaneous feeding tubes in individuals with advanced dementia; offer oral assisted feedings"
- [P1421] **HECHO** — Standard tube formulas: low residue, 1 kcal/mL, 13-17% calories as protein (e.g., Ensure HN, Osmolite HN)
- [P1422] **HECHO** — High fiber formulas (e.g., Jevity): 10-14 g fiber/L, 1 kcal/mL, 14-18% kcal protein
- [P1423] **HECHO** — High protein formulas (e.g., Replete, Promote): 20-25% calories as protein
- [P1424] **HECHO** — Lower volume formulas (e.g., Two-Cal HN, Magnacal): up to 2 kcal/mL, 14-17% protein, 68-78% water

### Ethical and Legal Issues Related to Feeding

- [P1425] **REGLA** — Withholding/withdrawing natural feedings when futile = ethically no different than not initiating tube feeding
- [P1426] **HECHO** — Tube feedings do not reduce risk of aspiration, pneumonia, pressure sores, infections; decrease QoL due to tube-related complications/mobility restrictions/discomfort
- [P1427] **REGLA** — Provision of food/fluids = basic caring; should never be withheld unless overt aspiration/discomfort
- [P1428] **REGLA** — Artificial tube feeding = medical intervention with risks/benefits; may be refused, not begun, or withdrawn when inappropriate
- [P1429] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Patients/families should provide informed consent under either feeding scenario

### Updated Regulations Related to Nutrition and Tube Feeding

- [P1430] **REGLA** — CMS F692 (Nutrition/Hydration Status): facility must ensure each resident maintains acceptable nutritional parameters unless clinical condition/resident preferences indicate otherwise
- [P1431] **REGLA** — CMS F693 (Tube Feeding Management): resident able to eat shall not be fed enterally unless clinically indicated and consented
- [P1432] **REGLA** — CMS F693: resident fed enterally must receive services to restore oral eating skills and prevent enteral feeding complications
- [P1433] **HECHO** — CMS F-Tags for nutrition: F692 (Nutrition/Hydration), F693 (Tube Feeding), F694 (Parenteral/IV Fluids), F800-814 (Food/Nutrition Services)

### Summary

- [P1434] **HECHO** — Malnutrition/weight loss often natural/inevitable process attributable to advanced age, loss of functioning, underlying multimorbidities
- [P1435] **REGLA** — Restricted diets generally not advisable/tolerated in LTC patients; avoid when not absolutely necessary
- [P1436] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Incorporate patient preferences regarding nutrition/hydration when discussing advance directives and advance care planning

## Wound Assessment and Management


### Introduction

- [P1437] **HECHO** — Four most common wound types in LTC: pressure, diabetic, ischemic/arterial, venous
- [P1438] **HECHO** — Upon NH admission 17-35% patients have pressure injuries; prevalence among NH residents 7-23%
- [P1439] **HECHO** — Among high-risk patients (bedridden/nonambulatory), pressure injury incidence ≈14/1000 patient-days
- [P1440] **HECHO** — CDC/NNHS data: 159,000 NH residents (11%) had pressure injuries; Stage 2 most common
- [P1441] **DEFINICIÓN** — NPUAP changed terminology: "pressure ulcer" → "pressure injury"
- [P1442] **HECHO** — Pressure injury 6-month mortality rate as high as 77.3%
- [P1443] **HECHO** — 55.7% residents who die with pressure injury do so within 6 weeks of onset
- [P1444] **REGLA** — CMS "never events": Stage III and IV pressure injuries acquired during hospitalization → hospitals not paid
- [P1445] **DEFINICIÓN** — "Never Event" term introduced 2001 by Ken Kizer MD, former CEO National Quality Forum
- [P1446] **HECHO** — Average hospital LOS 4-5 days; pressure wound occurrence considered unlikely/"should never occur"
- [P1447] **HECHO** — Wounded skin has ~80% tensile properties of intact skin → increased breakdown risk
- [P1448] **HECHO** — Patients with history of pressure injuries = 5x more likely to develop another
- [P1449] **HECHO** — Pressure injury complications: cellulitis, osteomyelitis, sepsis, limb amputation

### Introduction — Wound care domains

- [P1450] **REGLA** — Wound care guided by 3 domains: (1) Management (risk assessment, care plan, monitoring), (2) Prevention (off-loading, skin protection, nutrition), (3) Treatment (when wet make dry/when dry make wet, treat infections, remove dead tissue)

### Risk Assessment

- [P1451] **HECHO** — Risk factors for wounds: comorbid conditions (diabetes, ESRD, thyroid disease), drugs (steroids, antimetabolites), healed ulcer history, impaired blood flow, decreased mobility, malnutrition/dehydration → `Table 1`
- [P1452] **HECHO** — Among diabetics, 15% develop foot wound; 12-24% of those require amputation; recurrence rate 66%
- [P1453] **HECHO** — Steroids/antimetabolites hinder fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis
- [P1454] **HECHO** — Cognitive impairment: 45-67% assisted living residents, 69% NH residents → increased skin breakdown risk
- [P1455] **HECHO** — Moisture-related skin breakdown from perspiration, wound exudates, urine/fecal incontinence; fecal incontinence especially caustic due to bile acid/GI enzymes
- [P1456] **HECHO** — Nutritional risk factors for wounds: dehydration, arginine deficiency, vitamins A/C/zinc deficiency, protein-calorie malnutrition
- [P1457] **HECHO** — Obesity → skin breakdown risk under pannus/skin folds due to warm moist environment promoting yeast/bacteria
- [P1458] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Initial skin assessment must be performed immediately upon LTC admission; document all Present on Admission (POA) issues
- [P1459] **HECHO** — Pressure ulcer can develop after only a few hours of pressure

### Wound Assessment

- [P1460] **HECHO** — Risk assessment scales: increased awareness but limited predictability; meta-analysis of 33 studies = lack of evidence for decreasing pressure injury incidence, but increased preventive interventions
- [P1461] **DEFINICIÓN** — Braden Scale: 6 categories (sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition, friction/shear); score ≤18 = at risk for pressure sores
- [P1462] **DEFINICIÓN** — Norton Score: 5 categories (physical condition, mental condition, activity, mobility, incontinence)
- [P1463] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Practitioners must document wound number, location, size (L/W/D in cm), exudate, odor, sinus tracts, necrosis/eschar, tunneling/undermining, infection, healing signs, wound margins
- [P1464] **HECHO** — Pressure injury risk significantly high within first 4 weeks after LTC admission
- [P1465] **REGLA** — Weekly reassessment during first 4 weeks; then ≥quarterly + when patient status changes
- [P1466] **REGLA** — Reassess overall clinical condition when pressure injury fails to show healing within 2-4 weeks of intervention
- [P1467] **DEFINICIÓN** — Kennedy terminal ulcers: develop 2-3 days before death over sacrum; start as blister/Stage 2, rapidly progress to Stage 3/4; pear/horseshoe/butterfly shaped; color red/purple → yellow → black; marker of imminent death

### Staging (NPIAP Table 2)

- [P1468] **DEFINICIÓN** — SDTI (Suspected Deep Tissue Injury): pressure-related necrosis soft tissue with intact overlying skin; crimson→purple discoloration
- [P1469] **DEFINICIÓN** — Stage I: localized nonblanchable erythema, skin intact, between bony prominence and external surface
- [P1470] **DEFINICIÓN** — Stage II: exposed dermis; shallow ulcer with crimson wound bed (no slough/bruising) OR intact/ruptured fluid-filled blister
- [P1471] **DEFINICIÓN** — Stage III: full thickness skin loss; adipose visible but bone/tendon/muscle not exposed; tunneling/undermining may occur
- [P1472] **DEFINICIÓN** — Stage IV: full thickness skin + tissue loss; exposed bone/tendon/muscle; rolled edges, undermining/tunneling often occur
- [P1473] **DEFINICIÓN** — Unstageable: full thickness skin/tissue loss cannot be staged until slough/eschar removed
- [P1474] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Do not use Stage II for skin tears, tape burns, dermatitis, maceration, excoriation
- [P1475] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Do not remove eschar present on heels

### Types of Wounds

- [P1476] **HECHO** — 95% wounds develop on lower body; 65% over sacrum/pelvic area; 30% lower extremities
- [P1477] **HECHO** — Three mechanical tissue damage factors: pressure, friction, shear
- [P1478] **DEFINICIÓN** — Pressure injury: localized damaged/necrotic tissue from soft tissue compressed between bony prominence and external surface for prolonged period
- [P1479] **HECHO** — 2009 NPUAP-EPUAP redefined pressure "ulcer" as "compressive tissue injury" caused by pressure alone or pressure + shearing; friction alone ≠ direct cause
- [P1480] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Skin failure at end of life ≠ pressure injury; skin tears, abrasions, lacerations ≠ pressure injuries

### Diabetic Wounds

- [P1481] **HECHO** — Diabetic wounds: commonly over metatarsal heads; due to vascular compromise + peripheral neuropathy; typically painless
- [P1482] **REGLA** — Diabetic wound should be probed with sterile implement to assess depth/undermining/osteomyelitis

### Ischemic Wounds

- [P1483] **HECHO** — Ischemic wounds: typically lower extremities; diabetes/smoking = contributing factors; painful with discrete "punched out" borders or wet/dry gangrene
- [P1484] **HECHO** — Ischemic wound signs: cold/pale/cyanotic foot, absence digital/lower extremity hair, thin atrophic leg skin
- [P1485] **HECHO** — Ischemic ulcer base: dry black/brown eschar or pale pink fibrous

### Venous Wounds

- [P1486] **HECHO** — Venous wounds: lower extremities; caused by peripheral edema/venous insufficiency/stasis; associated with heart/liver/kidney dysfunction
- [P1487] **HECHO** — Venous wounds: less painful than ischemic; irregular borders; hyperpigmented surrounding skin

### Prevention of Wounds

- [P1488] **REGLA** — IHI 6 essential elements pressure injury prevention: (1) conduct assessment on admission, (2) reassess risk daily, (3) inspect skin daily, (4) manage moisture, (5) optimize nutrition/hydration, (6) minimize pressure
- [P1489] **REGLA** — Supine patient: maintain lowest head elevation <30°; elevation ≥30° = as much pressure as seated position
- [P1490] **HECHO** — Repositioning every 4h shown as effective as 2h intervals for wound healing; partial turning does not always remove sacrum/heel pressure
- [P1491] **HECHO** — 2h turning schedule established 1946 on spinal cord injury patients; optimal interval not established
- [P1492] **HECHO** — Study with healthy volunteers: 1-1.5h intervals (not 2h) needed to prevent skin erythema on standard mattress
- [P1493] **REQUISITO** — Nutritionally compromised patients with/at risk of pressure injuries: 30-35 kcal/kg/day caloric intake, 1.2-1.5 g/kg/day protein
- [P1494] **REQUISITO** — Adequate fluid hydration: 30-35 mL fluid/kg/day OR 1 mL fluid/calorie for enteral tube feeding
- [P1495] **HECHO** — Enteral nutritional support can reduce pressure injury risk up to 25% in selected patients
- [P1496] **TENSIÓN** — Benefit of vitamin C supplementation in wound healing disputed; 2 RCTs had contradictory results
- [P1497] **REGLA** — Patient never directly positioned on greater trochanter for more than momentary positioning

### Unavoidable Pressure Ulcers/Injuries

- [P1498] **DEFINICIÓN** — NPUAP criteria for unavoidable pressure injury: (1) patient risk evaluated, (2) interventions consistent with needs/goals/standards implemented, (3) interventions monitored/evaluated/revised
- [P1499] **DEFINICIÓN** — Avoidable pressure injury: facility failed to implement evaluation/intervention/monitoring
- [P1500] **HECHO** — Vasoconstrictive medication for low BP can predispose to skin ischemia
- [P1501] **HECHO** — Advance directive deferring artificial nutrition/hydration → risk for malnutrition/skin ulcer development

### Staging of Pressure Ulcers/Injuries

- [P1502] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Reverse staging shall not be used (e.g., Stage IV cannot become Stage III)
- [P1503] **REGLA** — Healed Stage IV pressure injury reopening at same site = always Stage IV
- [P1504] **REGLA** — Wound covered by eschar → categorized as Stage IV until eschar debrided/self-debrides
- [P1505] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Do not debride heel wounds; exposing calcaneum → osteomyelitis risk
- [P1506] **HECHO** — PUSH tool (Pressure Ulcer Scale for Healing) by NPUAP: evaluates wound surface area, exudate, tissue type in wound bed

### Management/Treatment of Wounds

- [P1507] **REGLA** — 13 principles wound care treatment in LTC: assess risk factors, consider analgesia, clean with saline, debride necrotic tissue, fill dead space, decrease bacterial load, keep wound moist, support healing with adjunctive treatments, relieve pressure, optimize nutrition, manage comorbidities, address complications, track progress
- [P1508] **REGLA** — Saline chosen for wound irrigation; cytotoxic antiseptic agents (Dakin's, iodine, acid/alcohol-based) retard healing
- [P1509] **HECHO** — Five debridement methods: sharp/surgical, autolytic, mechanical, biologic (maggots), enzymatic (collagenase/papain-urea)
- [P1510] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Dry black heel eschar: do not debride unless tender, fluctuant, erythematous, or suppurative
- [P1511] **HECHO** — Major wound dressing categories: films, foams, alginates, hydroactives, hydrogels, hydrocolloids
- [P1512] **HECHO** — VAC (vacuum-assisted closure): closed system using negative pressure to drain wound fluid/approximate edges; may improve healing but not cost-effective
- [P1513] **HECHO** — Topical metronidazole controls wound odor by eradicating anaerobic bacteria

### Complications of Wounds

- [P1514] **HECHO** — NH residents with pressure injuries = 2-3x more likely to die; 1-year mortality 50%
- [P1515] **HECHO** — Hospitalization LOS for NH residents with pressure injuries = 2-3x greater than without
- [P1516] **HECHO** — NH residents with pressure injuries → high sepsis risk; mortality rates as high as 50%
- [P1517] **HECHO** — Nonhealing wound: evaluate for fistula formation, heterotopic calcification, squamous cell carcinoma
- [P1518] **HECHO** — Cost of care triples for NH resident with pressure injury

### CMS Wound Management (F686)

- [P1519] **REGLA** — CMS F686: facility must ensure patient receives care per professional standards to prevent pressure injuries (unless unavoidable) and heal existing ones
- [P1520] **REGLA** — F686 nursing facility may be cited for: failure to prevent new pressure injury, failure to promote healing, failure to prevent progression, failure to treat infectious complications, development of Stage 4 ulcer (unless unavoidable)

### Documentation

- [P1521] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Wound documentation parameters: type (etiology/duration/setting), size (cm: L/W/D), color (% red=granulation/yellow=slough/black=necrotic), exudates (serous/serosanguinous/sanguineous/purulent), odor, peri-wound tissue, undermining/tunneling
- [P1522] **REGLA** — Scheduled 2-3 week reevaluations by trained staff with consistent wound care documentation approach

### IDT Roles

- [P1523] **HECHO** — Wound care IDT roles: Nurse (identify/follow orders), PCP (care plan/orders/referrals), Consultant Pharmacist (antibiotic review), Dietitian (nutritional support), Medical Director (comprehensive skin care program/QAPI), Admin (staff/supplies), Wound Care Team, Transitionist, Homecare, Hospice
- [P1524] **REGLA** — Hospice-nursing facility written agreement must specify separation of wound care role/responsibilities

### Summary

- [P1525] **RESTRICCIÓN** — NPUAP staging guidelines cannot be applied to traumatic, ischemic, venous, or diabetic wounds
- [P1526] **REGLA** — All wounds covered by eschar → categorized as suspected deep tissue injury (SDTI)

## Dementia, Delirium, and Depression


### Introduction

- [P1527] **HECHO** — Cognitive disorders: dementia (chronic), delirium (acute), depression (subacute)
- [P1528] **HECHO** — Clinical features comparison (Table 1): Depression onset=gradual/recurrent, mood=low, attention=intact/impaired; Delirium onset=sudden, attention=impaired; Dementia onset=gradual progressive, attention=intact early→impaired later
- [P1529] **HECHO** — Hallucinations/delusions: absent in depression (except psychotic), present in delirium, variable in dementia
- [P1530] **HECHO** — IADLs impaired before ADLs in dementia

### Dementia — Differential Diagnosis

- [P1531] **DEFINICIÓN** — Dementia = syndrome of chronic, irreversible, progressive global cognitive decline with memory loss, occurring with clear sensorium
- [P1532] **HECHO** — Most common dementia types: Alzheimer's (DAT), vascular (MID), Lewy Body (LBD), frontotemporal (FTD), HIV/AIDS-related
- [P1533] **HECHO** — DAT = most common dementia, accounts for 55-75% of all cases; classified as neurocognitive disorder (NCD) due to AD in DSM-5
- [P1534] **HECHO** — DAT: personality/attention preserved early; executive function declines progressively; behavioral symptoms most common reason for NF admission
- [P1535] **HECHO** — Vascular dementia (Vascular NCD in DSM-5): 2nd most common, 13-16% of cases; early decreased attention, blunted affect, memory disturbance; stepwise progression
- [P1536] **HECHO** — LBD (LBD NCD in DSM-5): 3rd most common, 20-25% of all dementia; earlier onset, faster progression than DAT; increased sensitivity to antipsychotics
- [P1537] **HECHO** — LBD features: visual hallucinations, paranoid delusions, Parkinson motor syndrome (bradykinesia, rigidity, tremors, gait difficulties), autonomic dysfunction (constipation, orthostatic hypotension), REM sleep behavior disorder
- [P1538] **TENSIÓN** — LBD patients sensitive to both antipsychotics (worsen cognition) and antiparkinsonian drugs (increase confusion)
- [P1539] **HECHO** — Creutzfeldt-Jakob (Prion disease NCD): rapidly progressive, myoclonic jerks, seizures
- [P1540] **HECHO** — FTD (frontotemporal lobar degeneration NCD): mood swings, impulsivity, personality coarsening, inattention, affective flattening
- [P1541] **HECHO** — Huntington's disease: inherited, onset 35-45 years; mood changes → dementia with behavioral disturbances + chorea
- [P1542] **HECHO** — Potentially reversible dementia causes: B12 deficiency, thyroid disease, subdural hematoma, normal pressure hydrocephalus, primary brain tumors
- [P1543] **HECHO** — COVID-19/SARS-CoV-2: RNA found in CSF; CVAs within 2 weeks of symptom onset; immune response accelerates neuroinflammatory degeneration → worsens dementia

### Diagnosing Dementia

- [P1544] **DEFINICIÓN** — DSM-5 criteria for major NCD (dementia): significant cognitive impairment in ≥1 domain (learning/memory, language, executive function, complex attention, perceptual-motor, social cognition) + acquired decline from previous level + interferes with independence in everyday activities + not exclusively during delirium/not explained by other mental disorder
- [P1545] **HECHO** — Signs suggesting dementia in LTC: inability to perform ADLs, hygiene changes, altered staff/resident interactions

### Screening/Evaluation

- [P1546] **HECHO** — No evidence for/against routine screening for dementia in asymptomatic adults; not currently performed
- [P1547] **HECHO** — Medical conditions that can cause/worsen dementia: delirium, developmental disability, HIV/AIDS, hyper/hypoglycemia, normal pressure hydrocephalus, TBI sequelae, subdural hematoma, tertiary syphilis, vasculitides, B12 deficiency → `Table 3`
- [P1548] **HECHO** — Gold standard dementia diagnosis = clinical (patient history + physical assessment); ancillary neuropsychologic testing/brain imaging as needed
- [P1549] **HECHO** — Dementia screening tools: MOCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment), Folstein MMSE (30-point, copyrighted), SLUMS (St. Louis University Mental Status Exam)
- [P1550] **HECHO** — MDS in SNFs provides BIMS (Brief Interview for Mental Status) + functional status evaluation
- [P1551] **REGLA** — Neuroimaging should be considered when dementia onset within past 6-12 months, rapidly progressive, or unpredictable course
- [P1552] **HECHO** — Basic cognitive impairment evaluation labs: CBC w/diff, CMP, B12, TSH, medication levels (digoxin, lithium, theophylline, anticonvulsants, TCAs)

### Prognosis

- [P1553] **HECHO** — Advanced dementia: patients become unable to care for self, incontinent, unable to safely swallow/maintain nutrition; develop urinary retention, constipation, repeated UTI/respiratory infections (aspiration)
- [P1554] **HECHO** — Observational studies show no benefit of tube feeding in advanced dementia patients
- [P1555] **HECHO** — Dementia = terminal illness; families often do not understand this

### Pharmacologic Treatment — DAT

- [P1556] **HECHO** — DAT treatment: neurotransmitter modulation; no medication found to slow DAT progression
- [P1557] **HECHO** — Cholinesterase inhibitors for DAT: donepezil (Aricept), rivastigmine (Exelon), galantamine (Razadyne); indicated for mild (MMSE >19), moderate (MMSE 19-10), severe (MMSE <10)
- [P1558] **HECHO** — Memantine (Namenda): only available NMDA receptor antagonist; indicated for moderate-severe AD, monotherapy or with cholinesterase inhibitor
- [P1559] **HECHO** — NSAIDs, estrogen, ginkgo biloba = not effective in treating/preventing AD

### Non-DAT Dementias

- [P1560] **HECHO** — Donepezil tested off-label in LBD and MID; not FDA-approved for these diagnoses

### Challenging Behaviors in Dementia

- [P1561] **HECHO** — Challenging dementia behaviors: pacing, wandering, hoarding, agitation, insomnia, aggression, hypersexuality, perseveration, hallucinations, paranoia, emotional lability
- [P1562] **DEFINICIÓN** — Sundowning: syndrome of disorientation/confusion/agitation starting middle-late afternoon, progressively worsening through evening into night
- [P1563] **HECHO** — Common medical causes of disruptive behavior in dementia: pain, constipation, urinary retention, drug effects, dehydration, infection
- [P1564] **REGLA** — Evaluate reversible environmental/physical stimuli before initiating pharmacologic treatment for behavioral symptoms
- [P1565] **REGLA** — Ask "What is this behavior attempting to communicate?" and "What are their unmet needs?" before prescribing for agitation

### Non-Pharmacologic Treatment of Behaviors

- [P1566] **REGLA** — Establish daily routine; if resident resistant, re-approach shortly later
- [P1567] **HECHO** — COVID-19 restrictions (isolation, routine changes) → overall worsening cognitive symptoms in NF dementia patients (memory, orientation, behavior)
- [P1568] **HECHO** — COVID-19 experience demonstrated non-pharmacological measures have major impact on dementia treatment

### Pharmacologic Treatment of Behaviors

- [P1569] **HECHO** — Agitation with psychosis (hallucinations/delusions/perseverative behaviors) → may respond to antipsychotics
- [P1570] **HECHO** — Sundowning/insomnia → trazodone (sleep) or antipsychotic (confusion)
- [P1571] **HECHO** — Anxiety-component behaviors → SSRI or trazodone
- [P1572] **HECHO** — Hypersexual behaviors → antipsychotic or antiandrogenic hormone (estrogen in males)
- [P1573] **HECHO** — Anger/aggression → serotonergic agents, mood stabilizers (divalproex, carbamazepine, gabapentin), or antipsychotics (risperidone, olanzapine)
- [P1574] **REGLA** — Medication regimens reviewed regularly including periodic dose reduction/discontinuation attempts; 3-6 months post-admission medications often can be reduced/discontinued
- [P1575] **HECHO** — Antipsychotics and benzodiazepines can cause paradoxical agitation/worsen confusion

### Medication Management in Cognitive Impairment

- [P1576] **HECHO** — Average NF resident takes 7 medications; nearly 1/3 take ≥9 medications
- [P1577] **HECHO** — Medications commonly impairing cognition: antiarrhythmics, hypnotics, psychotropics, sedatives, analgesics, anticholinergics (e.g., urinary incontinence drugs)
- [P1578] **HECHO** — Beers list: created 1991 by Dr. Mark Beers; list of potentially inappropriate medications in geriatric population; continues to be updated

### Delirium

- [P1579] **DEFINICIÓN** — Delirium = medical emergency; acute/subacute onset; caused by brain acetylcholine imbalance due to homeostatic/metabolic changes disrupting neuronal circuitry
- [P1580] **HECHO** — Delirium should be anticipated whenever older adult undergoes surgery, is hospitalized, or suffers acute illness
- [P1581] **HECHO** — Delirium associated with cognitive/functional decline long-term; financial burden to society
- [P1582] **DEFINICIÓN** — Delirium types: hyperactive (increased psychomotor activity, anxious/agitated), hypoactive (reduced psychomotor activity, often under-recognized), mixed (fluctuating)
- [P1583] **HECHO** — Delirium affects ≥1/3 hospitalized elders; may last weeks-months; associated with increased in-hospital mortality, longer LOS, functional decline, institutionalization risk
- [P1584] **HECHO** — PA facility patients with delirium: 6-month mortality 25.0% vs 5.7% without delirium
- [P1585] **HECHO** — Hypoactive delirium = most frequent subtype
- [P1586] **HECHO** — Delirium occurs more frequently in women in LTC; found in 18% patients with acute illness
- [P1587] **HECHO** — Delirium risk factors: dementia, older age, functional impairment, multiple comorbidities, dehydration, malnutrition, sensory impairment, history depression, substance abuse history, frailty, polypharmacy, restraint use → `Table 4`
- [P1588] **HECHO** — Hospital setting: up to 1/3 delirium cases potentially preventable or iatrogenic

### Assessment of Delirium

- [P1589] **DEFINICIÓN** — CAM (Confusion Assessment Method): 4 items: (1) acute onset + fluctuating course, (2) inattention, (3) disorganized thinking, (4) altered consciousness; diagnosis requires both (1)+(2) with either (3) or (4)
- [P1590] **HECHO** — CAM: excellent sensitivity/specificity; validated in some care settings
- [P1591] **HECHO** — Other delirium tools: 3D-CAM (3-min diagnostic), 4AT (validated with dementia), DRS-R-98, CAM-S, MDAS
- [P1592] **REGLA** — Delirium evaluation must include: complete interval history, medication review (Rx, OTC, supplements, topicals, substances of abuse), consideration of withdrawal syndromes
- [P1593] **REGLA** — Physical exam for delirium: vital signs + pulse oximetry + blood sugar + mental status/neurologic exam
- [P1594] **HECHO** — Delirium labs: CBC, electrolytes, renal function, urinalysis; consider TSH, B12, medication levels; if indicated: ABG, CXR, neuroimaging, EKG, LP, EEG

### Prevention/Treatment of Delirium

- [P1595] **HECHO** — Delirium prevention interventions (Table 5): reorientation/cognitive stimulation for cognitive impairment, volume repletion for dehydration, cerumen disimpaction/hearing aids, early ambulation for immobility, noise reduction for sleep deprivation, eyeglasses/magnifying lenses for vision impairment
- [P1596] **HECHO** — Pharmacist medication review on computerized system to identify high-risk medications supported in literature for delirium prevention
- [P1597] **REGLA** — Delirium treatment: safe/calming environment, adequate nutrition/hydration, reassurance/redirection/reality orientation, family involvement; treat underlying cause
- [P1598] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Antipsychotic for delirium: only short-term LOW DOSE when necessary to prevent harm; haloperidol most clinically established
- [P1599] **HECHO** — Benzodiazepines not effective for delirium except in acute withdrawal from sedatives/hypnotics/alcohol
- [P1600] **HECHO** — PA patients without objective delirium evidence who received antipsychotics → worse outcomes, increased death rate

### COVID-19 and Delirium

- [P1601] **HECHO** — COVID-19 inpatient delirium incidence: 11-42%; common in older patients with neuropsychiatric comorbidity, higher serum urea, elevated LDH
- [P1602] **HECHO** — COVID-19 delirium pathways: cytokine storm, endothelial damage, increased oxidative stress, hypoxemia, direct CNS infection
- [P1603] **HECHO** — COVID-19 atypical LTC presentation: prodromal diarrhea, fall, hypothermia, delirium, hypotension → respiratory symptoms

### Depression

- [P1604] **HECHO** — Depression prevalence in NF residents: estimated ~7%; extended literature shows MDD ranges 10-29%
- [P1605] **HECHO** — Per Hoover et al., 54% NH residents >65 had physician-diagnosed depression in first year of residence
- [P1606] **HECHO** — Untreated depression worsens coexisting medical illness → poorer outcomes + increased suicide risk
- [P1607] **HECHO** — WHO (2004): unipolar depressive disorders = leading cause global disease burden; projected 1st by 2030

### Symptoms and Risk Factors

- [P1608] **HECHO** — Classic depression symptoms: depressed mood, anhedonia, weight loss/gain, sleep change, psychomotor disturbance, fatigue, guilt, inability to concentrate, suicidal thoughts
- [P1609] **DEFINICIÓN** — "Depression without sadness": prevalent in elderly; presents with lack of vigor, social withdrawal, apathy
- [P1610] **DEFINICIÓN** — "Depression-executive dysfunction syndrome": psychomotor retardation, loss of interest/task initiation, visual naming/verbal fluency difficulties
- [P1611] **HECHO** — Elderly depression risk factors: low economic status, psychosocial stressors, chronic illness, disability, disrupted relationships, poor self-rated health, comorbidities, frequent hospitalizations, lack of NF friends, delirium, dementia, MCI, MI/CAD, pain, diabetes, hearing loss

### Differentiating Depression from Dementia

- [P1612] **DEFINICIÓN** — Pseudodementia: cognitive impairment actually due to depressive disorder, not dementia; aggressive depression treatment → gradual improvement/resolution
- [P1613] **HECHO** — Cornell Scale for Depression in Dementia: interviews patient + caregiver; validated for dementia population
- [P1614] **HECHO** — Dementia = known risk factor for depression; evidence depression may be precursor to dementia

### Diagnosis

- [P1615] **DEFINICIÓN** — DSM-V MDD criteria: ≥5 symptoms for continuous 2-week period; ≥1 must be depressed mood OR lack of interest/pleasure; other symptoms: weight change, appetite change, insomnia/hypersomnia, psychomotor agitation/retardation, fatigue/anergia, guilt/worthlessness, lack of concentration, recurrent death/suicidal thoughts
- [P1616] **HECHO** — Standard depression criteria may not apply to frail elderly; change in functioning/mood from baseline >2 weeks may indicate depression
- [P1617] **HECHO** — Medications causing depression: cardiac (antiarrhythmic/antihypertensive), psychotropic (benzodiazepines/barbiturates), antiseizure, steroids, GI drugs (H2 blockers/metoclopramide), opioid analgesics, carbidopa/levodopa
- [P1618] **DEFINICIÓN** — Other specified depressive disorders: recurrent brief depression, short duration depressive episode (4-13 days), depressive episode with insufficient MDD symptoms

### Screening Tools

- [P1619] **HECHO** — Depression screening tools in PA/LTC: BDI (Beck Depression Index), GDS (Geriatric Depression Scale), CSDD (Cornell Scale for Depression in Dementia)
- [P1620] **DEFINICIÓN** — SIGECAPS mnemonic: Sad, Insomnia, Guilt, Energy lack, Concentration difficulty, Appetite changes, Pleasure (anhedonia), Suicide thoughts; ≥5 positive → may indicate depression
- [P1621] **DEFINICIÓN** — GDS-30: 30-question yes/no interview; 0-10 normal, 11-20 mild depression, 21-30 severe depression; poor predictive value if MMSE ≤15
- [P1622] **DEFINICIÓN** — GDS-15: short form; score >5 suggestive depression → follow-up interview; score >10 almost always depression
- [P1623] **HECHO** — CSDD: 19-item tool; validated across wide cognition range; requires patient + interviewer observation; more time + trained clinician
- [P1624] **HECHO** — SIGECAPS and GDS are public domain; Cornell Scale interviews both caregiver and resident

### Suicide Risk

- [P1625] **HECHO** — Highest suicide risk: Caucasian males; elderly men >80; followed by males 65-80
- [P1626] **HECHO** — Most important suicide risk factors: prior attempt, poor social support, severe depression, psychotic depression, alcoholism, recent loss/bereavement, sedative/hypnotic abuse, development of disability, analgesic abuse
- [P1627] **HECHO** — Suicide rates nearly 2x higher in elderly vs general population; most older adults who commit suicide saw clinician within previous month

### Treatment

- [P1628] **HECHO** — STAR*D study: sustained remission rate only 30% after citalopram treatment
- [P1629] **HECHO** — Psychotherapy not helpful in severe cognitive impairment; CBT and learning-based therapy shown useful for cognitively able patients

### Pharmacotherapy

- [P1630] **REGLA** — SSRIs = first-line antidepressant therapy in elderly due to ease of use/low side effects
- [P1631] **HECHO** — Most tolerated SSRIs in elderly: sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram; fluoxetine least desirable (long half-life); paroxetine not preferred (anticholinergic, sedating)
- [P1632] **HECHO** — SSRI side effects: nausea, dyspepsia, hyponatremia (SIADH), anorexia
- [P1633] **DEFINICIÓN** — Serotonin syndrome: fever, confusion, muscle rigidity, tachycardia, hyperreflexia, agitation; triggered by SSRI + serotonergic agents (SNRIs, buspirone, oxycodone, St. John's wort, linezolid)
- [P1634] **HECHO** — SNRIs (duloxetine): useful with coexistent pain, especially neuropathic; side effects: nausea, agitation, insomnia, increased diastolic BP
- [P1635] **RESTRICCIÓN** — TCAs contraindicated in: cognitive impairment, recent MI (<2 weeks), cardiac conduction abnormalities, orthostatic hypotension, narrow angle glaucoma, urinary retention, BPH
- [P1636] **HECHO** — Nortriptyline and desipramine = more desirable TCAs (less anticholinergic)
- [P1637] **RESTRICCIÓN** — MAOIs typically not used in elderly due to drug-drug interactions/dietary modifications
- [P1638] **HECHO** — Mirtazapine: serotonergic + noradrenergic; useful for insomnia/weight loss (appetite increase, weight gain, nighttime sedation)
- [P1639] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Bupropion lowers seizure threshold → not for patients with seizure disorder
- [P1640] **DEFINICIÓN** — Treatment-resistant depression: failure of 2 oral antidepressants; next options: atypical antipsychotics (aripiprazole), ECT
- [P1641] **HECHO** — Newer agents for elderly depression: psilocybin and ketamine; not yet statistically validated
- [P1642] **REGLA** — First depression episode: 6-12 months antidepressant therapy before reassessment
- [P1643] **REGLA** — ≥3 lifetime depression episodes → consider lifelong antidepressant therapy
- [P1644] **HECHO** — Geriatric patients: lower mortality after >4 years depression treatment with antidepressants + psychotherapy vs usual care

## Ethical and Legal Issues in Post-Acute and Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P1645] **DEFINICIÓN** — Healthcare ethics: framework for applying values (patient, caregiver, societal) to clinical decision-making; requires weighing values, facts, prognostication

### Principles of Health Care Ethics

- [P1646] **DEFINICIÓN** — Five principles healthcare ethics: autonomy, beneficence, justice, non-maleficence, fidelity; do not stand alone/in hierarchy; must be considered in context of each other
- [P1647] **DEFINICIÓN** — Autonomy: individual's right to sovereignty over oneself; encompasses self-determination + privacy; recognized in HIPAA, informed consent, right to decline life-sustaining treatment
- [P1648] **DEFINICIÓN** — Beneficence: practitioner responsibility to do good; any proposed intervention must provide benefit without greater burden than benefit
- [P1649] **DEFINICIÓN** — Non-maleficence: responsibility to do no harm; avoid interventions whose burdens/potential harm outweigh benefit
- [P1650] **DEFINICIÓN** — Justice: treat patients equitably; consider patient + good of society; includes distributive justice (equitable allocation healthcare resources)
- [P1651] **DEFINICIÓN** — Fidelity: keep doctor/patient contract terms; includes truthfulness, informed consent compliance, non-abandonment
- [P1652] **REGLA** — No ethical obligation to provide/offer intervention incapable of providing benefit or whose burden > benefit (futile care)
- [P1653] **DEFINICIÓN** — Paternalism: physicians making decisions based on beneficence without appreciating patient autonomy; countered by Patient's Bill of Rights
- [P1654] **HECHO** — Autonomy subordinate to beneficence/justice when contagious illness poses public health threat
- [P1655] **HECHO** — Beneficence/justice subordinate to autonomy when capacitated adult Jehovah's Witness declines lifesaving transfusion; but State interest prevails for child below age of consent

### Resident Rights

- [P1656] **REGLA** — Entering LTC facility does not cause loss of fundamental constitutional rights
- [P1657] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Providers must inform residents of their rights, encourage/assist exercising them
- [P1658] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Illegal for facility/employee to threaten, coerce, intimidate, or retaliate against resident exercising their rights
- [P1659] **REGLA** — Resident rights may only be restricted to protect resident/another from potential harm or protect another's rights
- [P1660] **HECHO** — CMS resident rights include: treated with respect, own schedule, free from discrimination (race/color/national origin/disability/age/religion), free from abuse/neglect, free from restraints (physical/chemical), complaint without punishment, proper medical care, advance directives, refuse experimental treatment → `Table 1`
- [P1661] **HECHO** — Resident with mental incapacity retains all rights; rights advocated by legally designated surrogate
- [P1662] **HECHO** — Litigation alleging resident human rights infringement may not be covered under malpractice insurance

### Elder Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation

- [P1663] **DEFINICIÓN** — Abuse: act of commission intended to do harm; can be physical, financial, sexual, emotional
- [P1664] **DEFINICIÓN** — Neglect: act of omission; failure to meet obligation to anticipate/meet needs of vulnerable/elderly person; may be purposeful, retaliatory, or from inadequate understanding
- [P1665] **DEFINICIÓN** — Exploitation: misappropriation of vulnerable person's money/property
- [P1666] **HECHO** — Abuse risk factors: caregiver lacks knowledge of alternative behavioral approaches, unresolved stress/depression, cultural acceptance of punishment, history of being abused
- [P1667] **HECHO** — Neglect risk factors: morbid obesity, slow assisted feeding, progressive disability unrecognized, understaffing, inadequate supervision/training, high staff turnover
- [P1668] **OBLIGACIÓN** — CMS SOM requires NF: abuse prevention policies, employee screening, initial/ongoing staff training, identify potential abuse, investigate allegations, protect residents from retaliation, report incidents per state/federal requirements
- [P1669] **HECHO** — Exploitation by families: misappropriating pension/Social Security checks, illegal property transfers; exploitation by staff: pilfering personal property/medication
- [P1670] **HECHO** — Courts require yearly report from guardians to discourage exploitation; no such oversight for other surrogate decision-makers

### Racial Disparity

- [P1671] **HECHO** — Poorer outcomes for patients of color: higher restraint use, more pressure injuries, decreased immunizations, inadequate pain control, poor QoL
- [P1672] **HECHO** — Racial disparity factors: geographic/demographic (discriminatory housing/lending), financial (inadequate Medicare/Medicaid funding + racial wealth disparity), attitudinal (racist attitudes, facility insensitivity, mistrust) → `Table 2`

### Mental Capacity, Competence, Surrogate Decision-Making

- [P1673] **DEFINICIÓN** — Competency = legal determination by court; mental capacity = clinical determination by physician/provider
- [P1674] **REGLA** — All adults presumed mentally competent by law unless adjudicated otherwise
- [P1675] **DEFINICIÓN** — Decisional capacity requires: understand benefits/risks/alternatives (including no treatment), reason among alternatives, communicate decision (i.e., informed consent)
- [P1676] **HECHO** — Incapacity may be permanent (advanced dementia) or temporary (delirium, intoxication)
- [P1677] **DEFINICIÓN** — Testamentary capacity: know nature/extent of property, know heirs, understand disposition will makes, understand implications of bequeathal, free of undue influence
- [P1678] **REGLA** — Many states have statutory hierarchy of family decision-makers: spouse → oldest adult child → etc.; obviates need for guardianship
- [P1679] **DEFINICIÓN** — General Power of Attorney: capacitated person chooses another to make decisions; effective from execution until death/incapacity
- [P1680] **DEFINICIÓN** — Springing Power of Attorney: gives surrogate decision-making powers at future time of incapacity
- [P1681] **DEFINICIÓN** — Durable Power of Attorney: effective at execution + continues beyond occurrence of incapacity
- [P1682] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Only person with mental capacity can execute Power of Attorney; once incapacitated, POA cannot be legally completed → guardianship required
- [P1683] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Guardian may not have authority to: consent to inpatient psychiatric care, sign DNR, authorize withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment without separate court petition
- [P1684] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Surrogate decision-maker generally cannot authorize hiding antipsychotic in food, giving by force/injection on routine basis, even for severely demented/mentally ill patient who objects
- [P1685] **HECHO** — Dementia diagnosis ≠ mental incapacity; Marson study: most AD patients with MMSE ≥19 had capacity to make/communicate choices

### Evaluation of Capacity

- [P1686] **DEFINICIÓN** — Mental capacity evaluation elements (JOMAC): Judgment, Orientation (time/person/place), Memory (recent/remote), Abstract thinking, Calculation + ability to explain decision/implications/rationale/personal goals, persist in decision, negotiate, evaluate undue influence, consider mental illness effect → `Table 3`
- [P1687] **RESTRICCIÓN** — MMSE score should not be sole criterion for determining capacity
- [P1688] **HECHO** — Lifelong poor judgment ≠ mental incapacity; poor judgment from brain disease representing decline from prior functioning may indicate incapacity

### End-of-Life Issues

- [P1689] **HECHO** — LTC facility residents commonly have limited life expectancy: 2-3 years
- [P1690] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Federal mandate: upon admission to hospital/LTC/home health/hospice, inquire whether patient has advance directive
- [P1691] **HECHO** — In emergency, persons without known advance directive = presumed to have elected life-saving/sustaining treatment
- [P1692] **DEFINICIÓN** — Living Will: outlines general/specific wishes of capacitated individual regarding healthcare interventions irrespective of future incapacity
- [P1693] **HECHO** — Living Will issues to consider: POA authority for research consent, organ donation, psychotropic/ECT treatment wishes, relationships/intimacy wishes
- [P1694] **REGLA** — Living Will wishes do not oblige practitioner to provide futile care
- [P1695] **DEFINICIÓN** — AMA defines futile care = medical treatment providing little/no benefit
- [P1696] **REGLA** — No ethical difference between withholding and withdrawing treatment
- [P1697] **HECHO** — Feeding tube begun acutely with uncertain prognosis may be legally/ethically removed when poor prognosis becomes clear

### Research in LTC

- [P1698] **HECHO** — LTC residents = "vulnerable population" requiring high ethical/legal standard for informed research consent
- [P1699] **REGLA** — Elder demonstrating lack of assent to research → not enrolled or withdrawn, regardless of surrogate consent

### Liability in LTC

- [P1700] **DEFINICIÓN** — Medical malpractice requires: (1) defendant had duty, (2) breach of standard of care, (3) breach was substantial proximate cause of harm, (4) harm/damages occurred
- [P1701] **HECHO** — Common liability issues in LTC: dehydration, elopement, emotional distress, falls/fractures, improper restraints, medication errors, pressure ulcers, sexual assault, single event injuries, weight loss → `Table 4`
- [P1702] **HECHO** — Standards of care now viewed as national (not local) in scope
- [P1703] **HECHO** — Factors increasing litigation risk: inadequate documentation, failure to follow policies, documentation not concordant with care, failure to notify physician/family, outdated policies, care plans not updated, unrealistic care plan goals

## Working with Families and Person-Centered Care


### Introduction / Person-Centered Care

- [P1704] **DEFINICIÓN** — CMS definition Person-Centered Care (PCC): "focus on resident as locus of control; support resident making own choices and having control over daily lives"
- [P1705] **HECHO** — PCC benefits: decreased behavioral symptoms, decreased psychotropic medication use, improved family satisfaction, improved psychological wellbeing/QoL
- [P1706] **HECHO** — Family role under PCC: "partners" in care; provide input on past medical/social history, post-traumatic events, customs, likes/dislikes, daily routine, personal values/preferences

### Attitudes

- [P1707] **REGLA** — IDT must examine own attitudes toward families; personal biases about family structure, gender roles, filial obligation can impact effectiveness
- [P1708] **HECHO** — "Dysfunctional" label for families signals caregiver feeling hopeless/powerless; dwelling on it interferes with problem solving

### Knowledge

- [P1709] **HECHO** — Moving into PA/LTC community = major stressor for resident and family
- [P1710] **REGLA** — Upon admission, discuss facility/state policy regarding restraints; explain risks/indignity of restraints to resident/family
- [P1711] **REGLA** — Educate person/family about PCC; address misperceptions

### Skills — Communication

- [P1712] **HECHO** — Issues confronting families requiring proactive IDT effort: incident notification, acute status change, problematic behavior/consequences, progressive frailty/palliative care consideration
- [P1713] **REGLA** — Ask family caregiver "How are you doing with all this?"; encourage self-care, caregiver support group participation
- [P1714] **HECHO** — Advance Care Planning (ACP) mixed efficacy; consensus definition proposed (Sudore 2017)
- [P1715] **DEFINICIÓN** — ACP: process supporting adults at any age/health stage to understand/share personal values, life goals, preferences for future medical care; goal = ensure medical care consistent with values/goals/preferences during serious/chronic illness

### Skills — Problematic Phrases

- [P1716] **REGLA** — Avoid "There is nothing more to do" (sounds like abandonment); use "There is nothing more we can do to cure your illness" → opens door for symptom management/comfort/support
- [P1717] **REGLA** — Avoid "Would you like us to do everything?" (families answer "yes" thinking broadly); instead explain what can be done at facility + offer aggressive symptom management

### Family Spokesperson

- [P1718] **REGLA** — Designate family spokesperson for communication with IDT; choice rests with family
- [P1719] **REGLA** — Determine spokesperson's preferred communication method (phone/text/email/in-person); confirm contact info; provide facility contacts; determine backup family member

### Responding to Unhappy Family

- [P1720] **REGLA** — Before returning family call, contact facility staff for relevant information; if complex/emotionally distraught, consider visiting resident first
- [P1721] **REGLA** — Listen to whole story before interrupting/explaining; reflect back what you heard with enough detail to convince family you understood
- [P1722] **HECHO** — Transcribed messages can appear more hostile than actual voice tone; when hostility perceived, respond by phone to hear tone/inflection

### Family Meetings (Table 1)

- [P1723] **HECHO** — Family meetings: usually at health crisis, urgent decision needed, often involve IDT-patient/family conflict or intra-family conflict; goal = resolve conflict/reach acceptable action plan
- [P1724] **REGLA** — Preparation: learn about patient/situation, consult social worker, review medical record, invite all family members + remote members by phone/HIPAA-compliant platform
- [P1725] **REGLA** — Meeting rules: each person invited to speak without interruption; patient/least empowered person speaks first; intimidating/opinionated person speaks last
- [P1726] **REGLA** — After all speak, moderator summarizes → obtains assent; most credible person provides education + realistic goals; agree on measurable outcomes + communication plan

### Care Plan Meetings (CPMs) (Table 2)

- [P1727] **DEFINICIÓN** — Care Plan Meeting (CPM): federally mandated RAI-based; RAI components: (1) MDS 3.0 (comprehensive standardized assessment), (2) CAA (care area assessment for decision-making), (3) RAI utilization guidelines
- [P1728] **REQUISITO** — F657 Comprehensive Care Plan IDT must include: attending physician, RN with responsibility, nurse aide with responsibility, food/nutrition staff, resident/representative (to extent practicable)
- [P1729] **REGLA** — If resident/representative participation not practicable, explanation must be in medical record
- [P1730] **REGLA** — CPM moderated by RN (MDS nurse); collaborative tone; goal = develop/review/revise person-centered care plan
- [P1731] **REGLA** — Avoid medical jargon/abbreviations in care plan meetings; periodically check understanding

### Empowering Patient/Representative

- [P1732] **HECHO** — >70% hospitals with 50+ beds have inpatient palliative care programs
- [P1733] **HECHO** — Hospital-to-LTC transfers contain many clinical details but little information about patient as person
- [P1734] **REGLA** — Family genogram helpful for understanding family structure/function; update periodically; review before calls/meetings

### During Care Plan Meeting

- [P1735] **REGLA** — Subsequent CPMs start with determining impact of previous interventions on QoL; patient may report intervention more burdensome than problem it addressed
- [P1736] **REGLA** — Patient/resident or family input critical in deciding how to proceed; if intervention declined, discuss risk mitigation
- [P1737] **REGLA** — Patient/resident and/or family member should receive copy of care plan

### Cultural Differences

- [P1738] **REGLA** — Reflect on personal/professional culture assumptions; learn basic knowledge of patient/resident culture but do not assume stereotypes apply
- [P1739] **REGLA** — When translator needed: talk directly to person not translator; professional translators preferred over family members

## Documentation and Coding


### Introduction

- [P1740] **DEFINICIÓN** — E/M (Evaluation and Management) code determines reimbursement level for physician/practitioner services in LTC facilities
- [P1741] **DEFINICIÓN** — Admission = patient enters NF/SNF with no open clinical or financial records for current stay
- [P1742] **DEFINICIÓN** — Readmission (sense 1) = patient returns to NF/SNF after bed hold; clinical/financial records remained open; bill subsequent care codes 99307-99310
- [P1743] **DEFINICIÓN** — Readmission (sense 2) = patient returns to NF/SNF after discharge; prior records closed; bill initial care codes 99304-99306
- [P1744] **DEFINICIÓN** — Discharge = patient leaves NF/SNF to another care venue; clinical/financial records closed, even if return probable
- [P1745] **DEFINICIÓN** — Bed Hold = patient leaves NF/SNF (usually to hospital); records remain open expecting return
- [P1746] **DEFINICIÓN** — MDS (Minimum Data Set) = comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation performed on admission + periodically; submitted electronically to State/Federal agencies
- [P1747] **DEFINICIÓN** — Initial Visit = initial comprehensive assessment + plan of care development + admitting orders written/verified
- [P1748] **DEFINICIÓN** — NF (Nursing Facility) = institution providing skilled nursing/rehab; residents do NOT receive Medicare Part A benefits
- [P1749] **DEFINICIÓN** — SNF (Skilled Nursing Facility) = institution providing skilled nursing/rehab; residents receive Medicare Part A benefits
- [P1750] **HECHO** — CMS issues documentation guidelines for E/M services; AMA responsible for CPT codes establishment/updating
- [P1751] **HECHO** — AMDA issued Guide to PA/LTC Coding, Reimbursement, and Documentation based on AMA CPT guidelines
- [P1752] **HECHO** — Updated CPT E/M codes effective January 1, 2023
- [P1753] **REGLA** — Many E/M service codes now based on medical decision making OR time (as of 2023)

### Documenting the Visit

- [P1754] **HECHO** — 7 components constitute practitioner patient visit; first 3 = Key Components determining E/M service level
- [P1755] **DEFINICIÓN** — Key Components: (1) extensiveness of history, (2) extensiveness of physical exam, (3) complexity of medical decision making
- [P1756] **HECHO** — Additional components: patient counseling, coordination of care, severity of presenting problem, suggested time/duration
- [P1757] **REGLA** — Chief complaint required for each encounter
- [P1758] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 history levels: problem focused, expanded problem focused, detailed, comprehensive
- [P1759] **DEFINICIÓN** — Problem focused history = brief HPI
- [P1760] **DEFINICIÓN** — Expanded problem focused history = brief HPI + problem pertinent system review
- [P1761] **DEFINICIÓN** — Detailed history = extended HPI + problem pertinent ROS + pertinent past/family/social history
- [P1762] **DEFINICIÓN** — Comprehensive history = extended HPI + full ROS + complete past/family/social history
- [P1763] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 physical exam levels: problem focused (limited affected area), expanded problem focused (limited + related areas), detailed (extended affected + related), comprehensive (general multisystem or complete single organ system)
- [P1764] **HECHO** — MDM complexity factors: number/complexity of problems, management options considered, amount/complexity of data reviewed, risk of complications/morbidity/mortality + comorbidities
- [P1765] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 MDM levels: straightforward, low complexity, moderate complexity, high complexity

### Visit Time

- [P1766] **REGLA** — As of January 1, 2023, time allocated to E/M visit code can determine level of service
- [P1767] **REGLA** — Visit time determines E/M level if counseling + coordination of care > 50% total time with patient
- [P1768] **REGLA** — Documentation of each encounter must be sufficient detail to support E/M code

### Coding and Billing for Skilled Nursing and Non-Skilled Nursing Facility Visits

- [P1769] **REGLA** — Initial NF care codes: 99304, 99305, 99306; used on admission for initial comprehensive assessment
- [P1770] **REQUISITO** — Attending physician of record must append modifier "AI" to bill when performing initial visits
- [P1771] **REGLA** — Subsequent NF care codes: 99307, 99308, 99309, 99310
- [P1772] **REGLA** — Discharge service codes: 99315 (≤30 min) or 99316 (>30 min)
- [P1773] **HECHO** — Code 99318 (annual health evaluation) deleted as of January 1, 2023
- [P1774] **REGLA** — All NF codes apply to both new and established patients

## Medication Management in Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P1841] **HECHO** — 2/3 of Americans >65 have multiple chronic conditions (multi-morbidities)
- [P1842] **HECHO** — Leading causes of death in older adults: heart disease, cancer, stroke, respiratory disease, dementia, diabetes mellitus
- [P1843] **HECHO** — Census projections: by 2030, 20% of US population will be ≥65 years
- [P1844] **HECHO** — Chronic disease, limited reserves, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic changes, impaired immune systems predispose elders to serious ADEs: falls, hip fractures, weight loss, cognitive/functional decline
- [P1845] **HECHO** — 27% of LTC patients routinely take ≥9 medications/day
- [P1846] **HECHO** — >65% of LTC patients will have ADE during 4-year period; 1 in 7 ADEs results in hospital transfer

### Physiologic Changes in the Elderly

- [P1847] **HECHO** — Aging effect on decreased gastric/intestinal motility has NOT shown consistent effect on drug absorption
- [P1848] **HECHO** — Gastric hypochlorhydria with aging decreases absorption of some medications (e.g., ketoconazole)
- [P1849] **HECHO** — PPI/H2 antagonist use → hypochlorhydria → decreased drug and vitamin B12 absorption
- [P1850] **HECHO** — CHF causing bowel wall edema can decrease furosemide absorption → reduced efficacy
- [P1851] **HECHO** — Epidermal thinning/skin changes in aging significantly decrease transdermal fentanyl absorption; reduced clearance may increase analgesic effect/risk
- [P1852] **HECHO** — Factors decreasing transdermal absorption: diminished peripheral blood flow, increased keratinization, decreased hydration/surface lipids, increased intramuscular connective tissue

### Distribution

- [P1853] **HECHO** — Aging decreases lean-to-fat ratio; total body water reduced 10-15% by age 80
- [P1854] **REGLA** — Recommended to reduce loading doses by 10-20% for water-soluble medication in elderly
- [P1855] **HECHO** — Age-related body fat increase → increased Vd for lipid-soluble drugs (benzodiazepines, amiodarone, thyroid hormones) → reduced clearance + increased half-life
- [P1856] **HECHO** — Albumin/alpha-1-acid glycoprotein concentrations do not normally decline significantly with aging
- [P1857] **HECHO** — Nutritional deficiencies/catabolic states may cause clinically important decline in plasma proteins
- [P1858] **HECHO** — Highly protein-bound drugs (carbamazepine, phenytoin, valproic acid, warfarin) → higher free serum concentrations in elderly with decreased plasma proteins
- [P1859] **REGLA** — Dose adjustments may need to be based on free medication concentration, not total blood concentration (e.g., free phenytoin)

### Metabolism

- [P1860] **HECHO** — Liver mass and blood flow decrease with aging → reduced clearance + increased half-life/bioavailability for drugs with extensive first-pass metabolism (propranolol, labetalol)
- [P1861] **HECHO** — Bioavailability of some ACE inhibitors (enalapril) and opioids (codeine) reduced as they require hepatic activation
- [P1862] **HECHO** — CYP450 oxidase activity decreases with age; Phase I reactions (reduction, oxidation, hydroxylation, demethylation) decline
- [P1863] **HECHO** — Phase II reactions (glucuronidation, sulfation, acetylation) minimally influenced by aging
- [P1864] **HECHO** — Grapefruit juice = known CYP3A4 inhibitor → decreased clearance + increased half-life + toxicity risk
- [P1865] **HECHO** — CYP3A4 interaction: fat-soluble statin + amiodarone/cipro → increased statin level → myopathy risk
- [P1866] **HECHO** — CYP3A4 interaction: azithromycin/amiodarone/cipro → QT prolongation → arrhythmia
- [P1867] **HECHO** — CYP3A4 interaction: warfarin + amiodarone → high INR → bleeding
- [P1868] **HECHO** — CYP2D6 interaction: metoprolol + fluoxetine → bradycardia, AV block
- [P1869] **HECHO** — CYP2C9 interaction: warfarin + fluoxetine → high INR → bleeding
- [P1870] **HECHO** — CYP2C9 interaction: glipizide + amiodarone → hypoglycemia
- [P1871] **HECHO** — CYP2C19 interaction: omeprazole + warfarin → high INR → bleeding

### Renal Elimination and Clearance

- [P1872] **HECHO** — Decreased renal elimination = most significant pharmacokinetic change in older adults
- [P1873] **HECHO** — 40% of 10,000 LTC residents had significant renal insufficiency
- [P1874] **HECHO** — Renal mass decreases ~20% from 4th to 8th decade; concomitant reduction in renal blood flow, GFR, tubular secretion
- [P1875] **REGLA** — Drugs dependent on renal clearance require dosage reduction in elderly
- [P1876] **HECHO** — Serum creatinine alone NOT accurate measure of renal function in elderly (decreased muscle mass, reduced activity, decreased protein intake, altered tubular secretion)
- [P1877] **REGLA** — Cockcroft-Gault equation (serum creatinine + sex + age + estimated lean body weight) provides more accurate creatinine clearance approximation
- [P1878] **HECHO** — MDRD equation also estimates GFR; standard in some labs but hasn't replaced Cockcroft-Gault

### Pharmacodynamics

- [P1879] **HECHO** — Elderly show increased sensitivity to: CNS effects of benzodiazepines/alcohol, opioid analgesia, anticoagulants (warfarin/heparin)
- [P1880] **HECHO** — Elderly increased risk of: delirium from anticholinergics, bladder outlet obstruction from anticholinergics, EPS/tardive dyskinesia from antipsychotics
- [P1881] **HECHO** — Elderly show decreased response: reduced sensitivity to beta-adrenergic agonists/antagonists

### Medication Selection in the Elderly

- [P1882] **HECHO** — Medication management triad in LTCF: prescriber + nursing personnel + pharmacy provider/consultant
- [P1883] **HECHO** — AGS Beers Criteria = evidence-based list of medications with increased risk-to-benefit ratio, best avoided or cautiously prescribed in elderly → `AGS`
- [P1884] **HECHO** — Beers Criteria includes medications with significant anticholinergic effects: antihistamines (diphenhydramine), antiemetics (promethazine)
- [P1885] **HECHO** — Beers medications propensity: worsen mental status (delirium), falls, urinary retention, orthostatic hypotension, dehydration, EPS, tardive dyskinesia
- [P1886] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Limiting AGS Beers Criteria medications NOT necessarily contraindicated in hospice patients
- [P1887] **HECHO** — STOPP (Screening Tool of Older Person's Prescriptions) = alternative screening tool; may be more user-friendly than Beers; more sensitive measure of potentially inappropriate prescribing → `STOPP`

- [P1912] **REGLA** — Long-term opioids in dementia → avoid unless palliative care
- [P1913] **REGLA** — Regular scheduled opioids >2 weeks with chronic constipation without laxatives → impaction, bowel perforation/ischemia

### STOPPFrail

- [P1914] **DEFINICIÓN** — STOPPFrail = 27 criteria for deprescribing in persons in last year of life → `STOPPFrail`
- [P1915] **REGLA** — STOPPFrail conditions: end-stage irreversible disease, poor 1-year survival, severe functional/cognitive impairment, symptom control priority, persistently refused/forgotten medications
- [P1916] **HECHO** — RCT using STOPPFrail: no undue harm/negative outcomes when potentially unnecessary medications discontinued vs. usual therapy

### START Criteria

- [P1917] **DEFINICIÓN** — START (Screening Tool to Alert Doctors to Right Treatment) = identifies potentially beneficial medications omitted from LTCF resident's regimen → `START`
- [P1918] **HECHO** — Drug omission examples in LTCFs: no PPI with NSAID/prednisone, no ACEi in diabetics, no vitamin D for osteoporosis risk, no VTE prophylaxis
- [P1919] **REGLA** — START: anticoagulation for chronic AF (DOAC/warfarin/aspirin); aspirin/clopidogrel for documented atherosclerotic disease in sinus rhythm
- [P1920] **REGLA** — START: antihypertensive therapy when SBP consistently >160 mmHg
- [P1921] **REGLA** — START: statin for documented vascular disease if functional ADL-independent + life expectancy >5 years
- [P1922] **REGLA** — START: optimize CHF treatment per guidelines; optimize post-MI/angina treatment
- [P1923] **REGLA** — START: optimize COPD/asthma treatment; optimize chronic respiratory failure O2 therapy
- [P1924] **REGLA** — START: optimize Parkinson's treatment with functional impairment; optimize moderate-severe depression treatment
- [P1925] **REGLA** — START: cognitive impairment/dementia treatment (AChI/NMDA) if ADL-independent + life expectancy >5 years + appropriate to goals of care
- [P1926] **REGLA** — START: optimize moderate-severe GERD treatment (PPI/H2RA); PPI/H2RA prevention with NSAID use
- [P1927] **REGLA** — START: fiber supplementation for chronic symptomatic diverticular disease with constipation
- [P1928] **REGLA** — START: optimize active RA treatment (DMARDs, biologics); optimize osteoporosis treatment (bisphosphonates, calcium, vitamin D)
- [P1929] **REGLA** — START: optimize DM/metabolic syndrome treatment (metformin); ACEi/ARB to reduce diabetic nephropathy risk; antiplatelet/statin to reduce DM CV risk

### Anticholinergic Burden

- [P1930] **HECHO** — Highly anticholinergic medications associated with increased risk of hospitalization, mortality, dementia, pneumonia
- [P1931] **DEFINICIÓN** — Anticholinergic burden tools: ACB (Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden List), ADS (Anticholinergic Drug Scale), ARS (Anticholinergic Risk Scale); scoring: 3=highly, 2=moderately, 1=mildly anticholinergic
- [P1932] **HECHO** — Multiple anticholinergic drugs → cumulative burden → increased delirium, worsening dementia, hospitalization, mortality risk

### "Deprescribing" and GDR

- [P1933] **DEFINICIÓN** — Deprescribing = systematic evaluation and discontinuation of potentially unnecessary medication
- [P1934] **DEFINICIÓN** — GDR (Gradual Dose Reduction) = CMS term for cautious gradual medication discontinuation
- [P1935] **HECHO** — AMDA 2021 launched "Drive to Deprescribe" (D2D): goal to reduce unnecessary medication use by 25%
- [P1936] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Federal NF regulation: GDR must be attempted ≥quarterly for all sedative/hypnotics and psychotropics prescribed scheduled beyond manufacturer's recommended duration → `F-Tag 757`
- [P1937] **HECHO** — Current practice extends GDR to all medications
- [P1938] **PERMISO** — Consultant pharmacist GDR recommendation can be declined if: (1) continued use = current standard of practice + GDR likely impairs function/psychiatric stability; or (2) target symptoms returned/worsened after most recent GDR
- [P1939] **OBLIGACIÓN** — SOM guidelines: medication prescribed only when necessary, lowest effective dose; each resident's drug regimen free from unnecessary drugs (F-Tag 757) → `CMS`
- [P1940] **DEFINICIÓN** — Unnecessary drug (F-Tag 757): excessive dose (including duplicate therapy), excessive duration, without adequate monitoring, without adequate indication, with adverse consequences indicating dose reduction/discontinuation, or any combination

### Transitions in Care and Medication Errors

- [P1941] **HECHO** — Medication-related adverse events common after hospital discharge → `JCAHO`
- [P1942] **HECHO** — JCAHO National Patient Safety goal: medication reconciliation + accurate medication information transmission between care settings
- [P1943] **HECHO** — Risk factors for medication-related adverse events during care transition: polypharmacy (>4 medications), inadequate monitoring of high-risk meds (insulin, warfarin), chronic complex illness, hurried transfers during nonstandard times, inadequate post-discharge support
- [P1944] **REQUISITO** — Hand-off communication must include comprehensive up-to-date records; universally accessible EHR across all care settings essential

### Use of Psychoactive Medication

- [P1945] **REGLA** — AGS 2014 Choosing Wisely: non-pharmacologic strategies = first-line treatment for dementia-related aggression/disruptive behaviors → `AGS`
- [P1946] **REGLA** — If antipsychotic prescribed for BPSD: inform patient/family of potential adverse effects; many facilities require family informed consent
- [P1947] **REQUISITO** — Proactive monitoring of BP, serum lipid, glucose, creatinine required for any patient on antipsychotic
- [P1948] **HECHO** — Meta-analysis: antipsychotics may be successfully discontinued in older adults with dementia/neuropsychiatric symptoms who have been on antipsychotics ≥3 months, with little/no rebound effect on BPSD

### Overprescribing of Antibiotics

- [P1949] **HECHO** — Antibiotic stewardship = integral component of quality assurance/performance improvement in LTCFs
- [P1950] **HECHO** — Antibiotic overuse linked to: drug-drug interactions, colonization with multiresistant organisms, "super-bugs"
- [P1951] **TENSIÓN** — Questionable evidence for: preventive antibiotics for recurrent UTIs, antibiotics for acute bronchitis (often viral), acute sinusitis, ongoing antibiotics in COPD, cellulitis prevention (often overdiagnosed), dental procedure prophylaxis

### Overprescribing of PPIs

- [P1952] **HECHO** — Long-term PPI adverse effects: C. difficile infection, increased infection in cirrhotics, acute interstitial nephritis, clopidogrel activation prevention, hip fracture risk, CKD risk, community-acquired pneumonia, decreased iron/B12/magnesium absorption, rebound gastric acid hypersecretion on discontinuation
- [P1953] **REGLA** — PPI deprescribing options: decrease dose, switch to PRN use, stop + start alternative medication
- [P1954] **REGLA** — H2 receptor blockers = appropriate PPI alternative; associated with less C. difficile + fracture risk
- [P1955] **HECHO** — Non-pharmacologic PPI alternatives: weight loss, elevated head of bed, limiting bedtime meals, avoiding high-fat greasy meals

### Selection of Diabetic Medication

- [P1956] **HECHO** — Intensive glucose control (HbA1c <6.5-7%) in elderly with high comorbidity → diminished CV benefit + increased hypoglycemia risk
- [P1957] **HECHO** — Metformin: oral, no hypoglycemia, weight loss, potential CV benefit; contraindicated if GFR <30; GI side effects, B12 deficiency risk, lactic acidosis risk
- [P1958] **HECHO** — Thiazolidinediones: oral, no hypoglycemia, weight gain; Black Box Warning for CHF (rosiglitazone/pioglitazone); fluid retention/edema, bone fracture risk, bladder cancer risk (pioglitazone)
- [P1959] **HECHO** — Sulfonylureas: oral, YES hypoglycemia, weight gain, CV neutral; avoid glyburide in renal impairment; glipizide preferred in older population
- [P1960] **HECHO** — GLP-1 analogs: subq, no hypoglycemia, weight loss, CV benefit (liraglutide/semaglutide); caution if GFR <30; thyroid C-cell tumor risk, GI effects, pancreatitis risk
- [P1961] **HECHO** — SGLT-2 inhibitors: oral, no hypoglycemia, weight loss, CV benefit (canagliflozin/empagliflozin); DKA risk, GU infection, amputation risk (canagliflozin), volume depletion
- [P1962] **HECHO** — DPP-4 inhibitors: oral, no hypoglycemia, weight neutral; CHF risk (saxagliptin/alogliptin); dose adjustment in renal impairment; well tolerated in elderly
- [P1963] **HECHO** — Insulin: subq, YES hypoglycemia, weight gain, CV neutral; lower doses for renal impairment

### Choice and Use of Analgesics

- [P1964] **REGLA** — AGS cautions against nonselective NSAIDs (especially long half-life: naproxen, piroxicam) and COX-2 inhibitors in elderly due to cardiac, GI, CNS, renal effects
- [P1965] **HECHO** — NSAIDs/COX-2 drug interactions: ACEi (hyperkalemia), diuretics (diminished diuresis), methotrexate (decreased clearance), anticoagulants (potentiated effects), lithium (decreased clearance → toxicity)
- [P1966] **HECHO** — Concomitant NSAID (especially ibuprofen) or COX-2 inhibitor with daily cardio-preventive aspirin may negate aspirin's cardio-preventive effect
- [P1967] **HECHO** — FDA Advisory Panel recommended acetaminophen max per-unit dose lowered to 325 mg tablet; max daily dose for OA <4 g/day (2-3 g/day)
- [P1968] **HECHO** — Opioid/acetaminophen combinations increase accidental overdose + acute liver failure risk, especially with OTC acetaminophen

### Medication Treatment Goals for Hypertension

- [P1969] **HECHO** — JNC-8 (2014): BP goal for persons >60 years = <150/90
- [P1970] **TENSIÓN** — Many clinicians advocate <140/90 for <80 years; 150/90 for >80 years vs JNC-8 uniform <150/90
- [P1971] **HECHO** — JNC-8 changed CKD/DM BP goal from <130/80 → <140/90
- [P1972] **HECHO** — ACC/AHA 2017: define elderly as >65; start pharmacotherapy at BP >130/80
- [P1973] **TENSIÓN** — ACP and AAFP did NOT support ACC/AHA 2017; continue to recommend JNC-8 (<150/90 for >60 years)
- [P1974] **HECHO** — ESC/ESH 2018: "very old" = ≥80 years; start pharmacotherapy at BP >160/90; lower target 130-139/70-79 for >65 but not very old (>85)
- [P1975] **REGLA** — DASH diet with sodium restriction 1500 mg/day (4 g or 2/3 teaspoon table salt) recommended for antihypertensive lifestyle modification
- [P1976] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Restrictive diets NOT recommended in frail elderly due to weight loss potential

### Beta-Blocker Use in Post-MI

- [P1977] **HECHO** — Age >75 associated with underuse of beta-blockers post-MI
- [P1978] **HECHO** — Mortality rate 43% less among beta-blocker recipients vs non-recipients post-MI

### Choice of Anticoagulants in Non-Valvular AF

- [P1979] **REGLA** — CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥1 (male) or >1 (female) → high embolic stroke risk → consider anticoagulation
- [P1980] **HECHO** — Current recommendation: DOACs (specifically apixaban) preferred over warfarin for AF anticoagulation
- [P1981] **HECHO** — Previously warfarin first-line with INR goal 2-3 for AF >75 years or 65-75 with stroke risk factors

### Treatment of Subclinical Hypothyroidism

- [P1982] **HECHO** — Subclinical hypothyroidism prevalence up to 18% in elderly; progresses to overt at 5-18%/year
- [P1983] **HECHO** — Most common cause = chronic autoimmune (Hashimoto's) thyroiditis
- [P1984] **REGLA** — TSH ≥10 mU/L → treat at all ages (universally accepted)
- [P1985] **REGLA** — Elderly (>65-70 years): avoid treatment when TSH <7
- [P1986] **REGLA** — TSH 7-9.9 in elderly: treat ONLY if symptoms suggestive of hypothyroidism
- [P1987] **HECHO** — No benefit treating older persons with asymptomatic subclinical hypothyroidism (TSH 7-9.9); treatment can increase iatrogenic thyrotoxicosis risk
- [P1988] **REGLA** — Start levothyroxine 25-50 mcg daily; recheck TSH in 4-6 weeks

### Choosing Wisely and Medication Management

- [P1989] **HECHO** — ABIM Foundation developed Choosing Wisely campaign to discourage low/no-benefit medications/interventions → `ABIM`
- [P1990] **HECHO** — AMDA spearheaded "Quality Prescribing Initiative" 2015 in collaboration with CMS + LTC provider organizations

### Choosing Wisely Recommendations (Appendix B)

- [P1991] **REGLA** — AMDA: do not use sliding scale insulin for long-term DM management in NF
- [P1992] **REGLA** — AMDA: do not routinely prescribe lipid-lowering medication with limited life expectancy
- [P1993] **REGLA** — AMDA: do not initiate HTN treatment in >60 years for SBP <150 or DBP <90
- [P1994] **REGLA** — AMDA: do not prescribe antipsychotics for BPSD without assessment for underlying behavioral cause
- [P1995] **REGLA** — AGS: do not prescribe medication without drug regimen review
- [P1996] **REGLA** — AGS: do not prescribe cholinesterase inhibitors for dementia without periodic assessment for cognitive benefits + adverse GI effects
- [P1997] **REGLA** — AGS: avoid prescription appetite stimulants/high-calorie supplements for anorexia/cachexia in older adults
- [P1998] **REGLA** — Hospice/Palliative: do not use ABH gel (Ativan/Benadryl/Haldol) for nausea → absent/insufficient transdermal absorption
- [P1999] **REGLA** — APA: do not routinely prescribe antipsychotics as first-line for insomnia; do not prescribe ≥2 antipsychotics concurrently
- [P2000] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not start new medications to treat symptoms caused by existing therapies (prescribing cascade)
- [P2001] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not use highly anticholinergic medications when safer alternatives available
- [P2002] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not use anticholinergics concomitantly with acetylcholinesterase inhibitors in dementia
- [P2003] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not use ≥2 anticoagulants/antiplatelet agents in older adults at high bleeding risk
- [P2004] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not use ≥3 CNS-active medications → high fall/fracture risk
- [P2005] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not combine opioids with benzodiazepines/gabapentin → excessive sedation/overdose/death risk
- [P2006] **REGLA** — ASCP: do not use tramadol without considering risks of sedation, serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, seizures; especially with renal insufficiency or SSRIs/CYP2D6 inhibitors
- [P2007] **REGLA** — ASN: do not administer ESAs to CKD patients with hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL without anemia symptoms
- [P2008] **REGLA** — ASN: avoid NSAIDs in HTN, HF, or CKD of all causes including diabetes

### F-Tag 757 (Appendix A)

- [P2009] **OBLIGACIÓN** — F-Tag 757: each resident's drug regimen must be free from unnecessary drugs → `CFR 483.25(l)`
- [P2010] **REQUISITO** — F-Tag 757 intent: medication regimen promotes/maintains highest practicable mental/physical/psychosocial well-being
- [P2011] **REQUISITO** — Non-pharmacological interventions (behavioral) must be considered/used when indicated, instead of or in addition to medication
- [P2012] **REQUISITO** — Potential contribution of medication regimen to unanticipated decline/new symptoms must be recognized/evaluated; regimen modified when appropriate

### Pharmacogenomics

- [P2013] **HECHO** — FDA provides pharmacogenetic information/recommendations within labeling for ~200 medications
- [P2014] **HECHO** — CYP2D6 gene has >160 versions; natural variation may cause overconversion (codeine → excessive morphine) or underconversion (ineffective analgesia)
- [P2015] **HECHO** — Pharmacogenomics Knowledge Base available at pharmgkb.org; links patient pharmacogenetic tests to dosing recommendations

## Rehabilitation and Maximizing Function in Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P2016] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Federal NF regulations require care/services enabling residents to attain/maintain highest practicable physical/mental/psychosocial well-being
- [P2017] **HECHO** — ~1.5 million persons receive rehabilitation in NF each year in US
- [P2018] **HECHO** — SNF residents = largest percentage of short-stay admissions
- [P2019] **HECHO** — Rehabilitation = service delivered WITH patient; patient motivation/participation paramount

### Background

- [P2020] **REGLA** — IRF requires patient able to participate in ≥2-3 h combined therapies daily (high intensity)
- [P2021] **REGLA** — SNF rehab = patient tolerates <2-3 h combined therapies daily (lower intensity)
- [P2022] **HECHO** — Rehab care sites: IRF, LTAC, NF/SNF, ALF, PACE, HHA, outpatient rehab center
- [P2023] **PERMISO** — Long-stay NF residents can receive rehab services if functional status deteriorates due to acute/chronic illness
- [P2024] **REQUISITO** — Rehab goals determined by IDT (OT, PT, ST, nurses, attending physician/NP/PA) aligned with resident preferences
- [P2025] **REGLA** — Goals must be realistic, attainable, aimed at improving function/independence while respecting QoL/dignity
- [P2026] **HECHO** — Restoration to pre-illness function may not be achievable; lesser functional level may suffice for goals of care

### Types of Therapy Services

- [P2027] **DEFINICIÓN** — PT = physical therapy, focus on mobility/ambulation/transfers (lower extremity function)
- [P2028] **DEFINICIÓN** — OT = occupational therapy, focus on self-care/ADLs (upper extremity function); some OTs also evaluate eating/swallowing difficulties
- [P2029] **DEFINICIÓN** — ST = speech/language therapy, addresses speech/language/voice/communication/cognition/swallowing; provides altered consistency diet recommendations for dysphagia
- [P2030] **HECHO** — Complementary modalities: respiratory therapy, psychotherapy, cognitive therapy, recreational therapy (music/pet therapy)
- [P2031] **REQUISITO** — All rehab providers must coordinate services as part of comprehensive assessment/plan of care following federally mandated care area assessments

### Payment for Rehabilitation Therapy

- [P2032] **REGLA** — Under Medicare, rehab therapy cost included in prospective payment daily rate paid to SNF
- [P2033] **REGLA** — SNF benefit eligibility usually requires ≥3-day hospitalization; waived during COVID pandemic
- [P2034] **REGLA** — After Medicare Part A/private insurance coverage ends, ongoing rehab covered under Medicare Part B (during NF stay or community)
- [P2035] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Medicare Part B therapy services limited to annual cap; must meet medical necessity requirements

### Multi-Morbidities and Frailty

- [P2036] **HECHO** — Common conditions delaying rehab: cardiac, pulmonary, GI, renal disease, malnutrition, depression, musculoskeletal/neurological problems, deconditioning, sarcopenia
- [P2037] **HECHO** — These conditions interact dynamically → further functional decline → limited rehab response → suboptimal outcome

### Prevention During Rehabilitation

- [P2038] **REQUISITO** — Pressure ulcer prevention principles: frequent total body skin inspection, turning/positioning ≥every 2 h, managing incontinence, preventing contractures, adequate nutrition/hydration, offloading devices, proper transfer/lift techniques
- [P2039] **HECHO** — NF residents frequently overlooked for VTE risk
- [P2040] **HECHO** — Universal VTE prophylaxis for all rehab patients = unproven benefit
- [P2041] **HECHO** — VTE prophylaxis proven benefit: post hip fracture surgery or knee arthroplasty; minimum 4 weeks duration
- [P2042] **TENSIÓN** — Some residents at high VTE risk are simultaneously at high bleeding risk from anticoagulants

### Stroke Rehabilitation

- [P2043] **HECHO** — Up to 20% of first-ever stroke patients die within 30 days from pneumonia, PE, or cardiac complications (MI, CHF)
- [P2044] **REGLA** — Stroke rehab should begin immediately upon medical stabilization
- [P2045] **HECHO** — Stroke complications requiring active prevention: pressure injuries, spasticity/contractures, bowel/bladder issues, respiratory complications
- [P2046] **HECHO** — Spasticity occurs in >60% of post-stroke patients; manifests as increased muscle tone + exaggerated DTRs
- [P2047] **HECHO** — Antispasmodic drugs poorly tolerated in elderly (sedation/confusion); focal phenol/botulinum toxin injections or serial casting for severe spasticity
- [P2048] **HECHO** — Post-stroke bowel/bladder dysfunction in 50-70% of patients; many resolve within 3-6 months
- [P2049] **HECHO** — Most strokes → hyperreflexic bladder + uninhibited contractions → urinary urgency/incontinence; managed with timed voiding schedule
- [P2050] **HECHO** — Up to 1/3 of patients with dysphagia have aspiration episodes; video fluoroscopic swallow study = diagnostic + helps select dietary consistency
- [P2051] **HECHO** — Post-stroke PE incidence = 10-15%; VTE prophylaxis recommended
- [P2052] **REGLA** — Ambulation of ≥50 feet/day reduces DVT/PE risk post-stroke
- [P2053] **HECHO** — Therapy interventions begin once neurological deficits no longer progressing, usually within 48 h post-stroke
- [P2054] **DEFINICIÓN** — Modern stroke rehab approaches: compensation strategies (using unaffected extremity/wheelchair), neurophysiologic training (using affected side, minimizes spasticity), task-oriented retraining (constraining unaffected extremity), strengthening exercises (progressive resistance)

### Fracture Care

- [P2055] **HECHO** — Annual mortality post hip fracture in elderly extremely high (up to 36%)
- [P2056] **HECHO** — Up to 50% of hip fracture patients do not regain previous ambulatory function; up to 20% become nonambulatory
- [P2057] **REGLA** — Hip fracture rehab should start ASAP after surgery; most important need = early mobilization
- [P2058] **HECHO** — Post hip fracture complications: pneumonia, DVT, PE, UTI, pressure injuries, delirium, depression, anorexia, weight loss, unsuccessful healing, constipation, fecal impaction, urinary retention
- [P2059] **HECHO** — Prosthetic joint replacement → full weight bearing as soon as day 2 post-surgery; progresses faster than pinning
- [P2060] **HECHO** — Balance retraining/ambulation within 4-8 days; stair climbing after ~10 days post joint replacement
- [P2061] **HECHO** — Pubic rami/ischium = most common pelvic fracture sites (not ilium); weight bearing achievable once pain eases; isolated pubic ramus fractures often heal without significant long-term disability
- [P2062] **HECHO** — Thoracic vertebral compression fractures → wedge deformity → kyphosis → impaired respiratory function; lumbar → vertebral plate narrowing
- [P2063] **HECHO** — Kyphoplasty may relieve pain/prevent deformity in selected vertebral fracture patients
- [P2064] **REGLA** — Total knee replacement rehab: balance preventing deconditioning vs avoiding overuse of new joint; cemented joints = weight bear as tolerated immediately; cementless = partial weight bearing initially

### Amputation and Orthotics

- [P2065] **HECHO** — Higher lower extremity amputation incidence in elderly related to higher prevalence of PAD + diabetes
- [P2066] **HECHO** — Common lower limb amputation sites: below knee (transtibial), above knee (transfemoral)
- [P2067] **HECHO** — Pre-prosthetic rehab: wound management, pain control, edema control, strengthening, functional training, ROM maintenance to prevent contracture
- [P2068] **HECHO** — Phantom limb sensation common; often improves over time; responds to desensitization treatment
- [P2069] **HECHO** — O2 demand increases ~33% in transtibial amputee; nearly doubles for transfemoral amputee during prosthetic training

### Deconditioned State

- [P2070] **DEFINICIÓN** — Deconditioning = generalized weakness/debility; common in NF residents admitted from hospital or after acute illness
- [P2071] **REGLA** — If not on skilled, deconditioning rehab covered under Medicare Part B; ALFs can arrange therapy via Part B or private insurance

### Maximizing Function

- [P2072] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Federal regulations require NFs to provide services to attain/maintain highest practicable functioning level
- [P2073] **DEFINICIÓN** — Restorative care = ongoing exercise + ADL training program developed by therapy team, carried out by nursing staff/assistants or designated restorative therapy aide

### Pain Management

- [P2074] **REGLA** — Analgesics should be provided around the clock (not PRN only) for continuous pain + additional PRN for breakthrough pain
- [P2075] **REGLA** — Common practice: pain medication given 30-60 min before therapy session to prevent excessive pain limiting performance
- [P2076] **REGLA** — As function improves/surgical sites heal, reevaluate + consider GDR of analgesic regimen

### Depression

- [P2077] **HECHO** — Depression prevalence >50% among LTC residents
- [P2078] **HECHO** — Depressed rehab patients: slower progress, longer LOS, worse outcomes, may not complete therapy program
- [P2079] **REGLA** — Depression treatment should be timely, comprehensive; include psychotherapy + pharmacotherapy

### Care Transitions

- [P2080] **REQUISITO** — Rehab therapy services must be available to all residents in all PALTC settings
- [P2081] **REQUISITO** — During care transitions, therapy progress notes must be available to therapists assuming care
- [P2082] **HECHO** — Evidence suggests IRF may predict better functional outcomes with shorter LOS but at higher cost vs SNF

### Summary

- [P2083] **HECHO** — For long-stay NF residents with progressive irreversible functional decline at end of life, QoL/dignity priorities substitute traditional rehab goals

## COVID-19 in Post-Acute and Long-Term Care


### Introduction

- [P2084] **HECHO** — SARS-CoV-2 caused >152,000 US nursing home resident deaths; >2,300 confirmed staff deaths by early 2021; >1.3 million confirmed cases among residents/staff
- [P2085] **HECHO** — Weekly COVID-19 cases in US NFs peaked late 2020; deaths peaked early 2021

### Demographics and Scope

- [P2086] **HECHO** — NF residents had COVID-19 infection rates 14x higher than community-dwelling older adults; accounted for 22% of all COVID-19 cases in Medicare population
- [P2087] **HECHO** — NF residents with COVID-19 had 12x likelihood of hospital admission vs community adults >65
- [P2088] **HECHO** — >43% of NF residents hospitalized with COVID-19 died vs 22% community-dwelling older adults
- [P2089] **HECHO** — Male/female NF residents had nearly equal COVID-19 infection incidence
- [P2090] **HECHO** — COVID-19 infection rates among age subsets 65-100 years showed no significant difference
- [P2091] **HECHO** — Hispanic, Black, Asian NF residents had higher COVID-19 infection rates
- [P2092] **HECHO** — As of April 2022, average 30% of nursing homes have shortages of direct staff (nurses/nurse aides)
- [P2093] **HECHO** — As of 2022, >79% NF residents fully vaccinated + boosted for COVID-19
- [P2094] **HECHO** — Per OIG June 2022: 91% NF staff received required COVID-19 vaccine doses; 56% staff received booster; 6% received religious exemption

### Clinical Presentation of COVID-19 in Older Adults

- [P2095] **HECHO** — Advanced age = strongest risk factor for critical COVID-19 illness
- [P2096] **HECHO** — COVID-19 clinical presentation often atypical in elderly; may not include respiratory distress/fever
- [P2097] **HECHO** — COVID-19 presentation in elderly: fatigue, myalgias, headache, nasal congestion, SOB (12% >60 yrs vs 3% <60 yrs), sore throat, loss taste/smell, dizziness, N/V, diarrhea, reduced mobility, falls, delirium (up to 28%), glycemic dysregulation
- [P2098] **HECHO** — Falls = presenting symptom of COVID-19 in 23-32% of persons >65 years
- [P2099] **HECHO** — Up to 37% lack classic COVID-19 symptoms of fever/SOB
- [P2100] **DEFINICIÓN** — IDSA fever definition in older adults: single oral temp >100 degF, OR repeat readings >99 degF oral, OR elevation ≥2 degF above baseline
- [P2101] **HECHO** — 38.3 degC (101 degF) or higher requires prompt intervention (severe infection)

### Symptoms of COVID-19 Versus Other Viral Infections

- [P2102] **HECHO** — COVID-19 symptoms onset 5-6 days (up to 14 days) after exposure; last days to weeks
- [P2103] **HECHO** — Influenza symptoms onset 2 days after exposure; last 3-7 days
- [P2104] **HECHO** — Common cold symptoms onset 1-3 days; resolve within 10 days
- [P2105] **HECHO** — COVID-19 onset gradual, influenza sudden, common cold gradual
- [P2106] **HECHO** — Coinfection with influenza/RSV can → more severe respiratory illness, hospitalization, mechanical ventilation, increased mortality
- [P2107] **HECHO** — COVID-19 asymptomatic period 2-14 days vs influenza 1-2 days

### Infection Prevention and Control Strategies

- [P2108] **REGLA** — Quarantine no longer needed for asymptomatic NF admissions who are fully vaccinated + boosted OR acquired natural immunity from COVID-19 in last 3 months
- [P2109] **REQUISITO** — Facilities ≥100 residents, or providing ventilator care/on-site hemodialysis, must employ full-time infection control preventionist
- [P2110] **REQUISITO** — Adequate PPE + FDA-approved hand sanitizer (60-95% alcohol) must be provided in all resident rooms + facility common areas
- [P2111] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Notify local health department: ≥1 residents/HCP with suspected/confirmed SARS-CoV-2; residents with severe respiratory infection → hospitalization/death; ≥3 residents/HCP with COVID-19-compatible illness onset within 72 h
- [P2112] **OBLIGACIÓN** — SARS-CoV-2 infections, staffing, POC testing data reported to CDC NHSN LTCF COVID-19 Module weekly; satisfies CMS COVID-19 reporting requirement
- [P2113] **DEFINICIÓN** — Source control = wearing well-fitting masks/respirators to prevent viral transmission (vs PPE which protects wearer)

### Visitation

- [P2114] **REGLA** — Safest indoor visitation: source control + physical distancing; if both resident/visitor up-to-date vaccinated → may choose whether to wear source control/have physical contact
- [P2115] **REGLA** — Visitors must wear source control in areas with HCP or other residents regardless of vaccination status

### Precautions when Providing Patient Care

- [P2116] **REGLA** — HCWs in high COVID areas: wear NIOSH-approved N95 or higher respirator
- [P2117] **REGLA** — Face shield/eye protection covering front + sides recommended during all patient encounters
- [P2118] **REGLA** — CDC recommended multidose inhalers instead of nebulizers during COVID pandemic due to aerosol transmission risk

### Patient Monitoring and COVID-19 Testing

- [P2119] **REQUISITO** — All residents monitored on admission + daily for fever (≥100.0 degF) + COVID-19 symptoms
- [P2120] **REQUISITO** — Pulse oximetry recommended included with standard vital signs
- [P2121] **REGLA** — COVID-19 viral testing for any symptomatic resident (even mild symptoms), regardless of vaccination status
- [P2122] **REGLA** — Asymptomatic resident with close SARS-CoV-2 contact → 2 viral tests: one immediately + if negative, second 5-7 days after exposure
- [P2123] **REGLA** — Testing not necessary in asymptomatic persons recovered from COVID-19 in last 3 months; if tested, use antigen test (not NAAT, as NAAT may be positive for 3 months yet non-infectious)
- [P2124] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Blood antibody tests should NOT be used to diagnose active COVID-19
- [P2125] **REGLA** — If healthcare-associated transmission detected → expanded testing of HCWs/residents; if additional infections detected → broad testing every 3-7 days until no new cases for 2 weeks

### Return to Work: HCWs Infected with COVID-19

- [P2126] **REGLA** — Mild/moderate COVID-19 HCWs (not immunocompromised): return to work at 7 days with negative test within 48 h of return; OR 10 days if no testing/positive result on day 5-7; PLUS ≥24 h fever-free without antipyretics + symptoms improved
- [P2127] **REGLA** — Severe/critical COVID-19 HCWs (not immunocompromised): return at ≥10-20 days; ≥24 h fever-free without antipyretics + symptoms improved; 2 consecutive negative respiratory specimens ≥24 h apart required
- [P2128] **REGLA** — Moderately/severely immunocompromised HCWs: may produce infectious virus >20 days; ID/occupational health consult required; symptomatic → fever resolved + symptoms improved + 2 consecutive negative specimens ≥24 h apart; asymptomatic → 2 consecutive negative specimens ≥24 h apart
- [P2129] **DEFINICIÓN** — CDC close contact = within 6 feet of confirmed SARS-CoV-2 person OR unprotected direct contact with infectious body fluids; indoor exposures >6 feet over long period still concerning

### Patient Assessment and Management

- [P2130] **HECHO** — Comorbidities worsening COVID-19 prognosis: HTN, HF, respiratory disease, DM, renal failure, cancer, cognitive impairment, frailty
- [P2131] **HECHO** — 40% of COVID-19-positive patients have no signs/symptoms; 30% present atypically (study of 400 residents across 4 LTCFs)
- [P2132] **HECHO** — Factors associated with increased COVID-19 mortality in NFs: male gender, age >85, DM, dyspnea, thermal dysregulation, altered consciousness, falls, dementia, incontinence, CKD, CVD, prior pneumonia, malnutrition/dehydration, functional dependency
- [P2133] **HECHO** — Mortality markers: supplemental O2 need, O2 desaturation despite supplementation, bilateral lung infiltrates, elevated CRP/IL-6, reduced lymphocyte count, low GFR, hemoconcentration, hypernatremia, reduced serum albumin

### Management of COVID-19 Infection

- [P2134] **REGLA** — Fever/headache/myalgias → acetaminophen; daily dose not to exceed 3 g
- [P2135] **REGLA** — Cough → antitussives; avoid codeine-containing preparations
- [P2136] **REGLA** — Dyspnea → prone position may help; supplemental O2 to maintain saturation >92%; incentive spirometry + breathing exercises
- [P2137] **REGLA** — COVID-19 polypharmacy management: discontinue unnecessary supplements/treatments; stop sliding scale insulin
- [P2138] **REGLA** — Paxlovid (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir): 300mg/100mg Q12h x5 days if eGFR >60; 150mg/100mg Q12h x5 days if eGFR 30-60; NOT recommended if eGFR <30; within ≤5 days from symptom onset; evaluate drug-drug interactions
- [P2139] **REGLA** — Remdesivir: 200mg IV day 1, then 100mg IV QD days 2-3; within ≤7 days from symptom onset; monitor during infusion + 1 h after
- [P2140] **REGLA** — Bebtelovimab: 175mg single IV injection in 30s; observe ≥1 h; within ≤7 days symptom onset
- [P2141] **REGLA** — Molnupiravir: 800mg PO BID x5 days within ≤5 days symptom onset; use only if Paxlovid unavailable
- [P2142] **REGLA** — Dexamethasone: 6mg PO daily if new/increased supplemental O2 need; not to exceed 10 days; equivalents: 40mg prednisone or 32mg methylprednisolone

### Medications Not Recommended

- [P2143] **EXCLUSIÓN** — NOT recommended for COVID-19: ivermectin, famotidine, colchicine, hydroxychloroquine + azithromycin (pre/post exposure prophylaxis)
- [P2144] **RESTRICCIÓN** — Inhaled corticosteroids and fluvoxamine for COVID-19 → only in clinical trial context

### Long-Term Consequences of COVID-19

- [P2145] **DEFINICIÓN** — Post-acute COVID-19 = symptoms present ≥3 weeks after initial presentation; chronic COVID-19 = symptoms beyond 12 weeks
- [P2146] **HECHO** — CDC telephone survey: 47% of older adults with ≥3 chronic conditions had not returned to usual health 14-21 days after positive test
- [P2147] **HECHO** — Long-term COVID effects in residents: fatigue, weight loss, dyspnea, O2 dependence (pulmonary fibrosis), encephalopathy, increased HF incidence, impaired foot circulation, headache/vertigo/impaired smell-taste, myopathy, peripheral neuropathy, functional decline, PTSD, depression/anxiety

### Long COVID-19

- [P2148] **HECHO** — Long COVID-19 persistent symptoms up to 15+ months: brain fog (cognitive deficit, memory loss, concentration difficulty), headaches, dizziness, tingling, blurred vision, tinnitus, chronic fatigue
- [P2149] **HECHO** — Long COVID may be related to: increased autoantibodies, EBV DNA in blood, SARS-CoV-2 RNA fragments in blood, Type 2 diabetes
- [P2150] **HECHO** — Cardiopulmonary symptoms common in Long COVID: persisting dyspnea partly due to reduced lung diffusion capacity
- [P2151] **HECHO** — More severe acute disease → more likely to develop Long COVID

### Summary

- [P2152] **HECHO** — COVID-19 transitioned from epidemic → pandemic → endemic phase (as of fall 2022)
- [P2153] **HECHO** — Omicron variant/subvariants more infectious than Alpha/Beta/Delta; persons 65% less likely to die from Omicron but increased Long COVID risk
- [P2154] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Requirement to notify local Health Department upon suspected/confirmed COVID-19 outbreak at NF/ALF
- [P2155] **REQUISITO** — If another COVID pandemic limits hospital access, NF staff/practitioners may need to provide hospital-level care; readiness vital
- [P2156] **HECHO** — NASEM 2020 consensus report (commissioned by CMS) recommended vast improvements in NF care delivery, financing, regulation, quality measurement
