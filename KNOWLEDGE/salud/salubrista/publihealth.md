## Chapter 1.1: The Scope and Concerns of Public Health

### Introduction — definitions

- [P042] **DEFINICION** — public health = "science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life, promoting health through organized efforts of society" (Acheson Report 1988)
- [P043] **DEFINICION** — public health goal = "biologic, physical, mental well-being of all members of society regardless of gender, wealth, ethnicity, sexual orientation, country, political views" (Detels 2003)
- [P044] **HECHO** — 1948 UN Declaration: health = basic human right
- [P045] **HECHO** — 17 SDGs declared 2012; focus on inequalities, poverty, climate change + areas essential for optimal public health
- [P046] **HECHO** — SDGs superseded Millennium Development Goals; underscore public health concerns beyond disease elimination

### Functions of public health

- [P047] **DEFINICION** — public health = process of mobilizing local/state/national/international resources to assure conditions where all people can be healthy (Detels & Breslow 2002)
- [P048] **HECHO** — 16 functions of public health (CDC 1994): prevent disease/injuries, promote healthy lifestyles, surveillance, investigate health problems, formulate/enforce policies, organize cost-effective services, reduce disparities, protect environment, disseminate health info, promote health, disaster preparedness, reduce violence/war, research, develop new methodologies, train workforce, evaluate programmes
- [P049] **HECHO** — US: behavioural patterns cause ~40% deaths; social circumstances 15%; environmental factors 5% (Schroeder 2007)
- [P050] **DEFINICION** — implementation science = field delineating barriers/facilitators for rapid translation of scientific advances → health practice/policy
- [P051] **HECHO** — international collaboration essential: data sharing, policy formulation, management of public health issues across globalized world
- [P052] **HECHO** — computational/AI advances greatly increase ability to analyse massive datasets with thousands of variables; machine learning for finding associations/patterns for targeted interventions

### Contemporary health issues — poverty

- [P053] **HECHO** — >3B people (>half world, 1/3 children) live below $2.50/day poverty line; 1.3B on <$1.25/day (World Bank 2012)
- [P054] **HECHO** — US 2016: 40.6M Americans below poverty level; highest among African-Americans/Hispanic-Americans (23.2%) → `US Census Bureau`
- [P055] **HECHO** — 844M people lack clean drinking water; ≥2B use faeces-contaminated water sources → `WHO`
- [P056] **HECHO** — world population 7B+ (2017); estimated >9B by 2050; growth flat in many areas but continuing in Africa

### Contemporary health issues — epidemiological transition

- [P057] **HECHO** — 20th century: transition from infectious/communicable diseases → NCDs as major disease burden
- [P058] **HECHO** — by early 21st century heart/chronic diseases = leading death causes; pneumonia/influenza no longer top 10
- [P059] **HECHO** — global longevity 71.4 years; population ageing from increased longevity + decreased birth rates; growth below replacement in many countries

### Measuring disease occurrence

- [P060] **DEFINICION** — YPLL (years of productive life lost): emphasizes diseases reducing productive lifespan (currently defined 75 years)
- [P061] **DEFINICION** — DALYs (disability-adjusted life years): emphasizes diseases compromising function + includes premature mortality
- [P062] **HECHO** — NCDs = 70% of disease deaths worldwide; cardiovascular diseases = half of NCD deaths
- [P063] **HECHO** — communicable diseases = major death cause only in Africa; account for ~30% global disease burden
- [P064] **HECHO** — WHO projected communicable diseases: decline from 39% → 30% of global DALYs by 2030
- [P065] **DEFINICION** — syndemicity (1990s): diseases occur in specific contexts; interactions between diseases affect vulnerability/severity; requires broader public health approach

### Communicable diseases

- [P066] **HECHO** — barriers to vaccine coverage: poverty, geographic obstacles, low education, logistics, civil unrest/wars, corruption, government mistrust
- [P067] **HECHO** — individual with communicable disease can circumnavigate globe while infectious but asymptomatic; SARS spread SE Asia → Canada within weeks
- [P068] **HECHO** — newly identified pathogens 1994-2019: SARS-CoV-2 (2019), acute flaccid myelitis (2018), Zika (2012), MERS-CoV (2012), H1N1 (2009), avian influenza human cases (2004), SARS (2003), Nipah (1999), H5N1 (1997), new variant CJD + Australian bat lyssavirus (1996), HHV-8 (1995), Savia + Hendra virus (1994)
- [P069] **HECHO** — SARS-CoV-2: recognized Jan 2020, spread from China → pandemic; >3.25M cases, >226K deaths in 4 months
- [P070] **HECHO** — NDM-1 (New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase) encodes multiple-antibiotic resistance; spread rapidly to many countries via transmissible genetic elements
- [P071] **HECHO** — >1B people (1/7 world) suffer ≥1 tropical disease: Buruli ulcer, Chagas, cholera, dengue, dracunculiasis, trypanosomiasis, leishmaniasis, leprosy, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, schistosomiasis, helminthiasis, trachoma
- [P072] **HECHO** — 20 factors for emergence/re-emergence of infectious diseases: demographic expansion into remote areas, proximity to animals, sanitary breakdown, land use changes, climate changes, behavioural changes, social inequality, international travel, food handling changes, pathogen evolution, drug resistance, vector resistance, immunosuppression, surveillance deterioration, illiteracy, lack of political will, biowarfare, war/unrest, famine, declining immunization

### Noncommunicable diseases

- [P073] **HECHO** — NCDs: 40M deaths = 71.4% of all deaths globally (2016); dominant + growing even in poor developing countries
- [P074] **HECHO** — type 2 diabetes prevalence in rural India: 7.3% (Anjana et al. 2017)
- [P075] **HECHO** — 2000-2008: stroke incidence in LMICs exceeded high-income countries by 20% (Feigin et al. 2009)
- [P076] **HECHO** — NCD age-standardized death rate: LMICs males 65% higher, females 85% higher than high-income countries
- [P077] **HECHO** — HPV = major cause cervical cancer; vaccine effective but expensive, requires pre-sexual-activity administration + multiple injections
- [P078] **HECHO** — insufficient physical activity: 3.2M deaths + 69M DALYs/year; obesity: 2.8M deaths + 35.8M DALYs globally (WHO)
- [P079] **HECHO** — most NCDs reducible by: not smoking + moderate alcohol + exercise (Breslow & Breslow 1993)

### Mental illness

- [P080] **HECHO** — 1.1B people suffer mental health problems; psychiatric disorders = 15.5% global disease burden
- [P081] **HECHO** — global 2017: any mental disorder 10.7% (792M); depression 3.4% (264M); anxiety 3.8% (284M); bipolar 0.6% (46M); eating disorders 0.2% (16M); schizophrenia 0.3% (20M); any mental/substance use 13% (970M); alcohol use 1.4% (107M); drug use 0.9% (71M)
- [P082] **HECHO** — global median mental health expenditure: $2.5/capita; <2% of government health expenditure; >80% funds → mental hospitals in LMICs
- [P083] **HECHO** — mental health workers: median 9/100K globally; <1 in low-income → 72 in high-income countries
- [P084] **HECHO** — 76-85% of mentally ill in developing countries lack access to appropriate care
- [P085] **HECHO** — 30% of governments lack mental health programme; 28% have no mental health budget

### Population projections

- [P086] **HECHO** — world population: 7.6B (2018); estimated 9.8B by 2050
- [P087] **HECHO** — 2017: ~1B people aged 60+ (13% global population); doubled since 1980; growing ~3%/year; expected 2B by 2050
- [P088] **HECHO** — among 65+ individuals: 30% men + 15% women active in labour force (UN 2015)
- [P089] **HECHO** — majority of oldest elderly = single women with more limited resources + lower education, especially in developing countries

### Nutrition

- [P090] **HECHO** — industry more effective promoting calorie-dense/salt-rich/unhealthy foods than public health championing healthy choices; especially targeting young

### Oral health

- [P091] **HECHO** — 60-90% school children + ~100% adults worldwide have dental cavities
- [P092] **HECHO** — ~30% adults aged 65-74 lost all teeth
- [P093] **HECHO** — oral cancers: 8th most common globally, 3rd in Asia; ~450K new cases/year; 5-year survival ~50%

### Injuries

- [P094] **HECHO** — injuries/violence cause ~5.8M deaths/year (~10% global deaths); 1.7x HIV/AIDS+TB+malaria fatalities combined
- [P095] **HECHO** — motor vehicle accidents: 23% of injury deaths (>3,400 deaths/day); tens of millions injured/disabled yearly
- [P096] **HECHO** — injury deaths ~3x greater in developing vs developed countries; for ages 5-44 injuries = 3rd leading death cause

### Homicide, violence, and suicide

- [P097] **HECHO** — ~30% women globally experienced physical/sexual violence from intimate partners (WHO)
- [P098] **HECHO** — in China, suicide = leading cause of death among rural women

### Vulnerable populations

- [P099] **DEFINICION** — vulnerable populations = groups with special susceptibility to poor health/quality of life: poor, minorities, women, children, elderly, handicapped, illiterate, orphans, street children, immigrants, refugees, homeless, mentally ill
- [P100] **HECHO** — even in US universal healthcare not available; funding universal healthcare = persistent challenge
- [P101] **HECHO** — US experiencing opioid overuse epidemic from overprescribing
- [P102] **HECHO** — gender = spectrum of sexual orientations/behaviours beyond male/female dichotomy

### The environment

- [P103] **DEFINICION** — environmental health = aspects of human health determined by physical/chemical/biological/social/psychosocial processes in environment (WHO Sofia 1993)
- [P104] **HECHO** — >14M known chemicals globally, >60K commonly used; 23% of all deaths globally attributable to environment (~12.6M deaths/year)
- [P105] **HECHO** — ambient PM2.5: ranked 5th global risk factor (2014); caused 4.2M deaths + 103M DALYs in 2015 (up from 3.5M deaths in 1990)

### Air pollution

- [P106] **HECHO** — main air pollutants: ozone, particulate matter, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, sulphur dioxide, lead
- [P107] **HECHO** — China: ~1.6M deaths/year from air pollution; cancer rates ≤4x in industrial provinces; ~4M cancer cases 2015 → ~3M deaths

### Water pollution

- [P108] **HECHO** — 40% world population lacks clean drinking water; ~60% lacks adequate waste disposal

### Other pollutants

- [P109] **HECHO** — nanoparticles (1 billionth metre) increasingly used in foods/drugs/cosmetics; reported serious damage to lung/liver/brain cells

### Climate change

- [P110] **HECHO** — from mid-1970s climate change contributed ~150K deaths + ~5M DALYs/year, mainly via malnutrition/diarrhoea/malaria (WHO)
- [P111] **HECHO** — climate change health pathways: heat waves, reduced cold deaths, increased natural disasters, changed vector-borne disease distribution, disaster/malnutrition risks

### Rescuing the environment

- [P112] **HECHO** — 2017: US President Trump refused to endorse Paris Accord on Climate Change

### Occupational health

- [P113] **HECHO** — ILO annual estimates: >2M work-related deaths, >300M work-related diseases, ~3.7M fatal/non-fatal work-related injuries globally
- [P114] **HECHO** — 12M serious injuries among young workers in 2011
- [P115] **HECHO** — informal/part-time workers = ≥50% global workforce; lack benefits, vulnerable to injury + limited healthcare access

### Provision of and access to healthcare

- [P116] **HECHO** — ACA (Affordable Care Act) under Obama extended coverage; Trump/Republican Congress undermined it → 13M Americans lost coverage

### Bioterrorism and war

- [P117] **HECHO** — biological weapons history extends centuries; Middle Ages: plague corpses catapulted into besieged castles

### Ethics in public health

- [P118] **HECHO** — emphasis on public health ethics post-WWII (Nazi Germany violations); Declaration of Helsinki, Belmont Report, CIOMS promulgated ethical guidelines + institutional review boards worldwide

### Public health interventions

- [P119] **DEFINICION** — public health interventions = 4 categories: social/biological/environmental, behavioural, political, structural
- [P120] **HECHO** — improved standard of living (clean water, safe waste disposal) = greatest impact strategy for public health
- [P121] **HECHO** — immunization = most cost-effective biological intervention; requires minimal behavioural change, usually single action series
- [P122] **HECHO** — smallpox vaccine available >150 years before elimination; key = "search and contain" strategy + political will + funding
- [P123] **HECHO** — DOTS for TB reduces transmission + MDR-TB development; successfully implemented in persisting countries
- [P124] **HECHO** — "treatment as prevention" = mantra of HIV/AIDS control
- [P125] **HECHO** — antismoking campaigns (US/Britain) + one-child policy (China) demonstrate community norm change is possible
- [P126] **HECHO** — China 2013: strong anti-pollution measures (especially stopping coal); air quality greatly improved

### Private support of public health

- [P127] **HECHO** — Rockefeller Foundation: first US public health school (Johns Hopkins); International Health Commission (1913); China Medical Board (1914); Peking Union Medical College (1921)
- [P128] **TENSION** — Gates Foundation massive HIV/malaria/TB funding has positive impact but influences public health priorities; some argue strong infrastructure in developing countries would have greater impact than disease-specific funding

### Social activism

- [P129] **HECHO** — HIV/AIDS social activism → policy of treatment access for all HIV+ individuals + recognition of rights of marginalized groups

### The future of public health

- [P130] **HECHO** — successful adding "years to life" but chronic diseases (e.g. Alzheimer's) reduce quality; must now add "life to years"
- [P131] **HECHO** — SARS-CoV-2 pandemic severely impacted global economy + societal functioning; long-term changes difficult to predict

## Chapter 2.1: Determinants of Health — Overview

### Introduction — framework

- [P132] **DEFINICION** — determinants of health = "range of personal, social, economic, environmental factors determining health status of individuals/populations" (Nutbeam/WHO 1998)
- [P133] **DEFINICION** — four classical categories: biological, behavioural, environmental, social determinants (Remais & Jackson 2016)

### Biological determinants

- [P134] **DEFINICION** — biological determinants = endogenous body features: genetic structures (body/brain development, disease susceptibility) + age (cell function deterioration)

### Behavioural determinants

- [P135] **HECHO** — tobacco smoking: ~6M premature deaths globally/year (WHO 2015)
- [P136] **HECHO** — obesity prevalence worldwide tripled since 1970s; growing risk in many countries

### Environmental determinants

- [P137] **HECHO** — diarrhoeal disease from contaminated water/food = major cause illness/death in children <5, especially LICs
- [P138] **HECHO** — ~4.2M deaths/year from ambient air pollution + ~3.8M from household air pollution (cooking fuels) (WHO 2018)

### Social determinants — introduction

- [P139] **DEFINICION** — SDH = daily social/economic living conditions + higher-level socioeconomic/political factors affecting distribution of power/money/resources (CSDH 2008)
- [P140] **DEFINICION** — key SDH mediating mechanisms: power, income/wealth, education, employment → `CSDH`
- [P141] **DEFINICION** — health inequities = socially determined health inequalities regarded as unfair/unjust; could be reduced if governments chose differently (Whitehead 1992)

### Healthcare services

- [P142] **HECHO** — universal access to PHC = important step improving population health + health equity in all countries (WHO 2008)
- [P143] **DEFINICION** — comprehensive PHC = first-level medical care + strategies for health promotion, disease prevention, community engagement, addressing local determinants

### Early child development

- [P144] **HECHO** — environments affecting early development (conception → 7 years) crucial for health prospects over life-course (Irwin et al. 2007) → `early child development`

### Income, education, and employment status

- [P145] **HECHO** — health inequities follow gradient: average health decreases at each SES step from highest → lowest (Fryers et al. 2003; Marmot & Friel 2008)
- [P146] **HECHO** — developed countries with higher inequality → worse health across ALL SES levels vs more equal societies (Wilkinson & Pickett 2009)

### Employment conditions

- [P147] **HECHO** — ~150M people migrated for work in 2013 (ILO 2015); economic inequalities between countries incentivize migration with higher exploitation risk

### Gender

- [P148] **HECHO** — women tend to live longer than men despite reporting more ill health in most of world
- [P149] **HECHO** — gender inequities → violence against women, discrimination against female children, unfair work divisions, lower wages, restricted education/healthcare

### Indigenous health

- [P150] **HECHO** — Indigenous peoples have significantly worse health than non-Indigenous; determinants: colonization, land dispossession, discriminatory policies, racism → `Indigenous peoples`

### Determinants of mental health

- [P151] **HECHO** — common mental illness (including substance abuse): ~20% global disease burden in years lived with disability (Whiteford et al. 2013)
- [P152] **HECHO** — depressive disorders = major/growing disease burden cause; contribute to ~800K suicides/year globally

### Urbanization and health

- [P153] **HECHO** — 2008: first time in history majority of world population lived in urban environments
- [P154] **HECHO** — urban health risk exposures cluster: environmental (air/noise/traffic), human (social isolation/safety), behavioural (eating/physical activity)
- [P155] **HECHO** — urbanization contributes to global issues: climate change + migration

### Globalization and commercial determinants

- [P156] **HECHO** — income inequality increased in nearly all world regions over recent decades (Alvaredo et al. 2018)
- [P157] **HECHO** — 2016: of top 100 world economies only 31 = countries; most = TNCs (Walmart, Royal Dutch Shell, Exxon Mobile, Apple)
- [P158] **HECHO** — TRIPS agreement requires ≥20-year patent protections; shaped by global pharmaceutical firms → `WTO`
- [P159] **HECHO** — IP extension in trade deals delays cheaper generic medicines → reduced affordable access → `TNCs`
- [P160] **DEFINICION** — commercial determinants of health = practices/strategies of corporations driving NCD risk factors (Kickbusch et al. 2016)
- [P161] **HECHO** — WHO FCTC (Framework Convention on Tobacco Control) adopted 2003, ratified by 181 countries; explicitly prevents engagement with tobacco companies

### Social determinants of health behaviours

- [P162] **HECHO** — Finnish North Karelia programme: rare successful heart health programme; success from community-level strategies + policy changes, not individual knowledge
- [P163] **TENSION** — despite evidence for structural determinants of behaviour, behaviourism still promoted as lifestyle disease solution ignoring structures; US individualism ideology undermines structural appreciation (Tesh 1988)
- [P164] **HECHO** — high Aboriginal Australian smoking partly = coping with daily racism; overweight/obesity primarily from marketing/supply of high fat/sugar foods → `commercial determinants`
- [P165] **REGLA** — behaviour change strategies alone likely increase health inequities; behaviour makes most difference when other life conditions favourable

### Climate change and social determinants

- [P166] **HECHO** — climate change effects fall most heavily on countries/groups already subject to socially determined health inequities
- [P167] **HECHO** — Syrian civil war: >5M refugees left country + ~6M displaced within Syria

### Complexity — temporality and multiple scales

- [P168] **REGLA** — SDH perspective: look "upstream" from biology/behaviour → address "causes of the causes"; modest changes in social/environmental factors across populations produce larger gains than high-risk individual interventions (Rose 1985; CSDH 2008)
- [P169] **HECHO** — Australian current smoker 9x more likely to develop lung cancer vs non-smoker; but temporal lag between exposure → health effect may be years/decades
- [P170] **DEFINICION** — "double burden of disease" = continued infectious disease impact among disadvantaged while NCDs predominate overall (Wahden 1996)

### Political challenges

- [P171] **HECHO** — Preston Curve: for LICs, national income growth from low base → marked increases in average life expectancy (Bloom & Canning 2007)
- [P172] **HECHO** — neoliberal development model adverse effects: poor nutrition, reduced healthcare, income inequalities, environmental degradation, poor urban conditions, low wages, population displacement, loss of domestic agriculture
- [P173] **HECHO** — South Australia pioneered "Health in All Policies" approach = whole-of-government action on SDH → `HiAP`

### Conclusion

- [P174] **REGLA** — addressing health determinants requires multiple forms of action at different scales, in different domains, attending to different lifespan phases (CSDH 2008)
