Transformation is what a process does to an object, which can be one of three things: creating a new object, consumption of an existing object, or change in the state of an existing object.
A consumption link is a unidirectional transformation link depicted as an arrow from the consumed object (or “consumee”) to the process that consumes it.
A result link is a unidirectional transformation link depicted as an arrow from a creating process to the resulting object (or “resultee”), which this process creates.
An effect link is a bidirectional transformation link depicted as two opposing arrows, which connect the affecting process to the affected object (or “affectee”).
The effect is a change in the state of that object.

### 09 System aspects

Intro
Hello again!
We are in Store-Dot, getting ready for your interview, as an engineer you'd like to get so much.
In order to make sure this is going to be the case,
I would like to discuss the three major aspects of any system: structure, behavior, and function.
Let's take the electric car as an example.
The system's structure pertains to: "what" the system is:
What are the parts of or components of the system, and what the static, time-independent relations among them are?
In our coffee making system example, the coffee making system is the coffee machine.
Its main parts are the water tank, milk frother, water heater, capsule compartment, cup holder, and operating button.
An example of a structural relation is the connection between the coffee machine and the capsule compartment.
This relation is time-independent: It is persistent over time and does not change.
The system's behavior pertains to "how" the system operates:
What are the processes and sub-processes that happen in the system?
And, what the dynamic, time-dependent relations among them and the objects they transform are.
In our coffee making system example, coffee making is the main process.
It consumes water, milk, and an intact capsule, and yields a coffee drink in a cup.
The main sub-processes are water heating, milk frothing, coffee brewing, and milk adding.
These sub-processes are related to each other by time; some happen sequentially while others in parallel.
Obviously, the behavioral aspect of the system is dynamic and time-dependent.
Finally, the function of an artificial, human-made system, pertains to "what for" or "why" the system operates the way it does:
What was the underlying motivation for developing the system?
Who are the system beneficiaries?
What value does each beneficiary get from the system's operation?
System structure
In our coffee making system example, the beneficiaries include the coffee drinker, the coffee maker, and the machine manufacturer, who benefits from selling those machines.
The simplest aspect of the system to understand is its structure.
This is the ''what'' of the system: what the system consists of, and what the fixed relations between its parts are.
Our system consists of an electric car, which, in turn, is comprised of a chassis, steering wheel, an electric battery, and a computer network for internal and external communication.
Of course, there are other parts - and all of them are physical.

Electric Car is the whole object – it aggregates all the other objects.

System behavior
The second aspect of every system is its behavior.
This is the ''how'' of the system: how it operates, and how it, and its various parts, change over time.
We called the main process Electric Car Operating, and it consists of Car Charging, Car Starting, and Car Driving.

So, what is the system's function? It is the ''why'' of the system. what is the operation of the system – its main process, and on what main object does it operate?
The combination of the system's structure with its behavior is the function of the system.

An OPM model is a conceptual model that uses objects, processes, and relations among them, to represent the structure, behavior, and function of a system at various levels of detail in a unified model expressed in both graphics and text.
The Structure of a system is its form—the assembly of its physical and informatical components along with the long-lasting relations among them.
Main object - The operand of the system, the object which is transformed.
The Behavior of a system is its dynamics—the way the system changes over time by transforming objects.
Main process - The process which transforms the main object or the operand of the system.
Main function of a system is the combination of the main object and main process of the system.
In a human-made system, the function is what provides the benefit to the system's beneficiary.

Each one of the information processing channels - the visual and the verbal - has limited capacity.
Each channel can hold only so much information at any given time.
If we try to condense too much information into one image or piece of text, our short memory will be overloaded with information and will not be able to retain or process it.

### 11 Section summary

Hello! Let's put together everything we have learned so far.
The focus of this course is model-based systems engineering, or MBSE for short.
MBSE is a methodology for designing systems based on conceptual models.
Such models represent the system at various levels of complexity and evolve during the system's lifecycle stages.
Using this conceptual model, we, the developers, as well as various other stakeholders of the system, are able to have an in-depth understanding of the system, and communicate with each other clearly and efficiently.
The model is like the blueprint of the system or product it represents.
It is the source of authority that enables all the parties involved to be on the same page at all times.
This way, we can progress with developing the system along its lifecycle.
When we say lifecycle, we mean the stages that the system undergoes as it progresses from problem recognition and definition, needs and requirements elicitation and modeling, concept evaluation, architecting, detailed design, all the way to implementation, use, service and retirement.
For now, we just note that the model, when used properly, can prevent misunderstandings and future problems with system development.
Object-Process Methodology, or OPM, is a methodology and language for conceptual modeling of systems. It has several advantages:
First, OPM is recognized as ISO 19450.
Therefore, an increasing number of Fortune 500 companies use OPM to model their systems.
Second, OPM has only one kind of diagram:
Object-Process Diagram, or OPD.
Additionally, OPM makes use of Object-Process Language, or OPL, which is a subset of natural English.
It is therefore straightforward, and the basics are easy to learn.
A third advantage of OPM is that it is domain-independent, or domain-agnostic.
This means that we can use OPM to model systems from any domain or from multiple domains.
For all these reasons, OPM facilitates communication and shared understanding among all the system stakeholders: customers, engineers from different disciplines, and other various stakeholders, such as public organizations and regulatory bodies.
The elements of OPM are Things and links between things.
A Thing can be either a process, or an object, which may have states.
Things can be physical - real, or informatical - virtual.
The links we have learned so far were
Aggregation-Participation and Transforming links.
Aggregation-participation allows us to represent the whole and its parts, be it an object or a process.
Transforming links connect processes to objects, and allow us to represent relations of consumption, creation, or change in state.
Every system has three aspects: structure, behavior and function.
The combination of system structure and behavior makes its function possible.
The system structure is its 'what' - the collection of system objects and how they relate to each other.
The system behavior is the 'how' of the system
- the collection of system processes and how they are connected to the objects and transform them.
System function is the 'why' -
Why do the system processes transform objects?
What is the cause and what is the effect of the transformations?
In the case of artificial human-made systems, we can also add to the system’s function the utility aspect: What was the system built to do, and for whom?
What is the value of this transformation, and who benefits from it?
Every model in OPM has two ways, or modalities, of expression: visual and verbal, or more specifically, graphical and textual.
The graphical modality is called OPD, and the textual modality is called OPL.
An object in OPM is represented by a rectangle with a green contour, a process by an ellipse with a blue contour, and states by a “rountangle”, rounded-corner rectangle with a gold-brown contour.
The reason OPM makes use of both modalities is that humans process information in separate channels, and preferences between the two modalities vary.
In order not to overload the viewer of the system model with information, one should limit the number of items in any given OPD and OPL.
OPCloud is a collaborative Cloud-based environment for constructing OPM system models easily and efficiently.
